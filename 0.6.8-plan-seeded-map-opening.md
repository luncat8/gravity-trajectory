# 0.6.8 plan — the map generator's own promises: gravity up to +5, and the stuck HQ

Standalone spec: a fork can rebuild this from this file alone. Source notes:
`drafts/flight-lab-and-modes.md` §1.4 (`random gravity of planets -1...+5`,
`career map generator improve`, plus the stuck-rival finding in the 0.5.21
worklog). Depends on nothing shipped after 0.5.21.

## problem

Three asks and one bug, all of them about the **seeded field** rather than a
rule.

1. The draft's field is `−1…+5`; the page draws `−1…+3.5`. The cap is one
   constant (`PLANET_GRAVITY_MAX`), but it is hashed into every career, every
   replay and every bench, so widening it without a plan would silently move
   every baseline the worklogs compare against.
2. The draft wants a career map where no company is born into a corner: «some
   player possible to land on only heavy G>~1.5 planets this cause big score
   impact from start». The opening planner already validates a first-flight
   landing at `G ≤ 1.5` (`openingTraceHome`, `career_start_fairness.js`), but the
   *set* of choices is only checked for existence, not for parity.
3. The 0.5.21 review found `stuck` rivals: seed 2 R1 sits on a `g 2.1` HQ for a
   whole 2400 s bench, 4 of 24 rival-games at 400 s. A company must never start
   above the shallow band — or the field that the bench sees differs from the
   one the page sees.

## 1. find the stuck start first (no balance work before this)

The planner forces its own home to `g 1` while tracing (`openingStrengths[home] =
1`), so the HQ it *returns* can be a planet whose real seeded value is `2.1`:
the plan validates a virtual field, then `startCareer` places the ship on the
real one. That is the first hypothesis to test, and it is testable today with no
rule change:

- `experiments/hq_gravity_probe.js` already exists; extend it to print, for
  seeds 1-30 and every company: the planned HQ, its **real** `planetGravity`,
  the forced value the trace used, and the first-flight profit the planner
  claimed vs the one the launched sim measures.
- Acceptance for this step: the two numbers agree for every company, or the
  discrepancy is named (`planned g 1.0, real 2.1`) in a log line. If the mismatch
  is the cause, the fix is to trace with the real value and only *fall back* to
  the forced one, never the other way round; `career_start_fairness.js` gains the
  assertion `real g ≤ HQ_GRAVITY_MAX` for every company.

## 2. equal openings, not equal sites

Today the planner maximises the *minimum* company's first-flight pay
(`OPENING_BANDS`, `OPENING_RATIO_EPS`) and breaks ties by site count and light.
The note asks for a weaker, more honest thing: every company's first flight pays
within a band of every other's, **and** each has at least one unclaimed landing
it can leave. The second half already exists (`exitOk`); the first half is the
band.

- Report, do not change, first: `career_start_probe.js` prints per seed the
  spread `max(pay) / min(pay)` across companies and the number of exitable
  unclaimed landings each has. A band of `≤ 1.6` and `≥ 2` exitable sites is the
  proposal; the log decides whether the current assignment already meets it.
- Only if seeds fail the band: change the tie-break (site count before profit
  ratio, which the note prefers: "equal ability to land on a not heavy planet"
  is a *possibility* statement, not a money statement).

## 3. widening the ceiling to +5 (last, and as a baseline)

`PLANET_GRAVITY_MAX = 3.5 → 5` with `PLANET_GRAVITY_STEPS` unchanged changes the
draw grid and therefore every seeded field. That is a deliberate baseline break,
so it ships with:

- one commit that only changes the constant — `PLANET_GRAVITY_CHOICES` is already
  derived from it, so nothing else may move in that commit;
- `gravity_seed_test` re-pinned to the new hash values, with the old table kept in
  the worklog;
- every bench re-baselined in `experiments/logs/0.6.8-gravity-max5.txt`, both
  `bench` and `start` tables, because the 0.5.x reference numbers are then
  historical;
- the lab's `g` slider and the rule help's `−1 to +3.5` sentence read the
  constant, never a literal (they already do: `gui_view.js` reads
  `PLANET_GRAVITY_MIN/MAX`), so no copy change should be needed — check the
  help text with `grep`.

The +5 cap also needs one *design* answer the draft does not give: at `g 5` an
`EMC-1` cannot leave and an `EMC-3` may not either, so a heavy world becomes a
one-way trap unless `0.6.2`'s deep-site rule or a pad tier above 5 exists. Pin
that in the worklog before merging: the cap may be accepted only with the
"blocked share" re-measured (§3 of `drafts/hud-input-and-help.md` asks for 2–4 of
10 free planets being starter-blocked).

## Validation

- Step 1: every company's real HQ gravity `≤ HQ_GRAVITY_MAX` in the 30-seed
  probe, and the seed 2 R1 case reproduces and then disappears.
- Step 2: the spread and site table printed for seeds 1-30; a band that holds on
  every seed, or the tie-break change measured against it.
- Step 3: `gravity_seed_test` green on the new constants, all benches re-run and
  logged, and the blocked-share histogram printed (the draft's 2–4 of 10).
- No new failure name anywhere else; `node --check`, `git diff --check`.

## Scope boundary

No new planet kinds, no change to the orbit geometry or the trade world's own
seed stream (`rnd` order is untouched — the gravity stream is a separate hash).
No change to what a heavy planet *does* beyond being harder to leave; traps are
`0.6.2`'s and `0.6.3`'s business.
