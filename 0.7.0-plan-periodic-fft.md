# 0.7.0 plan — periodic FFT gravity

Standalone spec: a fork can rebuild this from this file alone. Source note:
`drafts/map.md` §gravity:

	add option periodical FFT gravity solver.
	if enabled then xy wrap map, 4x more default and max planets. probably 2x less gravity.

Depends on the Plummer force in `gravity_sim.js` (`MU`, `SOFT`), the 1600×1000
field in `orbit_world.js`, and the experimental panel (`g field`, `catapult`,
stay-pay). Space weather in the same draft is a different note and is out of
scope.

## 0. review decisions

Five points changed after reading the code; everything else is the same shape.

1. **The FFT runs only on a static field.** `GravitySim` already carries
   `fieldStatic`: the planet field in career/lab/tour/money/route is static, while
   the optional `ports` source is a moving field of at most 32 bodies. Static
   planets solve once; moving ports keep the direct sum (now min-image) and never
   touch the grid. No per-step FFT, no 30 Hz solver.
2. **`route` is not periodic.** Its score is path length; on a torus a straight
   line circles until the wall clock, so every plan converges to the same
   maximum. The checkbox is disabled (and `&rule=route&fft=1` rejected) instead
   of shipping a degenerate rule. `tour`, `money`, `career` and the lab use it.
3. **The planet cap and the array cap are two different numbers.** `PLANETS`
   becomes 192 always; the UI cap (`PLANET_UNIVERSE_MAX`) becomes a variable,
   48 or 192. Loops that mean "the active map" move from `PLANETS` to
   `career.planetCount` / `planetCount`; otherwise a 26-planet classic map still
   reads as 192.
4. **The replay end field grows to three digits.** `END_FORM` is `visited.steps`,
   and `visited` can be 104 or 192, not 26/52. `SHOT_FORM`'s origin grows for the
   same reason.
5. **The periodic field view is CPU-rastered from the grid.** A WebGL1 float
   texture is an extension (`OES_texture_float`), and `file://` must not depend
   on one. A 256×256 offscreen canvas blitted with smoothing is cheap once per
   solve and works everywhere.

## problem

The force at a ship is a direct sum of at most 48 Plummer wells in an open
plane. A flight that leaves the 2400×1500 play area ends (`END_EXIT`); the
`full area` zoom exists only to show that empty margin. The WebGL field shader
loops the same 48 bodies as uniforms, so a denser map cannot even paint.

The note wants one option that changes three things together: the solver
(FFT on a torus), the topology (xy wrap, no margin), and the roster (4×
planets, gentler wells). Off, the open-plane game is byte-identical.

## 1. the option

One checkbox in the experimental panel, beside stay-pay:

	periodic FFT

- Off is the shipped game: open plane, `END_EXIT` at the area edge, 26 / 13 /
  48 planets, the body-loop shader, `full area` zoom.
- On is a different world, not a filter: the same seed with `&fft=1` is a
  torus of 104 (lab/tour/money) or 52 (career) planets, max 192, no margin,
  FFT gravity, half the per-body well.
- `route` is refused while the option is on: the rule's score is trajectory
  length and a torus has no edge. The checkbox is disabled in `route`; a link
  that carries both is rejected before it starts. The lab, `tour`, `money` and
  `career` keep it.
- The URL carries `&fft=1` the way it carries `&ht=1`. Replay links include
  it. It is not a `localStorage` pref — it is topology, and belongs with the
  seed.
- Toggling it rebuilds the world. In lab that is a field change (stale shots
  already say so). During a run it restarts the seed, like a mode switch:
  wrap mid-flight would teleport, and the roster size is part of the map.
- Locked at the recorded value while a replay runs, same as the two sliders.
- The experimental `g field` slider still multiplies every well on top. It is
  applied at sample time (`MU * gravityScale`), never deposited into the grid,
  so moving it does not rebuild or re-solve the field.

## 2. the force law

Keep the shipped 3D Plummer, evaluated in the plane:

	a = μ s r / (|r|² + ε²)^{3/2}
	φ = −μ s / sqrt(|r|² + ε²)

	μ = 400000, ε = SOFT = 60

Do **not** switch to 2D Poisson (log potential, 1/r force). That would be a
different game. The periodic option adds the torus, it does not change the
kernel.

On a 1600×1000 torus the true field is the lattice sum of that kernel. The
solver is particle-mesh convolution of the **minimum-image** Plummer on the
grid — the FFT analogue of wrapping `dx` into [−W/2, W/2]. One image per
body, not an Ewald infinite sum. For ε = 60 and W = 1600 the next image is
already a small correction; matching wrapped `fieldAt` is the oracle, not an
infinite lattice.

`PERIODIC_STRENGTH = 0.5` scales every deposited mass (the note's «probably
2× less gravity»). Four times the bodies in the same field leaves the mean
pull to the crowd and the nearest well at half strength; half the strength
keeps the field in the band the aim UI and the bot's scoring were tuned on.
The `g field` slider stacks: ×1 → 0.5 effective, ×2 → classic per-body. One
named constant, tuned from `gravity_bench` / the stats strip, not a third
slider.

## 3. the solver

New file `gravity_fft.js`, classic script, `module.exports` guarded, no
library. One radix-2 complex FFT, 2-D as row then column. Loaded by
`index.html` before `gravity_sim.js`; `gravity_sim.js` reaches it with
`require('./gravity_fft.js')` under Node and `root.GravityFFT` in the page.

	GRID = 256                    // power of 2
	dx = WORLD_W / GRID           // 6.25 px
	dy = WORLD_H / GRID           // 3.906 px

Cells are anisotropic; the kernel uses the real `dx`, `dy` and the real
`kx = 2π ix / W`, `ky = 2π iy / H`. Softening spans ~10 × 15 cells, so CIC
interpolation of this kernel needs no P3M near-field.

Module-level, built once:

- `kAx`, `kAy` — FFT of the min-image unit Plummer acceleration at each
  cell (origin at (0,0), wrapped). Force `kAx[0] = kAy[0] = 0` explicitly;
  symmetry makes them nearly zero, but a uniform background must be exactly
  zero (the Jeans swindle for free).
- `kPhi` — FFT of the min-image potential, for the potential view only.

Per periodic `GravitySim`, allocated at construction, never in the step loop:

- `rho`, `Ax`, `Ay`, `phi` — `Float64Array(GRID * GRID)`
- a `gridDirty` flag and the solved field time

	deposit   CIC splat of `PERIODIC_STRENGTH * strength[i]` at body i,
	          wrapping the body into [0, W) × [0, H)
	solve     FFT(rho) → multiply by kAx / kAy (and kPhi) → IFFT
	sample    CIC gather of (Ax, Ay) at (x, y), wrapping the sample point,
	          then × `MU * gravityScale`

**When it solves.** Only while `fieldStatic` is true. `setField`, `setCount`,
`refreshField` and the first `spawn` build the grid once; a static field never
rebuilds during a flight. The `ports` source (`fieldStatic = false`) keeps the
direct sum with min-image distances: at most 32 bodies, already cheap, and it
does not pay for a 256² solve. If a future moving field carries 192 bodies,
that is a new measurement, not part of this option.

Each sim (live, helper probe, bot, opening planner, every rival, `burnShotSim`)
owns its grids so a prediction cannot clobber the live field. They share the
kernel tables.

`fieldAt` and `advance` take the FFT sample when `periodic && fieldStatic`, the
direct min-image sum otherwise. Prediction, live flight, bot probes and the
opening HQ planner therefore agree.

**Cost.** Three 256² complex transforms are 3 × 512 × (256 · log₂ 256)
butterflies ≈ 3.1 M complex butterflies, roughly 20–30 MFLOP — not the ~1 M
ops a 256² grid suggests. That is fine *once*: a career field is static, so a
solve happens at set-up and after a planet-count change, not per frame. The
direct-sum moving-port path stays at 32 bodies. Measure the set-up and
`predictFull` costs in `gravity_bench`; if a static rebuild is visible, solve on
a budgeted frame and keep the previous grid until the new one lands.

`GravitySim.MAX_BODIES = 192`; the WebGL shader's `uBodies[48]` does not grow,
because the periodic field view is a grid blit (section 6).

## 4. the torus

The play area **is** the planet field. `padX = padY = 0`. `outside()` is
never true. `END_EXIT` stays in the enum (replays, tests) and is simply
not produced.

After every `advance` / `stepOne` / `routeStep`:

	x = x - W * floor(x / W)
	y = y - H * floor(y / H)

`spawn` wraps the initial state too: a shot fired from a core at x = 10
towards the left starts at x = W − gap, not at −gap. Launch velocity is
unchanged.

Length uses the pre-wrap step (`hypot(vx, vy) * dt`), so a seam crossing does
not add 1600 px. Impact, system ellipses, `markSystemsAt`, `stepInsideAt`,
`fieldDrift` and Voronoi sound use the minimum-image delta:

	dx -= W * round(dx / W)
	dy -= H * round(dy / H)

`fieldDrift` matters even though the field is static: without min-image a
planet whose *position* is stored near the opposite edge reports a 1500 px
drift and forces a prediction rebuild every frame.

A planet at x = 10 is hittable from x = 1590; a visit ellipse that straddles
the edge counts on both sides. `planetXAt` / `planetYAt` keep returning the
unwrapped core + ellipse position: they are a drawing and landing identity, and
wrapping them would make a station jump once per orbit. The drawing layer draws
the ellipse as it is and adds ghost copies where it crosses an edge.

The `full area` radio and `z` hide. The camera stays on `main`. If the current
frame is `full` when the option turns on, reset it to `main`. The backdrop
paints one rect, not two. `?view=` does not carry the frame today and still
does not.

Bot rescue-on-`END_EXIT` becomes a dead branch; leave it, it never fires.
`careerDeparture` and the helper scans already require `END_IMPACT`, so a
periodic launch's `END_TIME` is not mistaken for an exit.

## 5. four times the planets

Constants, gated by the option:

| | off | on |
|---|---|---|
| array capacity `OrbitWorld.PLANETS` | 192 (both; see below) | 192 |
| UI / universe cap | 48 | 192 |
| lab / tour / money default | 26 | 104 |
| career default | 13 | 52 |
| min centre gap | 165 px, Euclidean, 110×90 inset | 81 px, toroidal, no inset |
| orbit cap | room-to-edge, up to 180 | 78, and the ellipse wraps |

`PLANETS` becomes 192 always so career pads, market arrays and the sim body
buffers do not resize on toggle. `PLANET_UNIVERSE_MAX` stops being
`const ... = PLANETS` in `gravity_gui.js`: it becomes `planetUniverseCap`, a
`let` initialised to 48. `planetMask` and the strength arrays are allocated at
`PLANETS` (192), so the cap can rise after the URL is read without reallocating;
every loop that means "the usable universe" uses the variable, not `PLANETS`.
`PLANET_DEFAULT` and `PLANET_CAREER_DEFAULT` follow the same option. The planet
input's `max` attribute and the `+` / `−` player buttons read the cap, not
`PLANETS`.

**Classic is a prefix, exactly.** With the option off `placePlanets` /
`placeOrbits` / `placeTraits` still consume the historical stream for 26,
`placeExtraPlanets` still fills 26…47 from `seed ^ 0x48ab91` with its 165 px
threshold, and 48…191 are left zero and never in play. Do not run the classic
placer for 48…191: it would spend time and leave unusable slots. With the
option on, `OrbitWorld.create(seed, rand, true)` fills the 192-cell lattice,
but the active map still takes the first `n` (104 lab / 52 career) in
bit-reversal order; the rest are latent capacity for a later `+` or a
max-universe setting.

**The toroidal placer is a lattice, not rejection sampling.** At 192 bodies a
81 px gap covers `192 · π · 40.5² ≈ 0.99 M px²` of 1.6 M (62 %), above the
random-sequential-adhesion limit; a 300-attempt rejection loop would either
stall or quietly accept overlaps. Use a 16 × 12 lattice:

	cell = 100 × 83.33 px
	col  = 0…15, row = 0…11
	x = (col + 0.5 + jitterX) * cellW
	y = (row + 0.5 + jitterY) * cellH
	jitter = ±1 px, seeded

The lattice's own nearest spacing is 83.33 px vertically, so ±1 px jitter still
guarantees 81.3 px. Visit ellipses overlap; that is the denser map. To keep a
small universe well spread, order the 192 cells by **bit-reversal** of
`row * 16 + col` and take the first `n`; the first 52 career worlds then sample
the whole field instead of the top three rows. The same order makes a seed's
first 26/52 stable when the universe grows.

Specials remain two indices among the first four, so every legal universe
(`PLANET_UNIVERSE_MIN = 4`) still holds both.

**The active count is not `PLANETS`.** Three code sites meant "the map is not
full" and used `PLANETS`; they must use the run's active count now:

- `gravity_bot.js` `careerBetweenFlights`: `career.padCount < PLANETS` →
  `career.padCount < career.planetCount` (otherwise a 52-planet career keeps
  trying to expand past its map).
- `gui_replay.js` prints `visited / PLANETS`; it must print the run's
  `planetCount`.
- any new loop over "all planets" keeps `PLANETS` only when it is really the
  array (arrays, masks); a gameplay loop uses `planetCount` / `planetMask`.

`GravitySim.MAX_BODIES = 192`. The WebGL shader's `uBodies[48]` does **not**
grow — periodic view does not loop bodies (section 6). Classic view still
caps at 48, which is the classic max.

Shot encoding: `SHOT_FORM` origin `(\d{1,2})` becomes `(\d{1,3})`. `END_FORM`
becomes `(\d{1,3})` too: `end=visited.steps` carries the number of visited
systems, and a 104/192-world run can print three digits. Both decoders keep
their existing numeric bounds (`origin < PLANETS`, `visited` is just a count),
so old links still match.

## 6. what you see

Physics, prediction and the field/potential view sample the **same grid**.
A 192-body uniform array would also blow typical WebGL1 fragment-uniform
limits, so the body-loop shader is the classic path only.

**Field view.** Reuse the existing 1/4-resolution field canvas instead of a
texture: after a solve, write `GRID × GRID` pixels into an offscreen canvas,
then `drawImage` it stretched over `FIELD_BOX` with smoothing. Hue = direction
of `a`, lightness = |a| on the same knee as today (`L_MAX`, `G_KNEE`); the
existing 2D fallback already owns the colour ramp. One blit per solve, no
extension, no float readback.

**Potential view.** It needs its own `kPhi` solve: the existing `sampleZone`
kernel is a surface-density bump, not φ. Colour φ on the same signed palette
(green pulls, blue repels) and label the mode as the periodic potential, not the
classic bump. Do not pretend the two are equal.

**Space view.** Trails and predictions: a polyline that jumps more than W/2
(H/2) is a seam, not a chord — split the path. Orbit ellipses, wells, visit
rings, signs and labels whose AABB crosses an edge are wrap-blitted (up to four
copies) so a well at x = 10 is visible on the right. The ship itself is always
inside the field after the wrap, so it needs no ghost.

## Validation

- `experiments/fft_test.js`, headless:
  - for a static 8-body field, FFT `sample` vs min-image direct `fieldAt`
    at 200 Poisson points: max relative error on |a| of 5 % outside 2ε of
    any body, 10 % inside (CIC + grid is the method, not a bug);
  - a ship that crosses x = W appears at x − W with `vy` unchanged, no
    `END_EXIT`, `flightLength` continuous;
  - min-image impact: a planet at x = 10, ship at x = W − 5 inside
    `planetR + CARGO_R` returns that planet;
  - `fieldDrift` across the seam is small, not ~W;
  - a moving `fieldStatic = false` sim takes the direct path and never calls
    the FFT;
  - with the option off, `advance` of a 240-step shot on seed 1 is
    byte-identical to HEAD.
- `gravity_seed_test`: first 26 positions and gravities of a seed match HEAD
  when the option is off; 192-world positions have no pair closer than the
  lattice gap; `&fft=1` round-trips on a link; `end=192.123` round-trips.
- `gravity_bench`, `career_gravity_bench`, `bot_bench` without `&fft=1`:
  byte-identical lines / known failure names unchanged.
- `dom_smoke`: checkbox present; off → `full area` visible, planet max 48;
  on → `full area` gone, planet max 192, tag carries `fft`; `route` disables
  the checkbox; a tour boot with `&fft=1` wraps a shot that would have exited.
- `node --check` on every touched script, `git diff --check`.

## Scope boundary

No space weather, no jammer, no change to EMC / harvest / career scoring, no
world-width change (that is `0.7.4`). No third gravity slider —
`PERIODIC_STRENGTH` is a constant. No infinite Ewald sum and no 2D
log-potential. No raising the WebGL `uBodies` cap. No periodic `route`. Career
«complete» on 52 planets and the O(n²) opening search may get slower; measure,
do not rebalance in this plan. Classic open-plane physics, seeds and benches do
not move.
