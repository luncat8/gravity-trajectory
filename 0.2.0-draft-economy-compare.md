# 0.2.0 draft — trade, economy and colony growth: this build vs `galaxy-flythrough@31c6d5a`

Comparison of the trade backdrop shipped here with the colony-economy stage of
`luncat8/galaxy-flythrough` at `31c6d5a4b9e06eb87b79fb334c0defc535b5a0c4`
("ui.19: colony trade made visible"). The SHA is **not in this repository** — it
is the tip of that repo's colony-economy series (`colony-economy-plan.md`,
`src/core/colony.js`, `src/core/trade.js`). Everywhere below "31c6d5a" means that
tree; "here" means `orbit_sim.js` + `orbit_view.js` in this repo.

## 1. What each side actually is

| | here (`orbit_sim.js`, vendored exec_queue 0.4.0) | 31c6d5a (`colony.js` + `trade.js` + `main.js`) |
|---|---|---|
| world | 26 planets in a 1600×1000 field, one station per planet on a fixed ellipse | a galaxy: streamed stars, one station per star, 3D kpc, stars drift on star time |
| time | sim minutes on a heap event queue, fast-forward to `t+07:00`, `×0.5` | years on a colony clock (`econY`) = ship clock in game mode, star clock when synced (0.1 Myr/s default) |
| goods | 3 abstract: ice / ore / gas, each port specialises in one | 6: rare elements, special chemicals, antimatter, food, fab cores, software/data |
| "resource map" | the port's speciality is balanced by count only | deterministic per-system ease from star metal/lum + rock/giant/terran/belt fractions + hash jitter |
| production | `portProd` of its own good, `portCons` eats the other two, stock cap 140 | 6-rate production driven by capital/pop, 6-rate need driven by pop, stock uncapped |
| trade actor | one NPC ship per port, nearest-4 partners, flies its own good out and a partner's good back | one NPC ship per port, **every colony's best export** to a commissioned destination |
| who decides routes | `refreshPartners()` every tick, automatic | the player commissions routes (`PERIOD_Y = 2`, `CAP_T = 50 t`, 2-year guard), plus an expansion scenario that commissions on arrival |
| money | none | credits: `1.6 − 1.2·supply` × base, buy/sell at the docked port |
| colony growth | `portProgress += tons` on load and on delivery, `EXPAND_COST = 650` fills → colony ship to the nearest empty planet within 420 px | logistic population with carrying capacity `K`, comfort from met needs, famine below 0.4, capital reinvested from surplus |
| expansion | colony ship (44 px/min), target reserved, one per port | founded colonies sponsor founder expeditions (max 24 colonies, 8 in flight), auto-seeded Home, routes mesh on arrival |
| failure model | none; ports never starve | comfort shortfall, famine/recovery events, population floor 0.05k |
| events | `LOAD` / `ARRIVE` / `TICK` / `BUILD` in the shared min-heap | a separate 64-slot insertion-sorted ledger for depart/arrive/milestone/famine/expedition |
| UI | HUD stats (ports, ships, delivered, colonies, aim error), port hover with trade/growth %, growth ring per port, link/route toggles | economy panel (seed Home, expansion, colony list, route list, commission row, event log), HUD economy line, trade-net overlay on the canvas, market rows in the flight/system panels |

Measured here (`experiments`-style node run, seeds 1/7/42): 8 seed ports →
19–22 ports after the 7-hour fast-forward, **all 26 planets colonised by ~2000
sim minutes (33 h)**, 2.4–2.5 M t delivered by 60 000 min, growth rings pinned
at `EXPAND_COST` afterwards, aim error exactly 0 (closed-form rendezvous).

## 2. 31c6d5a: the plan vs what shipped

The plan (`colony-economy-plan.md`) is good and honest; the implementation,
measured, drifts from it in six places. Numbers below come from running
`src/core/colony.js` and `src/core/trade.js` directly with an average solar
system and a metal-poor one.

| plan says | code does | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| food dominates the need side: 20 t per 1k persons per year | `NEED = [20, 1.0, 0.5, 0.2, 0.2, 0.05]` indexed `rare, chem, anti, food, fab, info` — food gets **0.2**, rare gets 20 (the plan's table, transposed) | **bug**: famine is caused by rare elements; food is over-produced |
| factories run "per ship arrival or on inspection" | `advanceColony` is called from exactly two places: `colonyArrive` and `colonyInspect` | **bug**: the economy is frozen unless the player inspects; routes/expansion run on `econY` regardless |
| a starving colony (comfort < 0.4) loses population | comfort settles *exactly* at 0.40 and chatters across the threshold; every crossing emits a FAMINE event | **bug**: the ledger fills with famine/recovery pairs |
| a full ledger drops the latest event, the head survives | `schedule` returns false while `count == 64`, and the drain handler's re-arm runs **before** the pop — so at cap every route re-arm is silently refused | **bug**: routes die permanently once colonies start aging |
| routes move the surplus to where it is scarce | `departureCargo` takes `bestExport` with no look at the destination, price or need; `CAP_T/PERIOD_Y` = **25 t/y** vs a 67k colony's **1331 t/y** rare need | **bug**: the network is decorative (1.7 % of demand) |
| the player's margin comes from price differences | `buyAtPort`/`sellAtPort` never touch `colony.stock`; `bestExport` does not check stock; `marketSupply` is 60 % ease, 40 % stock | **bug**: prices never react, buying creates goods from nothing |
| expansion paces itself to the fleet | `nextExpandY = econY + Math.max(PERIOD_Y, eta.years)`; a diverged ETA is `Infinity` | **bug**: one outrun star blocks its source forever |
| a colony is founded with `stock = 0` and grows when needs are met | measured: founded comfort 0.64 (terran-rich), **0.32** (metal-poor) → the second starves from day one; food stock reaches 1.95 M t at 900 y | **bug**: founding is a coin flip, one good is unbounded |

Measured (metal-poor star, 1 000 y): pop 2.63k → 27.7k → plateau, comfort 0.40,
rare stock 0, food 126k t. Same on the rich star: pop 101k, comfort 0.40,
food 1.9 M t. Every colony ends in the same 0.40 famine equilibrium —
the six-resource map stops mattering after the first century.

Also measured: `advanceColony(1000 y)` vs 4 000 × `advanceColony(0.25 y)` gives
pop 73.02 vs 72.75 — the coarse fallback is numerically fine; it is the *event
ledger* and the *stale comfort gate* that break, not the integrator.

## 3. What 31c6d5a gets right and is worth taking

Ranked by value for a gravity-cargo backdrop, with the port cost into this
architecture (classic scripts, typed arrays, no allocation in the frame loop).

1. **Needs + comfort as the growth law** (not tonnage). A port grows because
   its people are fed, and a shortfall is visible (`comfort 62 %`, `starving`).
   Here: replace `portProgress += tons` with `growth += f(comfort)`, derive need
   from the two goods the port cannot make. Keep the existing 3 goods; the
   6-resource table is not needed to get the mechanic. *Cost: ~1 evening; the
   `accrue()` pass already exists; no new allocations.*
2. **A stable per-port resource map.** 31c6d5a derives ease from star metal,
   luminosity, rock/giant/terran fractions and belt count, hashed by star key.
   Here the analogue is per-planet: orbit radius (light), orbit period/tilt
   (volatility), planet radius (bulk), the port's good. Deterministic, so a
   reload reproduces the same world. *Cost: small; needs a handful of planet
   traits promoted to readable fields.*
3. **Colony stages instead of one threshold.** 31c6d5a's milestone ladder
   (0.5k → 40k) and "big colony" gate from the follow-up commit (pop ≥ 5 M,
   coverage ≥ 0.30) give a colony a *story*: outpost → town → city → sponsor of
   new colonies. Here: 3–4 stages on ring colour/size, expansion allowed only
   from a grown port, "newest colony" cue. *Cost: small; visual + one gate.*
4. **Commissioned routes + a route list.** Player-authored edges between two
   ports, a standing convoy per route, cancel, and the trade-net overlay is a
   cheap and legible visual of a growing network. Automatic nearest-4 stays as
   the default; commissions are the player's lever. *Cost: medium; the renderer
   already draws links and ships.*
5. **A narrative event log.** 31c6d5a's `econLog` (48 lines, event cadence) and
   the ledger's milestone/famine events are what makes the background read as
   "alive" from the HUD. Here the trade log was deliberately stripped; a
   20-line ring buffer costs nothing and never runs in the frame loop. *Cost:
   small.*
6. **Budgeted expansion scans + Home anchor.** "One scan at a time, sliced per
   frame with a 2 ms budget, never a hitch" and "centre the camera on the first
   colony" are reusable patterns even without stars. Here: `scoreTarget` already
   scans 26 planets — the *pattern* matters only if the world grows.
7. **Habitat scoring for the next colony** (from the follow-up commit, not
   31c6d5a itself): `habitat/(1 + ly/80)` instead of nearest-empty, so the
   network grows toward good worlds and not just the closest dot. *Cost: tiny;
   `scoreTarget` is one loop.*

## 4. What 31c6d5a does that should **not** be picked

- **On-demand factories** (advance only on arrive/inspect). It split the
  timeline in two: routes and expansion ran on the clock, colonies did not, so
  every readout was stale and the expansion gate read founding comfort forever.
  This sim is cheap enough to advance everyone per event tick.
- **Money and prices as shipped.** Stock-reactive pricing is only worth it if
  buying/selling moves the stock; otherwise the market is a vending machine
  (see §2). If credits are wanted, ship the stock side-effects with them.
- **The absolute-space convoy/ETA machinery.** It was written for stars drifting
  at 220 km/s and needed the "lane frame = origin" correction. Here the stations
  already move on closed-form ellipses and the rendezvous is solved exactly
  (`solveRendezvous`, aim error 0), so porting that math would be a regression.
- **The 64-slot ledger with drop-latest.** Its cap interacts badly with event
  churn. If a ledger is wanted here, the heap already exists — and events that
  must survive (route re-arms) should never share a capped queue with chatter.
- **The 13-resource industrial input matrix** from the later rewrite. It is a
  different game (no money, material balance, per-planet-type table); it is
  interesting as a design but is a rewrite, not a port.

## 5. Open decisions

1. **How deep does the economy go here?** three goods + needs/comfort only;
   + resources map and colony stages; or + credits/stock-reactive prices.
2. **Who owns the network?** keep automatic nearest-4; add player commissions;
   or replace automatic with expansion automation (Home → frontiers).
3. **Does the cargo experiment touch the economy?** a landed container could
   deliver goods, seed a colony or knock a port out; or stay a pure physics
   toy with the trade world as an untouched backdrop.
4. **How much GUI?** HUD line + hover only; a colony/route/event panel; the
   trade-net overlay on the canvas.

## 6. Decisions taken

- Take everything in §3 except the money: needs/comfort growth, the deterministic
  resource map, colony stages, automatic trading with a player-visible log, the
  budgeted expansion pattern, and habitat scoring. Prices stay deferred; the
  market reports supply and tags only.
- Two tiers instead of one: three goods inside the system (`bulk`, `goods`,
  `migrants`), seven between systems (`rare`, `exotic`, `antimatter`, `food`,
  `fabricators`, `info`, `colonists`). The inter-star tier is an average per
  system with one port per star — no inter-planet movement is simulated until the
  system is open, and the page is the system view.
- The network stays automatic (nearest-four partners); no player commissioning.
- A landed cargo container seeds a colony on a free planet.
- GUI: HUD, hover, a panel with the log, and the sky overlay.

Specified in `0.2.0-plan-colony-economy.md`.
