# 0.7.1 plan — the time model

Standalone spec: a fork can rebuild this from this file alone. Source note:
`drafts/career-economy.md` §2.9:

	i think we (should) have only 2 time
	- 'career' - actually not just for career but main time for game, useful for fly and play
	- background animation (trade, orbits) - this should be only in settings panel, no need in footer, because it currently not gameplay important.

That split exists. What does not is one written unit contract: the code calls a
sim second a "minute" in one place and a "year" in another, and the two are 60
apart. Wear (0.7.2) cannot be priced until the clocks have names.

## problem

Three conversions are in the tree today:

- `GravitySim.DT = 1/120`. `game.clockSteps` counts these steps. A flight's
  `flightTime`, the turn of an EMC barrel, a burn step and a contract deadline
  are all in **sim seconds**.
- Pad energy runs on **world seconds**: `careerAdvance` computes
  `seconds = steps · DT · rate · 60` and charges a panel at `power · light ·
  seconds / 3600`. The `wait · 30 min` button advances 30 world minutes (1800
  world seconds) of panel charge — 60 sim seconds, because a sim second is 60
  world seconds. The label says 30 minutes; the flight clock moves 60 s.
- Battery self-discharge gets `steps · DT · rate` (sim seconds) and its
  parameter is named `dtYears`; the table's `leak` is documented “per year”.
  The arithmetic is per **sim second**, whatever the name says.
- `politicsSeconds(market, step)` returns `step · DT · politicsRate` — sim
  seconds — while the help and drafts call it “world seconds”.

So the same flight second is 1 political second, 1 leak year and 60 pad
seconds. None of those units is written down, and `WORLD_SEC_PER_MIN = 60`
is a constant whose name says the opposite of its use.

The draft's second clock — the orbital-trade animation — is genuinely a
background clock in world **minutes** (`tradeSim.t`, `START_MINUTES = 420`,
`advanceTo`). It is a display clock: it owns station positions and trade
cycles, never a ship's age or a repair bill.

## 1. the units

Add three static members to `gravity_sim.js` (no new file, already required by
every consumer):

	GravitySim.DT                     // 1/120, unchanged
	GravitySim.WORLD_SEC_PER_SIM_SEC  // 60, unchanged for now
	GravitySim.WAIT_SECONDS           // 1800 = 30 world minutes
	GravitySim.simSeconds(steps, rate) {
		return steps * this.DT * rate;
	}
	GravitySim.worldSeconds(steps, rate) {
		return this.simSeconds(steps, rate) * this.WORLD_SEC_PER_SIM_SEC;
	}
	GravitySim.stepsForWorldSeconds(seconds, rate) {
		return Math.max(1, Math.round(seconds /
			(this.DT * rate * this.WORLD_SEC_PER_SIM_SEC)));
	}

The contract:

| clock | unit | readers |
|---|---|---|
| sim time | second of flight, `steps · DT · rate` | flight length/time, burns, turret, contracts, politics, **wear**, ship age |
| world time | 60 sim seconds, `steps · DT · rate · 60` | panel charge, battery leak, pad refill waits, `wait · 30 min` |
| orbit time | world minutes, `tradeSim.t` | station orbits, trade cycles, the background panel |

`rate` is `scene.trade.speed` / `career.rate`. One step at rate 2 is two sim
seconds; one step at rate 2 is 120 world seconds. Do not multiply by `60` in a
reader; call `worldSeconds`.

The draft's “two clocks” hold: career/flight time is the gameplay clock; the
orbital animation is the background clock. World seconds are not a third
gameplay clock — they are the unit inside the career's pad accounting, the same
way a battery has its own kWh.

## 2. what changes

### `gravity_game.js`

- Delete `WORLD_SEC_PER_MIN`. `careerAdvance` becomes:

		const simSec = GravitySim.simSeconds(steps, rate);
		const worldSec = GravitySim.worldSeconds(steps, rate);
		const charge = OrbitTech.batteryLeak(pad.battery, pad.chargeKwh, simSec) +
			OrbitTech.solarCharge(pad.panel, career.light[i], worldSec);

  The two numbers are deliberately different: a panel charges on the pad's
  day, a battery self-discharges on the ship's own clock. The existing
  behaviour is preserved exactly.
- `politicsSeconds` gets a comment and a rename in prose: it is **flight
  seconds**, not world seconds. Its arithmetic does not change in this plan.
  If the desired `120 s` corruption grace is really 120 world seconds, that is
  a balance change (60× faster) and belongs in a politics pass, not here.
- `careerWait` / `gui_career` build their step count with
  `GravitySim.stepsForWorldSeconds(GravitySim.WAIT_SECONDS, rate)` instead of
  `CAREER_WAIT_MIN / (DT * rate)`. Same number today.
- `CAREER_WAIT_MIN` stays a **label** (30), derived from `WAIT_SECONDS / 60`;
  no gameplay reads it.

### `gravity_bot.js`

- `careerPadGear`: `const flight = GravitySim.worldSeconds(record.steps,
  this.careerRate || 1);` (replaces `· 60`). A pad cycle is compared with pad
  refill waits, both world seconds.
- `CAREER_WAIT_SECONDS` becomes `GravitySim.WAIT_SECONDS`; keep the exported
  alias because `career_bot_bench` reads it.
- `careerRefillWait` already divides kWh by `power · light / 3600`, a world
  second; no change.

### `orbit_tech.js`

- `batteryLeak(battery, chargeKwh, dtSec)` (was `dtYears`). Document `leak` as
  “per second of flight”, not per year. **Do not re-scale the table**: the
  current numbers already multiply sim seconds, so renaming the parameter is
  the whole change and research costs / `METRIC_SCALE.battery` stay put.
- `solarCharge(panel, light, dtSec)` documents `dtSec` as world seconds.
- `README.md`'s `orbit_tech.js` line and the battery/panel rows stop saying
  “per year”.

### UI

- `wait · 30 min` stays in this plan. Its title says “30 world minutes = 60 s of
  flight at ×1” once, so the two clocks are visible without a new panel.
  `drafts/career-economy.md` §2.9 then asks for the label to become the real
  wait in wall-clock seconds; that rename is the next plan, not this one.
- `careerWaitInfo` keeps minutes; it is a world-time label.
- With the helpers landed, two readers still do the *panel* arithmetic by hand
  and must call `worldSeconds`: `gui_career.waitSettlement` (`minutes · 60` for
  `solarCharge`) and `gui_shop.panelWaitKwh` (`CAREER_WAIT_MIN * 60`, read by
  `waitGain`, `panelReadySec` and the `solar +X kWh/wait` line). Routing them
  through `GravitySim.worldSeconds` makes the next rename one constant instead
  of a grep.
- A career tooltip on the clock shows `flight 1:23 · pad 1 h 23 min` only if a
  readout needs both; not a new HUD row.

## 3. why not switch to 1:1 now

The obvious “real time” reading — one flight second equals one world second —
would make every panel 60× slower per flight second, a 30-minute wait 30
minutes of flight, and every rival's pad payback 60× longer. That is a full
economy rebalance (panel power, wait length, pad payback horizon, corruption
grace, fuel logistics). It is not a rename, and it would make the 0.7.2 wear
numbers look wrong for reasons that have nothing to do with wear.

This plan keeps `WORLD_SEC_PER_SIM_SEC = 60` and removes the ambiguity. If a
later pass wants 1:1, it changes one constant and re-scales the panel table:

	WORLD_SEC_PER_SIM_SEC = 1
	PANELS[].power *= 60          // same kWh per flight second
	WAIT_SECONDS = 1800           // 30 min of flight, now 30 min of world time
	// politics, leak and wear need no factor: they are already sim seconds

That pass is a bench-and-balance job, not a prerequisite for wear. The wear plan
prices seconds of flight, so it is independent of this constant.

**Decided (drafts §2.9):** 1:1 is rejected. The next step is the other reading
of «1x = real time» — keep this constant and the physics exactly as they are,
and renumber the *labels* so that today's default pace is called `×1`
(`wait · 30 min` = 60 flight s = 15 s of wall clock, so it reads
`wait · 15 s`). That step changes no number this plan pins, which is why it can
follow rather than lead.

## order

1. Add the three helpers and a test that `GravitySim.DT` is the sim step and
   `worldSeconds(120, 1) = 60`.
2. Convert `careerAdvance`, `careerPadGear`, `careerWait`, `waitSettlement` and
   `panelWaitKwh`; run
   `career_bench`/`career_stats_test`; the numbers must be byte-identical.
3. Rename `dtYears` and the README/docs. No table edits.
4. `grep` for `dtYears`, `WORLD_SEC_PER_MIN`, `* 60`, `/ 3600`; every remaining
   `* 60` must be a label, a trade-clock minute, or a deliberate constant with
   a comment.
5. Only then start 0.7.2; its `WEAR_SPACE` is `condition per sim second`.

## validation

- `career_bench`: the exact pad charge after `10 s` and `600 s` waits equals
  HEAD; leak after the same waits equals HEAD.
- `career_stats_test`: income/running/property/science unchanged.
- `politics_test`: `politicsSeconds` values unchanged (still sim seconds).
- A unit test on the helpers: `simSeconds(120, 1) = 1`,
  `worldSeconds(120, 1) = 60`, `stepsForWorldSeconds(1800, 1) = 3600`.
- `dom_smoke` pins `wait · 30 min` and `evacuate · 6.00` by text, so this plan
  changes neither label; the label rename (drafts §2.9) updates those checks
  in its own pass.
- `grep -rn "dtYears\|WORLD_SEC_PER_MIN" --include="*.js" .` is empty;
  `grep` for “per year” in `README.md`/`orbit_tech.js` is empty (the archive
  may keep history).
- `node --check` on every touched script, `git diff --check`.

## scope boundary

No wear, no repair prices, no fleet. No change to `WORLD_SEC_PER_SIM_SEC`, panel
power, wait length, corruption grace or politics rates. No new HUD clock. No
“year” unit anywhere in the live game. The orbital-trade animation remains a
background display clock, as §2.9 asks.
