# 0.7.1 plan — the time model and its labels

Standalone spec: a fork can rebuild this from this file alone. Source notes:
`drafts/career-economy.md` §2.9 (two clocks; *normalize time to 1x = real time*)
and `drafts/time-scale-units.md`:

	i think we (should) have only 2 time
	- 'career' - actually not just for career but main time for game, useful for fly and play
	- background animation (trade, orbits) - this should be only in settings panel, no need in footer, because it currently not gameplay important.

	i think better normalize it to 1x speed be IRL timer.
	currently "wain 30 min" button work good but text told player not useful info
	that is hard to imagine. so better operate real time with proper scale and tell
	how many real time it wait fast forward.
	current default we use x4. this is good speed so we can name it x1
	after this refactoring actual speed should remain same, but set visual units more simple.

Two jobs, one plan: give the clocks a written contract, then make the labels say
what the player can feel. The second job is label-only — the pace the game is
balanced at does not move.

## problem

Four conversions were in the tree, none of them written down:

- `GravitySim.DT = 1/120`. `game.clockSteps` counts these steps and a flight's
  `flightTime` is `liveSteps · DT` — **flight seconds**, the clock the HUD, the
  board deadlines, the contracts, the barrels and the arrests read.
- Pad energy ran on **world time**: `careerAdvance` computed
  `minutes = steps · DT · rate` and charged a panel at
  `power · light · (minutes · 60) / 3600`. `rate` is `scene.trade.speed`, the
  background slider — so one local was a *minute* for the panel, a *second*
  times 60 for `solarCharge` and a *year* for `batteryLeak`'s `dtYears`, with
  `WORLD_SEC_PER_MIN` declared in `gravity_game.js` where only the pads read it.
  The year was not a mistake: `OrbitSim.ECON_YEARS_PER_MIN = 1`, so an orbit
  minute *is* a colony year and the leak really was priced per year. What no
  file said is that the leak's year and the panel's second are the same product
  of `steps · DT · rate`, 60 apart — nor that neither is a second of flight.
- `politicsSeconds(market, step)` returned `step · DT · politicsRate` — the same
  product as the pad's `minutes` — while `OrbitEcon` documents its
  `CORRUPTION_GRACE = 120` as “world seconds”. `ARREST_SECONDS` alone really is
  flight seconds (`runStep + round(120 / DT)`).
- The labels then mixed all of it: `wait · 30 min` (world minutes), `ready in
  N s` (flight seconds), `play ×4` (flight seconds per real second), `rate ×0.5`
  (world minutes per flight second) — four numbers, no unit named on screen.

Two arithmetic traps a reimplementer falls into, both present in earlier drafts
of this plan and in `archive/0.7.5-worklog.md`:

1. `steps · DT · rate` is **not** a flight second. It is a *world minute*: one
   flight second is `steps · DT` whatever `rate` is. Wear, deadlines and every
   other flight-clock reader must not see `rate`, or the background animation
   slider becomes a cheat knob for the economy and for ship ageing.
2. The wait button does **not** cost 15 real seconds. `CAREER_WAIT_MS = 2000`:
   it fast-forwards its whole span in 2 s of eased real time (stretching below
   ~50 fps). 15 s is what watching the same span at the balanced pace would
   cost. Both numbers belong in the tooltip; only the 2 s belongs on the button.

The draft's second clock — the orbital-trade animation — is genuinely a
background clock in world **minutes** (`tradeSim.t`, `START_MINUTES = 420`,
`advanceTo`). It owns station positions and trade cycles, never a ship's age or
a repair bill. It stays in its own panel, out of the footer.

## 1. the clocks

One contract, in `gravity_sim.js` (no new file: every consumer already requires
it, page and node alike):

	GravitySim.DT                     // 1/120, the one physics step
	GravitySim.WORLD_SEC_PER_MIN      // 60
	GravitySim.WORLD_RATE_DEFAULT     // 0.5, the world rate the balance assumes
	GravitySim.WAIT_WORLD_SEC         // 1800 = one wait = 30 world minutes
	GravitySim.flightSeconds(steps)             // steps · DT
	GravitySim.worldMinutes(steps, rate)        // flight s · rate
	GravitySim.worldSeconds(steps, rate)        // world min · 60
	GravitySim.stepsForWorldSeconds(sec, rate)  // ≥ 1, rounded
	GravitySim.flightSecondsForWorld(sec, rate) // sec / (60 · rate), unrounded
	GravitySim.wallSeconds(steps, pace)         // flight s / pace, 0 when paused

| clock | unit | formula | readers |
|---|---|---|---|
| flight | second of flight | `steps · DT` | `flightTime`, board and contract deadlines, barrel turns, arrest, planet-profit age, politics' step base, **wear** (0.7.2), ship age |
| world | minute / second | `steps · DT · rate`, `· 60` | panel charge, battery leak, pad refill waits, the political clock, `tradeSim.t` |
| real | second of wall clock | `steps · DT / pace` | the wait button, its tooltip, the pace tag |
| orbit | world minute | `tradeSim.t` | station orbits, trade cycles, the background panel |

`rate` is `scene.trade.speed` (the world rate, `0.5…12`, default `0.5`) and
`pace` is `playPace()` — the playback rung times the `game` slider, capped at
`PLAY_MAX` exactly as the step loop caps it, in flight seconds per real second.
They are different knobs and neither belongs to the step: a step is `DT` of
flight time always.
No reader multiplies by 60 or by a rate of its own — it calls a helper.

The draft's “two clocks” hold: flight time is the gameplay clock, the orbital
animation is the background clock. World minutes are not a third clock on
screen — they are the unit inside the career's pad and political accounting, the
same way a battery has its own kWh. Real seconds are not a fourth: they are what
any flight-clock span costs the player at the live pace.

At the defaults (`rate 0.5`, career `pace 4`) one wait is:

	7200 steps = 60 flight s = 30 world min = 1800 world s of sunlight
	           = 15 real s to watch = 2 real s through the button
	           = +0.50 kWh on an SP-1 in full sun (BAT-1 full in 3 waits)

## 2. the labels

The one rule: **a pace label reads relative to the pace that mode is balanced
at, and a time the player spends reads in real seconds.**

- `play ×N` — both ladders are six rungs reading `×0.25 … ×8`; only the raw pace
  differs. `PLAY_SPEEDS = [0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 8]` in the lab, where ×1 is real
  time; `CAREER_SPEEDS = [1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32]` in career, where ×1 is the raw
  pace 4 the career economy was priced at and the ceiling is the raw 32. One
  index, one default (`PLAY_DEFAULT = 2`), one max (`PLAY_MAX_INDEX = 5`) serve
  both, so a mode switch only calls `resetPlayback()`. `CAREER_PACE_UNIT = 4` is
  the single number a career label divides by; `playScale()` keeps returning the
  raw pace, so the step loop, the tests and the physics are untouched. The
  `game` slider (`gameRate`, ×0.25…×8) still multiplies the rung, and the tag
  shows the product — capped, because the step loop caps it: `playPace()` is
  `min(playScale(), PLAY_MAX)`, and it is the pace every label and every
  real-second readout uses, so the tag never promises a pace the loop will not
  run (rung ×8 with the slider at ×2 asks for 64 and gets 32).
- `wait · 2 s` — the real seconds the press costs (`CAREER_WAIT_SEC`), the only
  time the player spends. Never world minutes.
- its live line — what the press buys, in the units those gains are in:
  `wait → P3 +0.50 kWh · board −60 s · visits +0.30` (flight seconds for the
  board, kWh for the pad).
- its tooltip — the only place all three clocks meet:
  `fast-forward 2 s of real time · the clock runs 60 s (30 world min, 15 s to
  watch at ×1): P3 +0.50 kWh · board −60 s (2 offers rotate) · planet visits
  +0.30 (3 growing)`.
- while it runs — the button counts percent (`waiting · 41 %`), the line counts
  the real seconds and the clock they buy:
  `fast-forward · 0.8 / 2 s · clock +24 s · P3 0.90 / 1.50 kWh`.
- the notice at the end — `clock +60 s · P3 pad +0.50 kWh`.
- `rate ×0.5` in background settings keeps its own unit and says it in the
  title: world minutes per flight second, the clock the pads and the politics
  live on during a run. It is the draft's background control; the URL params
  (`&rate=`, `&speed=`) keep the raw value, so every link still re-flies.
- `ready in N s`, `gun · N s`, `board −N s`, `t+ 0:01:00` stay flight seconds:
  they are deadlines on the game clock, and a deadline that moved with the pace
  slider would be unusable. The pace tag is the conversion.
- Because the wait's tooltip names the pace, it follows it: `refreshPlaybackUi`
  ends in `refreshCareerWaitPreview()`, which repaints the button and its line
  (not the drawer) whenever the rung, the `game` slider or a mode switch moves
  the pace — and only while the preview is on screen, since `startGame` resets
  the pace before the career it is starting has pads. The `game` slider's own
  handler now calls `refreshPlaybackUi()`, which it did not before, so the tag
  stops going stale while the slider moves.

## 3. what changes

### `gravity_sim.js`

The constants and the six helpers above, with the contract as their comment.

### `gravity_game.js`

- `WORLD_SEC_PER_MIN` is deleted; `careerAdvance` reads both clocks it actually
  uses, and says why they differ:

		const worldMin = GravitySim.worldMinutes(steps, rate);
		const worldSec = GravitySim.worldSeconds(steps, rate);
		const charge = OrbitTech.batteryLeak(pad.battery, pad.chargeKwh, worldMin) +
			OrbitTech.solarCharge(pad.panel, career.light[i], worldSec);

  A panel charges over world seconds, a battery leaks over world minutes: the
  table's `leak` is a fraction per world minute, and re-scaling it is a balance
  job, not a rename. Both numbers come from one `rate`, so the arithmetic is
  byte-identical to the old `minutes` / `minutes · 60`.
- `politicsSeconds` becomes `GravitySim.worldMinutes(step, market.politicsRate)`
  and its comment says the truth: the political clock counts world minutes while
  every `OrbitEcon.*_SECONDS` constant is priced in them, so the name lies about
  the unit (`CORRUPTION_GRACE = 120` is two world hours). Renaming that table
  would move the balance; `orbit_econ.js` documents the unit instead.
- `CAREER` documents that every `*Sec` in it (`deliverySec`, `competeSec`,
  `turretMinSec`, `turretSpanSec`) is flight seconds; `arrestCareer` and
  `turretAngle` say the same for `ARREST_SECONDS` and the barrel period.

### `gravity_bot.js`

- `CAREER_WAIT_SECONDS = GravitySim.WAIT_WORLD_SEC`; the export stays because
  `career_bot_bench` reads it.
- `careerPadGear`: `const flownSec = GravitySim.worldSeconds(record.steps,
  this.careerRate || 1)` replaces `record.steps · DT · rate · 60`, and the local
  stops being called `flight` — a pad cycle is world seconds, compared with
  `careerRefillWait`'s world seconds.
- `careerRefillWait` already divides kWh by `power · light / 3600`, a world
  second; no change.

### `orbit_tech.js` / `orbit_science.js` / `orbit_econ.js`

- `batteryLeak(battery, chargeKwh, worldMin)` (was `dtYears`) and
  `solarCharge(panel, light, worldSec)`, each documented with its unit; the
  `BATTERIES` table comment says “leak fraction per world minute” and the
  research-unit note in `orbit_science.js` says “per world minute”. **No table
  value moves**, so research costs and `METRIC_SCALE.battery` stay put.
- `orbit_econ.js` §8 states the political clock once for all its constants, and
  marks `ARREST_SECONDS` as the flight-second exception.

### `gui_tables.js`

- `CAREER_WAIT_MIN` is gone. `CAREER_WAIT_SEC = CAREER_WAIT_MS / 1000` is the
  label; `CAREER_WAIT_WORLD_MIN = WAIT_WORLD_SEC / WORLD_SEC_PER_MIN` is what
  the orbit animation advances (`tradeSim.advanceTo`). Both are derived, so a
  re-priced wait is one constant.
- The two pace ladders, `PLAY_DEFAULT`, `PLAY_MAX_INDEX`, `CAREER_PACE_UNIT`,
  `PLAY_MAX = CAREER_SPEEDS[PLAY_MAX_INDEX]`. The dead `GAME_SLICE` and
  `PLAY_GUARD` go with the old single ladder.

### `gui_view.js`

`playLadder()`, `paceUnit()`, `playPace()`, `paceTag()`; `playScale()` keeps
returning the raw pace; `refreshPlaybackUi` prints `paceTag()` and a `reset`
button whose title states what ×1 means in this mode, then repaints the career
wait preview. `playDefaultIndex()` and `playMaxIndex()` are gone — both ladders
share their indices.

### `gui_career.js`

- `waitSettlement` builds its steps with `stepsForWorldSeconds(WAIT_WORLD_SEC,
  rate)`, charges through `worldMinutes`/`worldSeconds`, and returns the two new
  numbers the labels need: `worldMin` and `watchSec = wallSeconds(steps,
  playPace())`.
- `waitCareer` uses the same helper for its step count; `careerWaitTick`
  advances the orbit clock by `CAREER_WAIT_WORLD_MIN · eased`; the progress line
  and the notice read as in §2; `realSecText(sec)` formats a real duration
  (one decimal below 10 s, whole above).
- `refreshCareerWaitPreview()` is the pace-side entry point: it repaints the
  button, its line and its tooltip alone, guarded by `careerRule()`, the
  button's own `hidden` and `careerWaitBusy()`.

### `gui_shop.js`

- `panelWaitKwh` charges `GravitySim.WAIT_WORLD_SEC` (was `CAREER_WAIT_MIN * 60`)
  and `waitClockSec(rate)` is `flightSecondsForWorld(WAIT_WORLD_SEC, rate)` — the
  flight seconds one wait spans, which is what `panelReadySec` and the pad
  block's `ready in N s` multiply their wait count by. Same numbers, no `rate`
  guard duplicated.
- `rebelMultiple` asks `GravityGame.politicsSeconds` for the rebellion's age
  instead of re-deriving `· DT · politicsRate`.

### `index.html` / `README.md` / docs

The button's static text and title, the playback group's title, the `rate` and
`game` slider titles; the README's wait, playback and world-rate rows, a new
**Clocks** bullet in *mechanics & visuals*, the barrel's “world time” → “flight
time”, and the `orbit_tech.js` and `gravity_sim.js` file lines. A “year”
survives only where it is a real unit — the colony economy's colony year, which
`OrbitSim.ECON_YEARS_PER_MIN = 1` makes the same minute the orbit clock runs on.
`balance_lab.html` keeps its literal but names `GravitySim.WAIT_WORLD_SEC` as the
source it mirrors.

## 4. why not 1 flight second = 1 real second

The literal reading of “1x = IRL timer” — one flight second per real second —
would make every flight four times longer to watch at the pace the game is
balanced at, and would invalidate every number priced in flight seconds:
`deliverySec 240`, `competeSec 180`, `ARREST_SECONDS`, the barrel periods, the
visit-profit ramp and 0.7.2's `WEAR_SPACE`. That is a full rebalance, not a
rename, and it would make the wear numbers look wrong for reasons that have
nothing to do with wear. **Rejected.**

Equally rejected: folding `rate` into the flight clock (a rate-free pad clock).
It would decouple the background slider from the economy, which §2.9 wants, but
it halves or doubles every pad number at any non-default rate, changes what a
shared `&rate=` link re-flies, and re-prices the bots' pad gear. That is a
bench-and-balance pass of its own; this plan keeps `rate` exactly where it is
and writes down what it means.

What the note actually asks for is the third reading: keep the physics and the
balanced pace, call that pace `×1`, and let every duration the player *spends*
be a real one. That changes no number this plan pins, which is why it lands in
the same pass as the contract instead of after it.

## order

1. The constants and helpers in `gravity_sim.js`, plus the `career_bench` checks
   that pin them and the kWh of one wait.
2. The readers: `careerAdvance`, `careerPadGear`, `waitSettlement`,
   `panelWaitKwh`, `panelReadySec`, `rebelMultiple`. Every number identical.
3. The labels: ladders, `paceTag`, the wait button, its line, tooltip, progress
   and notice; `index.html`; `dom_smoke`'s pins.
4. The prose: `orbit_tech`/`orbit_science`/`orbit_econ` units, README, drafts
   `→` lines, and 0.7.2's wear formula.
5. `grep` for `dtYears`, `WORLD_SEC_PER_MIN`, `CAREER_WAIT_MIN`, `PLAY_CAREER`,
   `* 60`, `/ 3600`: every remaining hit is the contract itself, a label, a
   trade-clock minute or a commented constant.
6. Only then start 0.7.2: `WEAR_SPACE` is condition per **flight** second.

## scope boundary

No wear, no repair prices, no fleet. No change to `rate`, `pace`, panel power,
the wait's world seconds, the corruption grace or any politics rate; no table
value moves. No new HUD clock and no new panel: the three clocks meet in one
tooltip. The orbital-trade animation remains a background display clock in its
own settings panel, as §2.9 asks, and the URL params keep their raw values so
every shared link re-flies unchanged.
