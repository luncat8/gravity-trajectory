# 0.4.27 plan — seeded-field bot balancing

## Goal

Make the `easy`, `normal` and `hard` rivals show a reliable skill progression in
seeded gravity without making the main-thread search stutter or changing replay
results. Keep the uniform-field ladder as a separate control.

The exploratory seeded-field money comparison in the 0.4.26 worklog found
`hard > easy` on only 2/4 seeds, while `normal > easy` held on 4/4. Treat the
seeded-field balance as unresolved; do not use the uniform-field result as its
proxy.

## Investigation

1. **Reproduce a comparable ladder.** Run all bot levels on identical seeded
   fields, seeds, market state, starting positions, rival count and money-game
   rules. Record per seed: winner, final purse/score, shots, visited systems and
   search work. Keep the existing uniform-field comparison as its own control.
   Retain the original four-seed set for direct comparison, then add a larger
   deterministic sample before accepting a balance change.
2. **Measure cost before changing the hard budget.** For cold and warm decisions,
   collect startup/first-result latency, candidates examined, total search time,
   and p50/p95/worst frame-slice duration. Repeat with multiple rivals and
   realistic frame cadences so the total available search time and its split
   across active jobs are visible. Separate startup/setup cost from candidate
   tracing and lookahead work.
3. **Compare budget strategies.** The initial candidate is to give `hard` more
   search time, but choose the mechanism only after the measurements: more total
   candidates, deeper lookahead, a larger share of the shared frame budget, or
   another targeted allocation may behave differently. Preserve bounded,
   incremental work and prevent one hard rival from starving the other jobs.
4. **Recheck behavior and determinism.** Compare the per-seed outcome table before
   and after each candidate change. Re-run synchronous-versus-sliced decisions,
   cross-cadence and skip-to-end replays, and the uniform control.

## Acceptance

- On the existing four seeded money fields, hard beats easy on at least 3/4
  seeds; report the per-seed table rather than only the aggregate. The expanded
  deterministic sample must support the same direction, with no single-seed
  regression hidden by an average.
- Normal remains a meaningful step above easy on the same sample; report its
  record and final outcome metrics independently of hard.
- Existing uniform-field bot thresholds still pass unchanged.
- Search remains sliced and responsive: no measured production slice exceeds
  the 10 ms budget, and additional active rivals do not cause starvation or
  unbounded completion time.
- Sliced and synchronous choices agree where they are expected to, and replay
  outcomes remain identical across frame cadences and skip.
- Store the seed set, measurement method, per-seed results and chosen budget
  policy in the worklog. If a larger hard budget is not supported by the
  measurements, retain the current budget and tune a better-targeted part of the
  decision policy instead.

## Scope boundary

This plan does not prescribe a hard-bot time increase. Measuring startup overhead
and how time is divided among search jobs comes first; any budget or policy
change must be justified by both the seeded outcome ladder and the frame-slice
measurements.
