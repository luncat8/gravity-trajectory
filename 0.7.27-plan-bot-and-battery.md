# 0.7.27 plan — batteries keep their charge; bots that claim their own ground

Two subjects, one reason: both are prices that lied. A battery arrives full, so a
"buy a battery" rung is secretly a fast-charge and every planner has to model the
instant fill to price it honestly. And a rival's rail, or the Capital's, is priced
as a *new pad on the network*, so a bot's fan prefers a landing that can never be
claimed over the unclaimed planet next door. Each fix removes the discount rather
than adding a counterweight.

Evidence: `archive/0.7.26-replay-review-report.md` (seed `93293`, 120 473 steps,
three companies) — across 23 bot flights neither rival ever landed on an unclaimed
planet, and 21 of those landings targeted a rival pad. Baseline replay of the same
map with all three seats on the bot: `archive/replays/career-3bots-baseline.txt`.

## 1 · a battery upgrade keeps the charge it found

`GravityGame.buildGear` and `GravityGame.bulkUpgrade` write
`pad.chargeKwh = item.capacity` when the bought kind is the battery. That makes a
battery a purchase of *energy*, not of storage: 1.5 kWh becomes 3.0 kWh of charge
for the price of the tank, which is a fast-charge nobody would print in the shop.

New law: a pad battery is storage. Buying or upgrading one keeps the charge that
is in the pad, clamped to the new capacity:

	pad.chargeKwh = Number.isFinite(pad.chargeKwh)
		? Math.min(pad.chargeKwh, item.capacity) : item.capacity;

A **fresh** pad is the one exception and stays as it is: `fitPad` charges a first
rail (the HQ, a claimed planet, a cheat) because there is no earlier charge to
keep — a claim that arrived empty would be a pad the ship cannot leave, and
`career_bench` asserts the starter grant flies.

Consequences that must be paid where they are spent:

- `careerPadGear` no longer credits the instant fill. A battery offer was
  `(money + reach gain) / (flown + refill above the *new* capacity)`, so a battery
  cleared the recharge wait of its own capacity. With preserved charge the wait is
  the one the pad already had: the battery sells reach (and later cycles), nothing
  else. The rail+mate offer prices the shot it buys against the charge the pad
  actually holds, so a pair is never sold on a fill it does not get.
- `careerDeparture` prices an exit on the charge the pad holds, not on its
  capacity. A battery bought in the build loop is clamped to that charge, so the
  loop can no longer buy its way out of a drained pad — a recharge, a stronger
  rail or an evacuation are the honest ways out.
- `careerScoreTail` charges recharge time for the energy the panel has to replace,
  i.e. the shot less the charge already in the pad.

## 2 · only buildable ground is a new pad; only own charge is an exit

`careerScoreFacts` gave `CAREER_NETWORK_PAD_VALUE (4.0) + careerSiteValue` to any
landing where `own.catapult === null`, which includes a rival's rail and the
Capital. A rival pad then scored `4.0 + site − tax` against an unclaimed planet's
`4.0 + site − EMC-1 (3.04)`, a phantom `+2.4…+3.0` in favour of a landing that
cannot be claimed, and it beat the company's own pads (`2.5`).

New table, by what the landing can become:

| landing | pad value |
|---|---|
| back on the pad it left | `CAREER_SAME_PAD_VALUE (-1)` |
| own rail | `CAREER_BUILT_PAD_VALUE (2.5) + 0.1 × (tier − 1)` |
| own starter rail, buildable | as above `+ careerSiteValue` (the site it could grow into) |
| unclaimed, buildable, starter rail can leave and is affordable | `CAREER_NETWORK_PAD_VALUE (4.0) + careerSiteValue` |
| unclaimed, buildable, but the proven exit is a paid rail (`departure.builds`) | `CAREER_STEEP_PAD_VALUE (2.0) + careerSiteValue` |
| rival / rebel rail | `CAREER_FOREIGN_PAD_VALUE (1.0)` — a landing, a usage tax, no claim |
| the Capital | `0` — the `−3` entry fine is left to the visit pay |

`careerSiteValue` returns `−Infinity` when the rail it was asked about reaches no
other system; that is now `−CAREER_DEAD_SITE` on top of the table above, so a steep
well is discounted instead of erased: a planet only an EMC-2 can leave is a real
claim when the purse can buy the EMC-2, and `careerEval.builds` says so.

## 3 · exits are priced on the charge that is there

`careerDeparture` read `pad.battery.capacity`. `careerRailCharge` answers the
honest question instead: your own pad recharges while the ship flies, so a remote
own pad counts its capacity; the pad under the ship, and any rail that is not
yours, count `chargeKwh − reservedKwh`. A drained rival pad is therefore priced as
drained, and `careerNextShot` may sit out a recharge (`careerRechargeSit`, 30 s a
try, at most `CAREER_SIT_TRIES`) instead of paying the `4.54` commercial fallback.

## 4 · claim before service, and a reserve sized to the exit

- `careerRepair` keeps the `EMC-1` price back while the ship stands on a buildable,
  rail-less planet, so an optional repair can never eat the claim.
- `expansionReserve` becomes one cheap commercial shot
  (`CAREER_PURSE_RESERVE`), plus the priced way off the planet the ship is on
  (`0` on its own rail), plus the repair its current wear costs. The old reserve
  (`2.27 + 6.00 evac + 2.00 repair + 0.76 hold ≈ 11`) demanded a `~14` purse for a
  `3.04` claim and made expansion a late-game move on every map.
- `careerNextPad(career, cash)` now prices each candidate with
  `careerDeparture`: a site is a candidate when the *cheapest proven exit* from it
  fits the cash, and the expansion buys that kit (a stronger rail on a steep world)
  rather than always an `EMC-1` that cannot leave.

## 5 · the hold is the board's entry fee, gated on having a board

`HOLD-8` costs `0.76` and `1.5 t`, which is `15 %` of a dry ship: every launch,
fee and tax rises with it. Both rivals bought it on landing 1 and delivered no
contract in the whole reviewed game. `careerWantHold` buys it once the company owns
`CAREER_HOLD_PADS (2)` pads, or when a local delivery on the board already carries
the hold (`tons ≤ 8`, `from` here) and pays more than the hold costs.

## 6 · contracts, patience and deconfliction

- `careerAccept` prices the Capital's flat `−3` entry fine per Capital endpoint, and
  skips a task whose load the rail at `from` cannot lift (`careerLoadLift`: tons
  aboard, `speedLimit` at the pad's full charge, `SPEED_MIN`). After a contract is
  dropped as unreachable, `career.acceptHold` keeps the company off the board for
  `CAREER_ACCEPT_HOLD` — the post-drop board is the one that just failed.
- `careerNoShot` evacuates from any planet with no affordable departure after
  `CAREER_STUCK_TRIES` (not only from a rule-stranded pad), which is the Capital
  deadlock: a free imperial rail nothing can lift and no money to repair.
- `careerScoreFacts` charges `CAREER_CONTESTED_COST (2.0)` when another company is
  already queued on the target body. Both rivals queued the identical commercial
  shot from `P11` and fired on the same step; the second harvested nothing.
- A losing self-bounce is never flown once patience is spent: `careerLaunch` hands
  the turn back to the shopping/no-shot path instead.

## 7 · search breadth

The primary fan now tests every rung of `CAREER_SPEED_FRACTIONS`, `1.0×` included
(it stopped at `0.86×`, so the top of a pad's rail was only reachable through the
fallback fan or the book). The horizon stays `45 s`; a longer primary horizon is
the next measurement, not part of this plan.
