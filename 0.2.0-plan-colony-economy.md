# 0.2.0 — colony economy for the trade backdrop

Standalone spec: a fork can rebuild this from this file alone. It replaces the
3-good tonnage economy in `orbit_sim.js` with a two-tier colony economy — planets
inside the system, averages between stars — on the growth law borrowed from
`galaxy-flythrough`'s colony series (`31c6d5a4b9e`), with its defects fixed.

Prices and credits are **deferred**: the market reports supply and tags
(surplus / fair / scarce) but no money changes hands. Nothing here depends on
money, so adding it later is one formula per good.

## §1 Two tiers

| tier | goods | simulated | who trades |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| inter-planet (inside the system) | 3: `bulk` (raw), `goods` (product), `migrants` | yes — the port ships already fly between stations | one NPC ship per port, automatic |
| inter-star (between systems) | 7: `rare`, `exotic`, `antimatter`, `food`, `fabricators`, `info`, `colonists` | **no movement** — system averages only | one abstract inter-star port for the whole system |

No inter-planet movement is computed while the system is closed. The page *is*
the system view, so the inter-planet tier always runs; the inter-star tier is an
average the system's single port reports (turnover, surplus, scarcity), with
ambient traffic drawn as dashes that carry no state.

Every colony is a planet of this system. A system has at most one inter-star
port (the star's), independent of how many planet ports exist.

## §2 The system and its traits

`placeWorld` gains a deterministic trait pass, seeded from the sim seed:

- `planetType[i]` — `rock, terran, super, dwarf, mini, gas, icegiant` by
  weighted draw, biased by the planet's radius and orbit radius.
- `planetMetal[i]` — 0.2…1.2 scale factor (stored); the system's `metal` is its
  mean, clamped to the authored star range.
- `planetLight[i]` — inverse-square of orbit radius normalised so the innermost
  planet is 1; the system's `lum` is the author's scalar (seed-drawn 0.3…3).
- `planetHabitat[i]` — `0.04…1.0` from type and light: terran 1.0, rock 0.35,
  dwarf 0.12, gas 0.18, icegiant 0.20, mini 0.16, super 0.04, each scaled by how
  close the world's light is to the habitable band (`|log₂(light)| / 3`) and by
  ±15 % luck.
- `beltCount` — counted planets above the field bound are not modelled; the
  system carries a `beltCount` field drawn 0…2 (the trade port's own berths).

The star is drawn at the field centre as the inter-star port, **not** a gravity
body: the cargo experiment's physics is unchanged. Making it gravitating is a
later option, deliberately not taken here.

## §3 Resource map (deterministic, per planet and per system)

All ease values are clamped to `[0, 1]` and jittered by a stable hash of
`(seed, planetOrSystemKey, goodIdx)` by ±0.06. Same seed → same map, every run,
every reload.

Aggregates (system level, over 26 planets):

```
metalRich = clamp01(metal / 0.030)          metal is Z, the mean world × solar
lumNorm   = clamp01(sqrt(lum) / 1.5)        sun 0.67, a 2.25 L star 1, an M dwarf ~0.16
rockyFrac = clamp01((rocky + 2·beltCount) / (planets + 2·beltCount))
giantFrac = clamp01(giants / planets)
beltFrac  = clamp01(beltCount / 2)
terranFrac= clamp01(terranCount / 4)        four terran worlds is "life-rich"
```

Inter-star ease (7 goods). Geology reads the **star**, agriculture reads the
**world** the colony stands on — people farm and multiply where they live:

| id | ease | why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| rare | `0.15 + 0.85·metalRich·(0.35 + 0.65·rockyFrac)` | metal-rich star *and* rock to mine |
| exotic | `0.10 + 0.55·giantFrac + 0.20·beltFrac + 0.15·lumNorm` | hard chemistry needs volatiles and power |
| antimatter | `0.05 + 0.95·lumNorm` | pure energy; capital-squared keeps it late |
| food | `0.05 + 0.60·habitat + 0.20·lumNorm + 0.15·terranFrac` | the world's own habitat, under habitable light |
| fabricators | `0.20 + 0.50·ease.rare + 0.30·lumNorm` | industry is metals plus power |
| info | `0.30` | culture is population, not geography |
| colonists | `0.10 + 0.70·habitat + 0.20·terranFrac` | people come off habitable worlds |

Inter-planet ease (3 goods, per planet `p`):

| id | ease |
| --- | --- |
| bulk | `0.10 + 0.90·planetMetal[p]·(0.4 + 0.6·rockWeight(type))` |
| goods | `0.20 + 0.50·ease.bulk + 0.30·habitat[p]` |
| migrants | `0.10 + 0.80·habitat[p]` |

`rockWeight` is the table in §2's habitat column reshaped so rock is highest and
gas lowest.

Founding score (sizes a new colony):
`score = clamp01(0.35·food + 0.15·rare + 0.10·fabricators + 0.25·migrants + 0.15·bulk)`
— half the system's star terms, half the site's own world. Measured means over
26 planets: terran 0.55–0.62, rock 0.49–0.51, mini/super/dwarf 0.37–0.40, gas and
ice 0.30–0.35. A barren rock in a rich system is a mining camp; a terran world in
a poor one is a farm; a gas giant is a bad idea.

## §4 Colony state and the growth law

`createColony(planetIdx, name, foundedY)`:

```
pop   = 1 + 9·score            k persons
capital = 1 + 5·score          industrial points
stock[10] = 0                  t (3 inter-planet + 7 inter-star)
lastY = foundedY
stage = outpost
```

Advance is **event-driven**, never per frame: `advanceColony(colony, toY)` runs
the fixed step `dt = 0.25 y` (a span over 512 y coarsens to `span/2048` and sets
`coarse`; the readout says so). Callers: the sim's `TICK` event (every 3 sim
minutes = 3 y, 12 steps) — **all** colonies advance on every tick, so readouts are
never stale. No arrive/inspect dependency.

Per step, for all ten goods in one pass:

1. **Produce** `prod[r] = ease[r]·RATE[r]·driver[r]·dt`

   | good | RATE (t/y at ease 1) | driver |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | rare, exotic, fabricators | 800, 800, 600 | `clamp01(capital/25)` |
   | antimatter | 400 | `clamp01(capital/25)²` |
   | food | 2000 | `clamp01(pop/50)` |
   | info | 600 | `clamp01(pop/100)` |
   | bulk, goods | 1200, 900 | `clamp01(pop/40)` |
   | migrants | `0` | produced by §5, not by a rate |

2. **Consume** `need[r] = pop·NEED[r]·dt`

   | good | NEED (t per 1k persons per y) |
   | --- | --- |
   | food | **20** (dominant — see the regression test) |
   | info | 1.0 |
   | fabricators | 0.5 |
   | rare | 0.2 |
   | exotic | 0.2 |
   | antimatter | 0.05 |
   | bulk | 12 |
   | goods | 6 |
   | migrants, colonists | 0 (they are flows) |

   `take = min(stock + prod, need)`; `stock += prod − take`.

3. **Warehouse cap** — the defect to fix: `stock[r] ≤ 150·need_annual[r] + 50`.
   A colony never accumulates a million tons of food it cannot use; surplus above
   the cap is what "export" means.

4. **Comfort** `= 1 − Σshortfall / Σneed` (1 when there is no need).

5. **Population** — logistic in `K`, famine below 0.4:

   ```
   K   = 40·(0.4 + 0.6·ease.food)·(1 + capital/25)            k persons
   dPop = G_MAX·comfort·(1 − pop/K)·pop·dt                     G_MAX = 1/40 per y
   if comfort < 0.4: dPop −= 0.04·pop·dt                       (4 %/y die)
   pop  = max(0.05, pop + dPop)
   ```

6. **Capital** `dCap = 0.02·comfort·Σmax(0, prod − need)·dt − 0.01·capital·dt`,
   floor 0.1.

7. **Stages** by pop: `outpost < 2 ≤ settlement < 10 ≤ town < 40 ≤ city`.
   A stage change is one event into the log; only `town` and `city` may sponsor
   an expansion (the existing colony ship).

8. **Abandonment** — a colony at the pop floor with `comfort < 0.4` for 20
   consecutive years is removed (planet freed, log line). The honest end of a
   doomed outpost.

Nothing allocates in the step: the ten-good scratch arrays live beside the
colony buffers, allocated once at setup.

## §5 Migrants and colonies

`migrants` is a flow, not a stock: each tick a colony with `comfort ≥ 0.6` and
`pop < 0.8·K` releases `min(pop·0.02·years, ship capacity)` to the port with the
greatest `habitat·(1 − fill)` inside the nearest-four set. Arrival adds the
migrants to the destination's pop (1 t = 1 person, the same bridge the hold
uses). A colony below `0.6` comfort receives none — misery does not attract
people, and famine stays visible in the numbers instead of being papered over by
immigration.

## §6 The market (no money)

Per good, per colony:

```
needAnnual = pop·NEED[r]                       t/y
stockNorm  = clamp01(stock[r] / (2·needAnnual + 50))
supply     = clamp01(0.6·ease[r] + 0.4·stockNorm)
```

Tags: `surplus` at `supply ≥ 0.72`, `scarce` at `supply ≤ 0.42`, else `fair`.
Prices and credits: **deferred**; the tags are the whole readout. `bestExport`
is the highest-surplus good with `stock ≥ 2·needAnnual`; `bestImport` is the
lowest-supply good with a real shortfall. Both are used by the ship dispatch and
the panel, and both are pure functions of the colony record.

The system's inter-star port reports the same three numbers aggregated over all
colonies (Σstock, Σease-weighted supply), plus the ambient turnover: exports per
year above a two-year guard, imports per year to cover shortfalls.

## §7 Ships (inter-planet)

The existing `KIND_LOAD / KIND_ARRIVE` cycle stays, with new decision rules:

- On load, the ship takes the port's `bestExport` (as above), capped at
  `SHIP_CAPACITY = 24 t`, never taking the port below a two-year guard.
- It picks a destination from the port's nearest-four partners by
  `(their shortfall of that good) / (1 + distance/220)`; ties go to the nearer
  port. No player commissioning — the network stays automatic.
- On arrival the tons enter the destination's stock; a migrants run adds people
  instead. Aim is still `solveRendezvous` (closed form, exact).
- The inter-star port has no ships: its turnover is arithmetic, drawn as
  ambient dashes only.

## §8 The cargo experiment seeds colonies

`gravity_gui.js` already reports the impact planet. On impact:

- If the container landed inside a planet's collision radius and that planet has
  no colony, `sim.seedColony(planetIdx)` founds an outpost: founding pop from
  `createColony`, the site's ease map, a log line
  (`outpost founded at planet 7 · pop 3.4k · score 0.42`), and the port ring
  appears that frame.
- On a habitable world the outpost grows; on a barren rock it starves, drops to
  the floor and is abandoned in §4 step 8 — the honest cost of a bad landing,
  and the reason to look at the map before shooting.
- A landing on a planet that already has a colony adds the container's cargo
  (`bulk`/`goods` tons) to that colony's stock, capped by the warehouse. The
  container carries `bulk` 2 t by default; the launch panel gains a good picker
  (bulk / goods / migrants) so the shot has a purpose.
- A miss (impact on nothing, field exit) changes nothing: the current physics
  rules are untouched, and the background clock is still independent of the
  cargo clock.

## §9 Time base

One sim minute = one colony year (`ECON_YEARS_PER_MIN = 1`). The boot
fast-forward to `t+07:00` therefore lands 420 colony years of growth, which is
tens of e-foldings: the system opens developed and keeps moving (at the default
`×0.5` speed, 2 s per colony year). `TICK_MINUTES = 3` → 12 advance steps per
tick, ~3.1 k good-updates for 26 colonies: well under a millisecond, per tick,
not per frame.

## §10 GUI

**HUD (top-left, trade)**: ports, ships, delivered, colonies by stage
(`3o · 5s · 2t · 1c`), mean comfort, inter-star turnover (exports/imports per
year). The cargo HUD is unchanged.

**Port hover** (existing card): colony name and stage, pop, comfort %, the worst
shortfall (`food −38 %`), best export, current ship state.

**Panel** (right column, collapsible, its own file): system market rows for the
7 inter-star goods (`ease bar · stock · supply % · tag`), the colony list
(planet, stage, pop, comfort; click selects the planet), the inter-star turnover
line, and an event log ring buffer of 40 lines (founding, stage, famine,
recovery, abandonment, expansion, cargo landing) at event cadence — 10 Hz at
most, never per frame.

**Sky overlay**: colony markers with stage colour and size (outpost small and
dim → city large and bright), the existing growth ring now reading
`comfort·(1 − pop/K)` (a ring that stalls is a colony that is unhappy), route
lines for the nearest-four set (already drawn), and the inter-star port at the
field centre with in/out ambient dashes. All positions come from the same camera
the cargo overlay uses.

## §12 Tests

- **Ease determinism**: same seed → identical maps for every planet and the
  system; different seeds diverge; jitter stays inside ±0.06 and inside `[0,1]`.
- **Needs order** (the regression): `NEED[food]` is the largest; a colony whose
  food ease is 0 starves while one with food ease 1 grows — the transposed table
  that made *rare elements* the famine good must fail this test.
- **Growth**: logistic toward `K`, never above it; famine at `comfort < 0.4`;
  capital grows only from surplus and decays at 1 %/y; stock obeys the
  warehouse cap.
- **Determinism of chunking**: one 100 y advance equals 400 × 0.25 y advances
  when not coarse; a 2 000 y advance flags `coarse`.
- **Events**: stage crossings ascend and never repeat; famine enters and lifts in
  order; a route re-arm is never lost (no capped queue: events ride the heap).
- **Trade**: a route load never takes the port below its two-year guard; the
  destination chosen is the biggest shortfall per distance; a migrants run moves
  people and not tons.
- **Seeding**: a cargo landing on a free planet founds an outpost with the site's
  score; on a barren planet it is abandoned within 200 y; on an existing colony
  it adds tons up to the cap.
- **Cost** (`colony_bench.js`): µs per colony-year and per tick at 26 colonies,
  zero allocations in the advance (typed scratch reused), no NaN over 5 000 y.
- Existing `gravity_bench.js` must keep passing untouched: the physics is not
  part of this plan.
