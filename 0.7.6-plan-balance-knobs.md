# 0.7.6 plan — balance knobs, separate from the clock

Open. The economy uses **game seconds**; playback changes watching speed.
The orbit-rate control is different: moving ports are gravity sources, so its
shared orbit/gravity clock is intentional and disclosed in the background
panel. What the player can still want is to move the *balance* — how fast a pad
charges, how fast rot grows, how fast a visit pays — without lying about time.
Those are different knobs and need their own plan.

## why not the old slider

The background `rate` slider used to be exactly such a knob by accident: pads
and the political clock ran on `steps · DT · rate`, so ×12 charged a pad twelve
times faster and rotted a world twelve times faster than ×0.5. It was labelled
as an animation control, sat in the background panel, and silently re-priced the
economy — the worst of both. 0.7.1 pinned the pads to `SUN_SEC_PER_GAME_SEC` and
the politics to `gameSeconds`, but the slider still controls moving gravity and can affect trajectories.

## what a balance knob has to be

A knob that moves the balance must be:

1. **named as balance**, in the same panel as the other balance experiments
   (`experimental`, next to `g field` and `catapult ×`), never as a clock;
2. **declared in the label with its own unit** — `panels ×`, `rot ×`, `pay ×` —
   never in seconds;
3. **measured against the bench**: pin the default law with a unit test and
   compare both ends against the page-seated `career_gravity_bench`, on paired
   seeds, so a knob cannot silently become the new balance;
4. **one number**, applied where the law lives, not where the label reads.

## candidates, in the order they are worth having

| knob | where it multiplies | what it changes | risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| `pads ×0…×4` | `careerAdvance`'s `padSeconds` | charge and leak together, so a pad's *shape* holds | a ×4 pad nearly deletes the battery tier as a cost; the bot's `careerPadGear` yield moves with it, so it must read the same factor |
| `rot ×0…×3` | `advancePolitics`'s `seconds` | passive/report rot rates and the rebellion epoch together | the epoch is an int; a ×3 rot must scale `REBEL_CHECK_SECONDS` too or the roll rate changes |
| `pay ×0…×2` | `planetProfitAtStep`'s age | how fast a visit's payout regrows | the pill prices a *rate*, so the HUD's `+0.00625/s` text is derived and must follow |
| `wear ×0…×3` | 0.7.2's `WEAR_SPACE` | ship ageing only | forecasts must account for report failure and the repair needed at 0 |

The first three share one shape: each is already a single age multiply inside
one function, so each knob is one factor at that call site plus one row in the
`experimental` panel. Each can ship independently with its own parity tests and
paired benchmark; re-price any active forecast when its value changes.

## what must not become a knob

- **The pace** is the player's watching speed, not a balance lever, and it
  already exists as `play ×N`; a balance knob that also sped the clock would
  reintroduce exactly the two-in-one that 0.7.1 removed.
- **Do not separate moving ports from their gravity.** The ports drawn in the
  sky are the bodies pulling the ship. Keep one orbit/gravity clock and disclose
  its effect. A cosmetic-only speed control is appropriate only for visual
  effects that are not gravity sources; it must not make the drawn ports lie
  about the field.
- **The wait's span** is 60 game seconds. A shorter or longer wait is a
  different feature (`0.7.5`'s evacuation trip already prices real time).

## political baseline (resolved)

Passive corruption stays `0` while uncollected visit profit is `≤ 1.00`
(`CORRUPTION_PROFIT_START`), then ramps linearly to `CORRUPTION_PASSIVE_RATE`
(`0.1/s`) at `3.00` (`CORRUPTION_PROFIT_FULL`), matching the planet card's
profit-pill colour stages. `REBEL_CHECK_SECONDS` (`60 s`), `FLYBY_RAMP_SECONDS`
(`240 s`), `CORRUPTION_REPORT_RATE` (`0.02/s`) and `ARREST_SECONDS` (`120 s`)
keep their game-second values as the baseline `rot ×1` is measured against.

## sharing

Use an optional URL parameter per knob (`&pads=2`), validated within its range
and omitted at the default `×1`, as with `&gs=` and `&fs=`. Copy the current
setting. A career link starts a new career, rather than replaying the history of
mid-run knob edits, so it cannot reproduce that history on its own.
