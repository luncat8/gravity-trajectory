# 0.4.0 — career mode with EM-catapult colonies, tech tree and science

Standalone spec: a fresh checkout can rebuild this from this file plus the
existing `0.2.0-plan-colony-economy.md` and current `gravity_*` / `orbit_*`
sources. Career is a new **game rule** (`career`) layered on the seeded world
and the colony economy — it reuses planet placement, orbits, traits, and the
growth law, but replaces “one flight at a time” with a persistent HQ, per-planet
catapults, cargo mass, and a knowledge-volume science system.

No change to `tour` / `route` / `money` / `lab` except the shared helpers
already added (route helper buttons, URL params, deselect).

---

## §1 Career loop

Player starts on one random planet with weak infrastructure:

- **HQ planet** (or “home”): a low-force electromagnetic catapult (EMC),
  a small solar panel, a small battery.
- **Ship**: minimal dry mass, **no cargo hold** (0 t). It can still do
  visit-observations and `route`-style sport competitions, but cannot accept
  delivery contracts.
- **Purse**: 3.00 (same as `money` start) plus a small founding grant.
- **Catapult on HQ only**: all other planets have no catapult — you can land
  there, but you cannot launch again until you pay to build one.

One career run is many flights (potentially dozens) over the same seeded
system. The world clock (`tradeSim.t`, colony `advanceColony`) advances as in
`0.2.0` (1 min = 1 colony year, tick every 3 min). Port ships, colony growth,
and rival flights all proceed between player launches.

Goal: accumulate wealth, unlock tech, and keep a network of catapults that
lets you take richer delivery chains. There is no single “win” — the
scoreboard shows `purse + network size + tech tier`; a seasonal leaderboard
ranks the same seed.

## §2 Tasks (money generation)

Tasks are generated against the shared market and refreshed every game tick.
Three families, matching the design brief:

### 2.1 Visit (fly near) — always available, low pay

- Condition: *enter* a planet’s port-orbit ellipse (≈ existing `markSystemsAt`
  / `predVisit`) without landing. The planet must have been **unvisited for
  long enough** — its `market.lastVisitStep` is old, so its `planetProfit`
  has regrown (same table as `MONEY.k`, but career re-tunes `k` and cap).
- Payout: `visitPay = baseVisit + kVisit * ageYears` capped at `0.75`.
  Early game the base is low (0.10), so chasing fresh planets is not worth it;
  old planets are.
- Visual: same profit pill (`+0.25` → `+0.75`) already drawn under each planet
  in game mode; career can keep it or restyle as “observation value”.
- Limit: one payout per planet per entry; repeated passes without enough
  time do not pay.

### 2.2 Delivery — frequent, needs cargo hold

- A customer on planet A wants `tons` of `good` (one of the 10 econ goods:
  `bulk`, `goods`, `rare`, `exotic`, …) on planet B within a deadline.
- **Cargo hold** required: the ship must have a hold whose `capacity >= tons`.
  Tons add **mass**: `shipMass = dryMass + Σ partMass + cargoMass`.
  More mass → higher required launch energy and fuel if an impulse engine is
  used (see §4).
- Payout: `deliveryPay = base * (1 + distanceFactor) * (1 + scarcityFactor)
  - fuelCost`. Distance is great-circle over field or number of gravity assists
  (proxy for difficulty). Scarcity reads the origin’s `supply` / destination’s
  `shortfall` (same tags as `orbit_econ.js` §6).
- Contract board: 5–7 offers visible, refreshed every 30 s of game time;
  expired/infeasible ones grey out.

### 2.3 Route competition — sometimes, sport

- Same as current `route` rule: one planned trajectory, longest wins, no
  launch cost.
- Frequency: ~1 per game hour, announced as “championship on <planet>”.
  Participation costs a small entry fee; top 3 split a purse.
- This lets a lightweight, no-cargo ship earn without mass penalties.

### 2.4 Returning to HQ

- Only HQ can **cash out** (convert purse ledger to permanent bank).
  Remote planets hold “field purse” until you return — this is why you need
  catapults.
- Before you can afford a new catapult, you must plan a **return trajectory**
  from the delivery target back to HQ. A delivery that cannot return is
  unprofitable — the loop is: `HQ → origin → destination → HQ` with proper
  gravity assists, not just one hop.
- Catapult build cost is paid from bank at HQ; the new pad appears that tick
  (panel feedback, log line) and thereafter unlocks launches from that planet.

## §3 Catapults, power, and logistics

### 3.1 EM-catapult

- Stats per level: `maxForce (kN)`, `efficiency (0..1)`, `buildCost`,
  `mass`, `chargeTime (s)`, `energyPerShot (kWh)`.
- Start catapult: `maxForce = 45 kN`, `efficiency = 0.62`, `energyPerShot ~ 1.2 kWh`
  at dry mass. It can barely lift a light ship; any cargo makes it stall.
- Upgrades (see §4) raise `maxForce` to ≈ 180 kN and efficiency to 0.88.
- Launch constraint: the aim must satisfy `requiredEnergy <= battery + solar`.
  If not, the launch button is disabled with tooltip “not enough charge — wait
  for sun or upgrade battery/panel”. The required energy is shown live
  as you drag (same place as `+0.25` profit preview).

### 3.2 Solar panel + battery on every pad

- **Panel**: generates `P = panelPower * planetLight * (1 + starLumBonus)` watts
  (W) continuously while `tradeSim.t` advances. `planetLight` is already
  1/(mean orbit²) normalized, star `lum` from `orbit_world.js`.
- **Battery**: stores `capacity (kWh)`, has `mass`, `buildCost`, `leak (kWh/y)`.
  Charge level is per-planet, persists between visits, and is drawn as a small
  vertical bar beside the planet or in the colony panel.
- Charging is what makes the clock matter: staying in sunlight between shots
  literally fills the bar; night side or dim outer worlds charge slowly unless
  you upgrade panel.

### 3.3 Mass and staging

- Ship mass is the sum of installed parts (see §4) + cargo. All parts have a
  mass that affects:
  - required catapult energy (`E ∝ m * v² / efficiency`)
  - impulse engine fuel burn (`Δv` via rocket equation, even if simplified)
- Parts can be **installed/uninstalled** at HQ (or at any pad with a workshop
  — cheaper to keep it HQ-only for 0.4.0). Swapping takes game time but not
  money; selling returns partial cost.

## §4 Tech tree — what is sold

Initial grant: one HQ pad with `catapult-1`, `panel-1`, `battery-1`. Ship has
no hold. The player can still do 2.1 and 2.3, but not 2.2.

| group | examples | primary stats per level | mass? | build cost | gameplay effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Catapult | `EMC-1` → `EMC-5` | `maxForce`, `efficiency` | pad mass, not ship | high (3–20 purse) | stronger launches, heavier ships allowed, less energy per shot |
| Solar panel | `SP-1` → `SP-4` | `power (W)` | 0 (pad) | medium | faster charging, viable on dim worlds |
| Battery | `BAT-1` → `BAT-5` | `capacity (kWh)`, `leak` | light pad mass, but ship battery variant has mass | medium | buffer one or two heavy shots without waiting |
| Auto-helpers | tier 0 → 2 | `helperBudget` (candidates, horizon) | 0 | 1–4 | unlocks the 3 helper buttons at higher quality (more candidates, longer horizon preview) |
| Impulse engine | none → `IMP-1` → `IMP-3` | `thrust (kN)`, `Isp (s)` | medium (ship) | high | **pause-at-any-time impulse**: during flight, hit `space`/`p` to pause, drag a Δv vector with same grip physics as catapult, burn fuel, resume. Same aim snap/grid. |
| Fuel tank | `FT-0` → `FT-3` | `fuelCap (kg)` | light + fuel mass | medium | more Δv budget per flight |
| Cargo hold | `0` → `8 t` → `24 t` | `capacity (t)` | empty mass + cargo | medium | unlocks delivery; bigger holds take more mass, need stronger catapult |

Balance note: **cargo capacity directly trades against catapult/impulse
requirement**. A 24 t hold full of ore is not lifted by `EMC-1` at all — the
UI must show this as red (“too heavy: need EMC-3 or less cargo”).

Auto-helpers start locked; buying them unlocks the same helper logic already
shipped for `route` (broad `quick scan`, focused `scan here`, `tweak best`).
In career, `quick scan` also proposes **return legs** to HQ when evaluating
delivery profitability.

## §5 How science works — knowledge volume

Beyond the shop list, truly new performance is **invented**, not bought.

### 5.1 The idea

Each tech family (engine, catapult, panel, battery, hold, tank) has an
N-dimensional **parameter space**:

- `engine`: `force`, `mass` (2D) — optionally `Isp` (3D) after IMP-2.
- `catapult`: `force`, `efficiency` (2D)
- `panel`: `power`, `mass` (2D)
- `battery`: `capacity`, `mass`, `leak` (3D)
- `hold`: `capacity`, `emptyMass` (2D)

Discovering a concrete item (e.g. “engine `force 1, mass 10`” and later
“`force 2, mass 15`”) defines points `P1, P2, ...` in that space. Inside the
**convex hull** (or its N-D volume) of those points, any interpolated product
is **cheap** — it is just a recombination of known engineering tradeoffs.
Outside the hull, you pay **research cost** proportional to distance to the hull.

Example from the brief, generalized:

```
P1 = engine(force=1, mass=10)  — discovered
P2 = engine(force=2, mass=15)  — discovered
→ hull is the line segment P1-P2. Any engine whose (force,mass) projects onto
  that segment costs base price.

P3 = engine(force=1, mass=1)   — discovered next
→ hull becomes triangle P1-P2-P3. Any point inside the triangle is cheap.
  A desired engine (force=1.5, mass=12) lies inside → cheap.
  A desired engine (force=3, mass=1) lies far outside → pay k * dist.
```

In 3+ dimensions the hull is a convex polyhedron; in 1D it is an interval.
The hull is stored incrementally as discovery adds vertices.

### 5.2 UI

- A **param slider** (Floating-point, per dimension) lets the player dial a
  desired tuple, e.g. `engine { force: 1.4 → 2.6, mass: 12 → 4 }`.
  As they drag, a cost preview updates live:
  `cost = base + outsidePenalty(dist) + complexityPenalty(volume)`.
  `outsidePenalty` is roughly `k * EuclideanDist(point, hull) * tierScale`.
- **Accept** mints the item: it is added as a new vertex, expanding the hull;
  the item becomes available to install.
- The panel shows the hull projection in 2D slices when `N>2` (e.g. mass vs
  force), with discovered points and current hull outline, so the player
  understands why “mass 1, force 1” is expensive after only heavy engines are
  known.

### 5.3 Why this is balanced

- Early game you can only interpolate between cheap, heavy, weak parts.
- To get a light **and** powerful engine you must do two separate research
  projects that together expand the hull toward that corner — exactly the
  “triangle” insight.
- The shop still sells canned upgrades for players who don’t want to use the
  slider; the slider is for min-maxing and is strictly optional in 0.4.0.

## §6 Balance tooling (LLM feedback loop)

Because mass/cost/power curves are hard to tune by hand:

- Next to every tech item in the shop / research panel, a **copy button** writes
  to the clipboard a single line like:

  ```
  tech: engine IMP-2 { force: 2.0 kN, mass: 8.0 t, Isp: 280 s, cost: 4.20 }
  ```

  or

  ```
  tech: catapult EMC-2 { force: 90 kN, eff: 0.71, energy: 2.1 kWh, build: 6.00 }
  ```

- The player can paste that line into an LLM chat and say “this item needs
  price up / mass down / force up”. The next build adjusts the table in
  `orbit_econ.js` / new `orbit_tech.js` and the balance bench re-runs.
- A `balance_bench.js` (node, deterministic) runs a few career scripts:
  “tour `seed=42` with `EMC-1` only, how many deliveries are possible?”
  and asserts thresholds (e.g. at least 1, at most 3).

## §7 Cheat test modes

Two toggles on the mode row (visible in `career`, hidden elsewhere):

- **Communism mode** (“all free”): every shop item costs `0`, research costs
  `0` but hull still expands. Used to test late-game networks without grinding.
- **Endgame mode** (“all upgrades installed”): on start, the ship is fitted
  with `EMC-5`, `SP-4`, `BAT-5`, `FT-3`, `HOLD-24`, `IMP-3`, all helpers unlocked,
  purse set to 99. Used to test that the heaviest delivery is still launchable
  and that helpers handle the longest route.

Both modes add `&cheat=communism|endgame` to the replay link so a bug report
cannot be confused with a legit score.

## §9 Bot updates

- Career bots must also build catapults: a bot that cannot afford a pad on a
  rich planet will instead do return-leg planning and visits, just like the
  player. A simple greedy “best ROI pad” heuristic is enough for 0.4.0.
- In `route` competitions, bots already compete; in career, the same helper
  search (broad → focused → tweak) is used to pick a competition entry.
- `GravityBot.prepareStep` gains a `careerMarket` view: depleted planets are
  less attractive, as they already are.

## §11 Tests / validation

- **Hull**: 2 points → line hull, point on line costs base; point off line costs `base + k*dist`. 3 points → triangle, interior cheap, exterior costs. 1D interval analogous.
- **Mass gating**: `dryMass + holdEmpty(24t) + cargo 24t` requires `energy > EMC-1` → `canLaunchFrom` false, helper does not propose it.
- **Delivery**: a contract requiring 20 t cannot be accepted without a 20+ hold.
- **Power**: `BAT-1` on a dim outer world cannot launch twice back-to-back without waiting — bench advances `t` and asserts charge rises.
- **Cheats**: communism reduces all costs to 0; endgame installs all and passes mass gating.
- **Balance copy**: clicking copy puts `tech: ...` on clipboard (stub DOM asserts `clipboardText`).
