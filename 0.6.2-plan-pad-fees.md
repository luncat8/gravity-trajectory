# 0.6.2 plan — what a pad earns: the guest fee, the commercial fallback and a rail worth upgrading

Standalone spec: a fork can rebuild this from this file alone. Source notes:
`drafts/career-economy.md` §2.1 (the delivery gate), §2.2 (the usage cost and the
deferred tax slider), §2.6 (the pad ladder has no demand). Depends on the pad
model in `orbit_tech.js` and the fee path in `gravity_game.js`.

## problem

Three complaints in the drafts, one subject — money a *pad* earns or costs.

1. **The guest fee is a hand-placed rate, and it falls as the host's rail gets
   better.** A guest pays `CAREER.taxPerKwh = 1.00` for each kWh the shot burns
   (`launchFee` → `OrbitTech.launchEnergy(mass, rail, speed)`), so the fee
   already depends on the shot, as the note asks: the dry 10 t ship pays `0.11`
   at the slowest shot (30 px/s) and `1.02` at the `EMC-1` cap. But the energy
   is divided by the rail's efficiency, so the same shot costs a guest `0.109`
   on `EMC-1`, `0.095` on `EMC-2` and `0.084` on `EMC-3`: upgrading a pad lowers
   what it earns per guest launch.
2. **The commercial rail is a flat `3.00`, whatever it lifts.** It is quoted in
   five places — `launchFee`, `exitCostAt`, the bot's `CAREER_PURSE_RESERVE` and
   its departure pricing, the pad block, the career help sentence. The note's
   core loop is «delivery has no profit without build catapult». Against `3.00`
   that holds for a `HOLD-8` load (a delivery pays `0.74…2.42`) and fails for a
   `HOLD-24` one (up to the `4.50` cap); and an `EMC-1` costs `3.04`, one
   commercial launch, so a pad is repaid on its second departure
   (`drafts/career-economy.md` §2.1).
3. **Nothing tells the player where `EMC-3+` is needed.** The demand is in the
   tables: an `EMC-2 + BAT-2` lifts the dry ship off 96 % of `g 3.5` planets,
   but a loaded `HOLD-24` (38 t) off 43 % at `g 2.0` and 13 % at `g 2.5`, where
   an `EMC-3 + BAT-3` lifts it off 100 % and 87 %
   (`RAIL=2 MASS=38 SEEDS=12 node experiments/hq_gravity_probe.js`). A delivery
   is loaded at its origin, so heavy freight out of a deep well is the job that
   wants the big rail — and the board offers it like any other.

The draft's own answer to (1) is a player-set tax, which it defers; (3) wants
the job that only a bigger rail can fly to be visible and paid. All three are
paid at the same moment — the launch click — so they are one plan.

## 1. the guest fee, re-derived

Price the energy by the host pad's own tier — a stronger rail is a better
service and earns more:

	tax(career, planet, speed) = launchEnergy(mass, rail, speed) × CAREER.taxPerKwh × rail.tier

- One multiply in `launchFee` and the same one in `exitCostAt`. `launchEnergy`
  takes no rail-force multiplier, so the `catapult` slider (`career.forceScale`)
  does not enter the fee.
- The rate stays `1.00`: an `EMC-1` host — the common case — charges exactly
  what it does today, to the cent. The slowest dry shot then costs a guest
  `0.11 / 0.19 / 0.25` on `EMC-1 / 2 / 3`. `career_bench` prints guest fees paid
  and earned per run before and after.
- A rival paying a guest fee reads the same function, so the bot's rail order
  (own → rival → commercial) keeps its shape and only its prices change. The
  five quoting sites become one `commercialFee(career, planet, speed)` plus the
  existing `price()` cheat wrapper; `CAREER_PURSE_RESERVE` becomes the *cheapest*
  commercial shot (`speed = SPEED_MIN` on the starter pad), so the reserve rule
  keeps meaning "one way off any planet, priced honestly".

## 2. the commercial rail as a *fallback*, not a floor

The note's gate is "no profit without a rail". Make the commercial fee **scale
with what it saves you**, and make it finite:

- `commercialFee(career, planet, speed) = COMMERCIAL_BASE + COMMERCIAL_KWH × energy`
  with `COMMERCIAL_BASE = 2.00` and `COMMERCIAL_KWH = 1.50`. The commercial pad
  is an `EMC-1 + BAT-1`, whose force limit caps any shot near `1.02 kWh`
  whatever the mass, so the fee runs from `2.16` (the slowest dry shot) to
  `3.52` (full force): a light hop gets cheaper, a loaded one dearer.
- `COMMERCIAL_KWH` is the knob the A/B turns. The starting value does not put
  the fee above a `HOLD-24` delivery's `4.50`, so the bench either raises it or
  records that the note's gate is a `HOLD-8` rule.
- Keep the *shape*: a commercial rail never claims the planet, never blocks a
  build, is always charged, and is always offered. The change is price, not
  availability, so nothing about `careerCanFire`/`railBlock` structure moves.
- `evacuate 6.00` stays the emergency and is now chosen by the bench rather than
  inherited: a delivery that cannot fund a pad must still lose money, and
  `career_bench`'s `broke` share is the check.

## 3. a reason to own `EMC-3`

`archive/0.6.3-plan-fragile-load.md` (built) is the pressure *down* (a load
that only a gentle launch carries). The pressure *up* is a **deep-origin contract**: a delivery of
`DEEP_TONS` or more whose **origin** has `gravity ≥ DEEP_G` is offered with the
note `needs EMC-3+ at P#` and pays `×(1 + DEEP_BONUS)` (`0.6`) on top of the
ordinary reward. It is one branch in `makeDelivery` and one field on the task,
priced by the same `careerLandingFacts` table — no new leg kind.

`DEEP_G = 2.2` and `DEEP_TONS = 16` start from the table in the problem: between
`g 2.0` and `g 2.5` an `EMC-2 + BAT-2` stops lifting a loaded `HOLD-24` and an
`EMC-3 + BAT-3` still does. The probe pins the boundary; it is a fact of the
tables, not a guess.

## GUI

- `pad` plate already prints the rail and the fee; it gains the **two prices** a
  guest sees (`fee 3.00 · own 0.00`) only while the ship is a guest — the
  existing `railOwnerText` line already names the owner, so this is one more
  clause.
- The career `upgrades` tab prints the tax a rival's pad would charge before a
  shot is fired, beside the existing `EMC` rows, so the bot's rail order is
  legible to a human too.
- A deep-origin delivery row carries a `deep` tag and the `+bonus` in words, not
  a new colour.

## Validation

- `career_bench`, `career_bot_bench`, `career_gravity_bench`: no new failure
  name; purse per horizon printed before/after in one log
  (`experiments/logs/0.6.2-pad-fees-ab.txt`).
- A new check in `career_departure_test`: the *same* shot from the same planet
  costs more from `EMC-3` than from `EMC-1` for a guest, and the own-rail cost is
  unchanged at `0.00`.
- A check for the deep-origin offer: it is tagged only when the origin is above
  `DEEP_G` and the load at least `DEEP_TONS`, and the note names the rail that
  lifts it (one helper, so the board and the bot agree).

## Scope boundary

No change to what a pad's battery holds, to the solar charge, or to the
`wait · 30 min` easing. No player-set tax yet — the note's *deferred* slider stays
deferred; this plan only makes the shipped fee derived, so a slider would then
have a number to slide. No new quest kind.
