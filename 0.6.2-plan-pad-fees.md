# 0.6.2 plan — what a pad earns: the guest fee, the commercial fallback and a rail worth upgrading

Standalone spec: a fork can rebuild this from this file alone. Source notes:
`drafts/career-economy.md` §2.1 (the delivery gate), §2.2 (the usage cost and the
deferred tax slider), §2.6 (the pad ladder has no demand). Depends on the pad
model in `orbit_tech.js` and the fee path in `gravity_game.js`.

## problem

Three complaints in the drafts, one subject — money a *pad* earns or costs.

1. **The guest fee is a round number with no derivation.** `CAREER.taxPerKwh = 1`
   was written when `EMC-1 + BAT-1` lifted a 10 t ship to ~92 px/s; the pad
   ladder has since been re-cut and every build cost is now derived from
   research work, so the one flat `1.00 / kWh` is the last hand-placed price in
   the career. It is also the **only** placeholder money on the launch path: the
   quoting sites — `launchFee`, `exitCostAt`, `CAREER_PURSE_RESERVE` and the
   bot's own copy, the pad block and the career help sentence — would otherwise
   all have to be re-typed when it moves, which is a second reason to route them
   through one function now.
2. **The commercial rail hides the draft's own gate.** The note's core loop is
   "a delivery has no profit without building a catapult" — but a commercial rail
   fires from every planet for `3.00` forever, so landing anywhere is never a
   dead end and the `6.00` evacuation is a curiosity. The gate the draft wanted
   is measured off (`drafts/career-economy.md` §2.1 has the figures).
3. **`EMC-3+` has no buyer.** Tiers 3…5 are research-locked, but nothing in the
   game *needs* more than `EMC-2`: no planet is deep enough, no cargo heavy
   enough, and no contract refuses a weak rail.

The draft's own answer to (1) is a player-set tax; its answer to (3) is a quest
that only a bigger rail can fly. Both belong in one plan because all three are
paid at the same moment — the launch click.

## 1. the guest fee, re-derived

Replace the flat `taxPerKwh` with the *energy* the shot costs, priced by the
host pad's own tier — a stronger rail is a better service and costs more to run:

	tax(career, planet, speed) = energy(planet, speed) × TAX_RATE × catapult.tier

- `energy` already exists (`launchEnergy`), so this is one multiply in
  `launchFee`, and `EXPERIMENT.forceScale` flows through it unchanged.
- `TAX_RATE` is a constant *chosen so a starter shot on `EMC-2` pays what
  `1.00 / kWh` pays today* — the plan does not move the balance, it moves the
  derivation. `career_bench` prints the fee before and after; the acceptance is
  that the per-launch fee moves by less than `0.05` on the six seeds it already
  runs.
- A rival paying a guest fee reads the same function, so the bot's rail order
  (own → rival → commercial) keeps its shape and only its prices change. The
  five quoting sites become one `commercialFee(career, planet, speed)` plus the
  existing `price()` cheat wrapper; `CAREER_PURSE_RESERVE` becomes the *cheapest*
  commercial shot (`speed = SPEED_MIN` on the starter pad), so the reserve rule
  keeps meaning "one way off any planet, priced honestly".

## 2. the commercial rail as a *fallback*, not a floor

The note's gate is "no profit without a rail". Make the commercial fee **scale
with what it saves you**, and make it finite:

- `commercialFee(career, planet, speed) = COMMERCIAL_BASE + COMMERCIAL_KWH × energy`
  with `COMMERCIAL_BASE = 2.00` and `COMMERCIAL_KWH = 1.50` — so the tiny starter
  shot still costs about `3.00`, but a loaded `24 t` hop off a deep well costs
  what the energy says instead of the same `3.00`.
- Keep the *shape*: a commercial rail never claims the planet, never blocks a
  build, is always charged, and is always offered. The change is price, not
  availability, so nothing about `canFire`/`railBlock` structure moves.
- `evacuate 6.00` stays the emergency and is now chosen by the bench rather than
  inherited: a delivery that cannot fund a pad must still lose money, and
  `career_bench`'s `broke` share is the check.

## 3. a reason to own `EMC-3`

The cheapest demand is already in the draft as a quest: **fragile load**
(`0.6.3-plan-fragile-load.md`) refuses a rail above `EMC-1`. That is the opposite
pressure. The pressure *up* is a **deep-site contract**: a delivery whose
destination has `gravity ≥ DEEP_G` (2.2) is offered only with a note
`needs EMC-3+ at P#`, and landing there pays `×(1 + DEEP_BONUS)` (`0.6`) on top
of the ordinary reward. It is one branch in `makeDelivery` and one field on the
task, priced by the same `careerLandingFacts` table — no new leg kind.

`DEEP_G` is calibrated by the ladder itself: pick the gravity at which an
`EMC-2` + `BAT-2` cannot lift a loaded `HOLD-24` ship but an `EMC-3` + `BAT-3`
can, measured with `experiments/build_cost_probe.js`, so the tier boundary is a
fact of the tables, not a guess.

## GUI

- `pad` plate already prints the rail and the fee; it gains the **two prices** a
  guest sees (`fee 3.00 · own 0.00`) only while the ship is a guest — the
  existing `railOwnerText` line already names the owner, so this is one more
  clause.
- The career `upgrades` tab prints the tax a rival's pad would charge before a
  shot is fired, beside the existing `EMC` rows, so the bot's rail order is
  legible to a human too.
- A deep-site delivery row carries a `deep` tag and the `+bonus` in words, not a
  new colour.

## Validation

- `career_bench`, `career_bot_bench`, `career_gravity_bench`: no new failure
  name; purse per horizon printed before/after in one log
  (`experiments/logs/0.6.2-pad-fees-ab.txt`).
- A new check in `career_departure_test`: the *same* shot from the same planet
  costs more from `EMC-3` than from `EMC-1` for a guest, and the own-rail cost is
  unchanged at `0.00`.
- A check in `politics_test`-style scope for the deep-site offer: it is offered
  only when the destination is above `DEEP_G`, and only with a rail that can lift
  the cargo (both facts read from one helper, so the board and the bot agree).

## Scope boundary

No change to what a pad's battery holds, to the solar charge, or to the
`wait · 30 min` easing. No player-set tax yet — the note's *deferred* slider stays
deferred; this plan only makes the shipped fee derived, so a slider would then
have a number to slide. No new quest kind.
