# 0.4.35 — Trajectory sonification

## Goal

Add a third, independent sound generator without replacing the existing selected-WAV player or its fixed procedural fallback. The new generator is the default for a fresh preference store; an explicit mute remains respected. A persistent sound-settings selector offers:

- **Trajectory synth** (new, default, no media files);
- **Selected samples** (existing player, with its existing missing-file fallback);
- **Classic synths** (existing fixed procedural cues, explicitly without samples).

The generator only reads simulation/prediction state. It never changes physics, economics, aim inputs, replay encoding or bot decisions. Replay and rival flights remain silent.

## Aim mappings

Two selectable views of the same finished or partially traced prediction:

1. **Outcome + EMC** (default): a quiet profit chord (major/bright for positive, minor/darker for negative; magnitude controls intensity), short ticks whose logarithmic rate encodes path length, and a destination beacon. In career the beacon distinguishes an affordable, physically usable departure EMC from a blocked destination; rail tier and departure speed affect its interval/brightness. The chord uses the displayed net career profit, including launch fee and viable exit cost, not gross visit receipts.
2. **Trajectory fly-through**: compress the predicted visit log into a short repeating audition, preserving encounter order and relative timing. The phrase opens with the launch planet note, plays each visit with its deterministic planet note and screen-side pan, and closes on the destination note plus a bright fifth. While a long route is still tracing, only the continuous layers and length ticks run; a fly-through never presents a partial endpoint as final.

**Aim orientation (stereo)**: while the grip is pulled, a quiet drone sits beside the player. Its pan is the aim direction, its pitch and loudness grow with pull strength, and its note is the launch planet — so the aim can be set and held by ear. It fades when there is no aim data and never masks the profit chord or the EMC beacon.

Use the actual `predLogPlanet`/`predLogStep`, `predLength`, `predTime`, `predBody`, `predReason` and existing career economics. Lab has no invented profit. Route planning may keep the new sonification active after release; classic engines retain their old silent route-armed behavior. Impulse-burn previews may use the same aim sonification while the live flight cue ducks underneath.

## Flight mappings

Both variants identify the nearest active planet and turn distance and direction into left/right volume: the nearer the planet, the louder both sides; the farther to one side, the more the balance leans that way. Distance means distance from the cargo to the planet surface, clamped at zero inside the disc.

1. **Pentatonic proximity** (default): `get_tone_pentatonic(planet_id)` selects a stable note from a shared pentatonic collection. Closer planets are louder; left/right direction controls the stereo balance. Smooth note transitions and a gentle tremolo avoid clicks and make a fly-by audible.
2. **Instrument range** (alternate): `get_instrument(planet_id)` selects a deterministic synthetic timbre. Farther planets have higher pitch and lower volume; nearer planets are lower and louder. The same left/right proximity pan applies.

A short synthesized ignition replaces the fixed rock loop. Landing, cancellation, reset, mute and backgrounding fade/stop the appropriate buses. Switching generator or mapping mid-flight must not restart the simulation or replay an ignition.

### Playing by ear

The cues are layered so a player who cannot see the screen can still aim and judge a route:

- the orientation drone says where the grip points and how hard it is pulled (pan = direction, pitch and volume = strength, note = launch planet);
- the profit chord says whether the route is worth taking (major/bright = good, minor/darker = bad, magnitude = intensity);
- the length ticks say how long the path is (logarithmic rate);
- the EMC beacon says whether the destination departure is affordable and usable (blocked destinations arrive low and dissonant);
- the fly-through lets the player hear the whole predicted visit log as a short melody in encounter order, panned to where each encounter happens.

A future full trajectory preview could add a per-point scan (pitch = local gravity or speed, pan = screen position) so the arc itself becomes a melody, but none of the cues above change physics, economics, aim inputs, replay encoding or bot decisions.

## Implementation constraints

- Separate classic-script file and guarded Node export; no build, modules, network or media fetch.
- Create/resume Web Audio only after a user gesture. Reuse oscillators, gains, filters, panners and a small note pool; telemetry updates are throttled and use preallocated GUI scratch objects.
- Route/visit event buffers are fixed-size. No per-frame object/array/string allocation in the sonification hot path.
- Settings live in a compact collapsible sound group in the cargo panel: generator, aim mapping, flight mapping and master volume. Store preferences locally, validate restored values, and keep working when storage is unavailable.
- Preserve the existing `GravitySound.create` sample/fallback behavior for current callers. Add a controller that switches backends while preserving enabled/active/volume/phase state.

- Browser/file:// playback check when a Chromium runtime is available; otherwise report that limitation explicitly.
