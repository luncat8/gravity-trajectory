# 0.5.18 worklog — a yard on every planet, the bribe as a state, panel gain, and a lab that reprices a ladder

Four notes from play, one job each: two rules that hid themselves behind HQ or
behind a price, one ladder whose steps were too small to feel, and the tool that
should have shown both. The first two halves were started in an earlier pass that
ran out of budget; they are reviewed, tightened and documented here.

## What the notes asked for

1. "I can't refit ship / install engine. it told at HQ, but it is not clear where
   HQ is — make it possible on any planet."
2. "Bribe button does not appear. It should be visible while the ship is landed
   until the bribe is taken, then show again after visiting **another** planet."
3. "Increase the solar panel upgrade gain: base level same, gain 2× more."
4. "Short help in `balance_lab.html` for such a case; improve the lab so it is
   clear what to do and what to look for."

## Changes

1. **Any planet is a shipyard** (`gravity_game.js`, `gui_shop.js`, `orbit_tech.js`).
   `partRefusal` knows one reason not to fit a part — the ship is not standing
   anywhere (`land on a planet to refit`) — plus the cargo and purse checks; the
   HQ test is gone from the model *and* from the view, so the shop cannot offer
   what the law refuses. The refit block in the upgrades tab is headed by the
   planet under the ship rather than the planet picked for the pad rows, and
   `partCap` reads the rail the vessel actually stands on (HQ only while the ship
   is still in flight, where the pad line is the only one it has). Research
   prototypes follow the same rule: an owned record fits free wherever the ship
   landed. Keeping the *pad* rows planet-picked and the *ship* rows ship-picked
   is deliberate: gear belongs to a world, a hull belongs to itself.
2. **The bribe button is a state of the visit** (`gravity_game.js`, `gui_shop.js`).
   It now stands on the pad block from the moment the ship lands and until that
   visit has paid one; the next landing anywhere reopens it. Three fixes came out
   of the review:
   - the drawer compared `bribeVisitStep[planet] === lastVisitStep[planet]`
     itself while `bribeRefusal` checked the same pair with an extra `>= 0`
     guard. Two readings of one rule: on a planet nobody has landed on yet (the
     ship's own start pad) the button hid although the rulers still owed one, and
     a bribe paid there could be paid again for ever. `bribeWindowOpen(market,
     planet)` is now the single predicate the law and the drawer share.
   - `bribeVisitStep`'s "no bribe yet" marker was `-1`, one constant change away
     from colliding with the `lastVisitStep` fill of `-initSteps()`; it is
     `BRIBE_OPEN = -0x7fffffff` now, outside any stamp a run holds, so the
     equality test needs no guard at all.
   - the button was disabled below `BRIBE_MIN`, which is the *bot's* floor, not a
     law: a small offer read as a missing feature. Any bribe the rulers actually
     pay (`amount > 0`) is the player's to take; under the floor the tooltip says
     what a bot would do instead of locking the choice. A clean world still keeps
     the button and prices it `+0.00`, because the rule ("the visit value × the
     rot × 4") is what the player is learning.
3. **Panel gain doubled, base untouched** (`orbit_tech.js`): `SP-1 1.0` stays and
   every step above it is doubled — `1.0 / 2.4 / 4.5 / 7.5` → `1.0 / 3.8 / 8.0 /
   14.0` kW, i.e. `0.50 / 1.90 / 4.00 / 7.00` kWh per 30 min wait at full sun and
   a `BAT-1` refill in 90 → 24 min at SP-2. Only the gains are a balance lever:
   the build price is derived from the spec, so the ladder now costs
   `0.40 / 2.15 / 5.64 / 11.74` where it cost `0.40 / 1.19 / 2.67 / 5.19`, and
   the `PANEL` frontier (`above: 1.00`) is what makes a stronger panel dearer to
   invent rather than merely rarer.
4. **The lab learned the case** (`balance_lab.html`): see the next section.

## The balance lab: help, and a card that reprices a table

The lab could price a *draft point* but not a *ladder*, which is what a note like
note 3 is actually about — so a panel change meant reading six numbers off the
build table by hand. Two additions:

- **help** — a collapsible card at the top: what each block is for (plot, draft,
  result, knobs, build table, copy boxes) and the five-step walk through note 3,
  including the numbers the shipped table came from, so the example can be checked
  against `orbit_tech.js`. It opens until it has been dismissed once
  (`localStorage`, and always shown where storage is refused).
- **ladder gain card** — works on every table, build-only kinds (`panel`, `hold`)
  included, which is the point: those have no research family, so the plot has
  nothing to show and the build cost is the whole price.
  - `keep` picks the rung that stays exactly as it is (the "base level same" of
    the note); `gain ×` scales every rung above it by its distance from that rung,
    rounded to the digits the table is written with, and the whole thing is one
    linear map — the shipped tables are never mutated.
  - each row prints the rung's value **in the unit the shop prints** — kWh per
    wait for a panel, px/s rail cap for a catapult, kWh for a battery, t for a
    hold, px/s of one burn for an engine or a tank — the `step` to it over the
    rung under it *and* the price that step costs, and `per 1.00`, the two numbers
    that decide whether a ladder is too weak.
  - the verdict line reads the curve: `!` on a flat rung (nobody buys it), `↓` on
    a rung paying less per money than the one below, `thin` when that falls under
    half — a mild decline is a normal convex ladder, and the note says so instead
    of crying wolf; then the total drift `Δ` against the shipped table, which is
    zero exactly when the knobs and `orbit_tech.js` agree.
  - `copy table patch` is the whole `const PANELS = […]` block — every record
    including the free tier-0 one, specs only, because `priceStock` derives the
    price from them — with the implied values and costs in a trailing comment.
  The card follows the fee knobs (`k`, `buildShare`, merit, band), because it
  calls the same mirrored pricing, so a repricing and a fee law can be tuned in
  one screen against each other.
