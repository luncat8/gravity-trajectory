# 0.4.34 worklog — one spec merit curve per science axis

## Review of 0.4.33

Its mass factor was the right idea and it reported the fee honestly, but it was
bolted to one characteristic:

- `MASS_REFERENCE = { engine: 1.0, tank: 0.5 }` hardcoded to `params[1]`, so
  only the two part families with a mass axis had an absolute curve at all.
- Par was the **lightest** rung, not the ordinary one, so the whole engine and
  tank ladder sat below ×1 and was discounted systematically (IMP-2 at 1.6 t
  already paid ×0.76).
- Every other axis kept `0.4.32`'s flat easy direction: worsening costs nothing
  extra and **saves** nothing. Measured on the base commit (hull seeded from the
  tech tables): battery 6 kWh costs 0.667 for every leak from 0.0003 to
  0.0012 /y, battery 22 kWh costs 11.227 for all of them, and a 100 kN rail
  costs 2.000 at 50 % and at 88 % efficiency. A battery that leaks four times
  faster than the best pad battery, or a rail that throws away twice the
  energy, was exactly as dear to invent as the tight one — the defect in the
  report.
- The panel told the story of mass only (`mass · 2× heavier = fee ÷1.5 ·
  reference 1 t`) and the copy-knobs balance line had no merit row.

## What 0.4.34 does

**`orbit_science.js`** — `MASS_REFERENCE`/`MASS_EXPONENT`/`massFactor` are
replaced by one `MERIT` row per research axis (`0.4.34-plan-sci-merit-curves.md`
has the reasoning):

| family | axis 0 (capability, ×1.25 per doubling) | axis 1 (waste, ×1.50 per doubling) |
|---|---|---|
| engine | thrust, par 24 kN | mass, par 1.6 t, `lower` |
| catapult | maxForce, par 135 kN | efficiency, par 0.20 **loss** (`mirror: 1`), `lower` |
| tank | fuelCap, par 90 kg | emptyMass, par 0.9 t, `lower` |
| battery | capacity, par 6 kWh | leak, par 0.0006 /y, `lower` |

- par is the **middle rung** of the family's own table (IMP-2, EMC-3, FT-2,
  BAT-3), one rule for every axis, and it costs exactly the base fee.
- a bounded axis is measured on its loss `mirror − value`, so halving a rail's
  losses is the same step as doubling a capability; a ratio on raw efficiency
  would be meaningless near 1.
- `axisMerit(family, axis, value)` returns `Infinity` for an impossible value
  (nonpositive, or at/past the mirror), so a massless engine or a lossless rail
  cannot be researched — the old nonpositive-mass rule, generalised.
- `meritFactor` multiplies the whole fee `(base + k·(dist + frontier))` and is
  clamped to `MERIT_MIN 0.35 … MERIT_MAX 2.50`: the lab is never free for a
  deliberately poor spec, and two compounding curves cannot price a corner out
  of the game.
- `previewHull` returns `merit` instead of `massFactor`.

**`gravity_gui.js`** — the preview line reads `spec ×…`; a new `sciMerit` line
shows the draft's per-axis factors (`spec · thrust ×1.03 · mass ×0.93 → ×0.96`,
patched live while a slider is dragged); a rule line gives par and the step
(`spec rule · thrust 2× more = ×1.25 (par 24.0) · mass 2× better = ×1.50 (par
1.6) · band ×0.35–×2.50`); `copy knobs` gained a `merit: …` balance row.

**`README.md`** — the mass-factor sentence is now the spec-merit sentence, and
`orbit_science.js`'s file note lists the new API.

**`experiments/sci_merit_probe.js` + `logs/0.4.34-sci-merit.txt`** — the fee
surface of every family over the slider grid (41×41), the par / slider-default
/ bot-R&D fees, and the four "worse is cheaper" sweeps, with the base-commit
run appended for comparison.

lightest rung; the catapult got cheaper because the bridge-rail target is far
below its mid rung. Every default still fits the 6.00 starter purse and both
bot R&D careers still complete with 3 researches.

## Next step

The prototype **build** cost still follows the primary stat's rung only
(`rungCost` in `orbit_tech.js`), so a custom battery that leaks four times
faster than BAT-1 costs the same to build as a tight one, and a heavy custom
engine costs the same as a light one of the same thrust. Extending the same
merit idea to `buildCost` is the missing half of "worse is cheaper" — but it
touches the shop, the bulk-upgrade total and every bot plan, so it wants its
own plan and a measured sweep like `logs/0.4.34-sci-merit.txt`.
