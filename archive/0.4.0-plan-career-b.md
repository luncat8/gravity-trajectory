## 1. Goal

Add a career mode to the gravity trajectory game. The player starts on a random
planet with a weak electromagnetic (EM) catapult, a solar panel and a battery.
The ship has no cargo hold at start, so only visit-observation and route
competitions can be flown — no cargo deliveries yet.

The player collects money by completing generated tasks:

- visit (fly near) long-not-visited planets — always available;
- land on a specific planet = delivery — often; requires a cargo hold of a
  specific size; cargo adds mass, so it needs stronger launch force and more
  energy to launch;
- longest route competition — sometimes.

A planet can only be launched from if it has a built EM catapult. Landing on a
planet without a catapult forces an expensive evacuation back to HQ, so any
delivery to a catapult-less planet is unprofitable — the player must build a
catapult on the target planet before (or right after) the delivery to make it
pay. Catapult, solar panel and battery are planet equipment, built once per
planet and shared by every visitor.

The mode ends when the player has built catapults on all planets (endgame) or
goes broke.

## 2. Starting state

- Random start planet (HQ of the first save).
- Ship: minimal dry mass, no cargo hold, no impulse engine, no fuel tank.
- Planet equipment on the start planet: weak EM catapult, small solar panel,
  small battery.
- One save slot per seed; visited planets and built planet equipment persist
  per save.
- Money screen shows purse and the catapult network (which planets have a
  built catapult / panel / battery and at which tier).

## 3. Task families

### 3.1 Visit (always available)

Fly near a planet that has not been visited for the longest time. Reward paid
on first entry into the planet's influence/sphere. No cargo hold needed.
Purpose: early-game income before the catapult network exists.

### 3.2 Delivery (often)

Land cargo on a specific target planet. Requires a cargo hold of at least the
task's size. Cargo mass adds to ship mass:

- bigger hold → heavier cargo → more launch force needed;
- more launch force → more energy from the target planet's battery/solar;

The delivery pays only if the player can **launch again from the target**. That
requires a built EM catapult on the target planet whose power is enough to
launch the loaded ship (and enough battery charge for the shot). If the target
has no catapult, the player must call an expensive evacuation back to HQ, which
makes the delivery unprofitable. So the rational play is: build a catapult on
the target planet before or right after the delivery, then launch with profit.

### 3.3 Longest route competition (sometimes)

Timed competition: longest successful route (flight length or flight time)
within a window beats the bot and other records. Entry fee optional; prize paid
on completion.

## 4. Money, launches and the catapult network

HQ is just the starting planet — there is **no special HQ cash-out**. Money is
earned directly from completed tasks.

A planet can only be **launched from** if it has a built EM catapult. Catapult,
solar panel and battery are **planet equipment**: built once per planet,
shared by every visitor, and persisting between visits.

- Landing on a planet **without** a catapult: the player cannot launch again
  from there. They must call an **evacuation** — an automatic, expensive return
  to HQ. The evacuation fee is large enough that any delivery to a catapult-less
  planet is unprofitable. This is the core progression gate: earn money from
  tasks → build a catapult on the target planet → land and launch with profit
  (if the catapult's power is enough to continue or return).
- The player can build a catapult (or panel/battery) on any planet at any time,
  provided they have enough money.
- **GUI bulk upgrade**: applying a selected upgrade (catapult / panel / battery
  tier) upgrades **all already-built facilities** of that kind across planets at
  once, and the GUI shows the total cost before confirming.

Progression: use available tasks to earn money → build a catapult on the target
planet → be able to land and launch with profit (if catapult power is enough to
continue/return).

## 5. Ship model

Only **ship parts** add to ship mass (which feeds launch physics):

- dry ship mass (base);
- cargo hold — empty mass + cargo mass (cargo tons add directly);
- impulse engine — mass;
- fuel tank — empty mass (+ fuel mass when loaded).

Catapult, solar panel and battery are **planet equipment** — they do **not**
add to ship mass. They determine whether a launch from a given planet is
possible at all, and how much energy it costs.

Bought ship parts can be installed/uninstalled at HQ; total ship mass is
recomputed from the active install set. No upgrade is permanent.

## 6. EM catapult / solar / battery power model (planet equipment)

Per planet, persisting between visits:

- **Catapult**: `maxForce`, `efficiency`, `buildCost`, `chargeTime`, `energyPerShot`.
  Launch constraint: the aim must satisfy `requiredEnergy <= planet battery charge`.
  If not, launch is disabled ("not enough charge — wait for sun or upgrade
  panel/battery"). Required energy is shown live while aiming.
- **Solar panel**: generates `P = panelPower * planetLight * starLum` watts
  continuously while the clock advances (`planetLight` / star `lum` from
  `orbit_world.js`). Charges the planet's battery.
- **Battery**: stores `capacity` kWh, has `leak` per year, charge level is
  per-planet and persists between visits. A dim outer world charges slowly
  unless the panel is upgraded.

Launch energy = f(ship mass + cargo mass, launch force, escape speed) /
catapult efficiency, drawn from that planet's battery. Without a catapult on
the planet, no launch is possible — evacuation is the only way out.

## 7. Tech tree — what is sold

Split into **planet gear** (built per planet, shared, no ship mass) and
**ship parts** (installed on the ship, add mass).

Start: HQ planet has `catapult-1`, `panel-1`, `battery-1`. Ship has no hold.

| group | examples | primary stats per level | mass? | build cost | gameplay effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Catapult (planet) | `EMC-1` → `EMC-5` | `maxForce`, `efficiency` | pad (not ship) | high (3–20 purse) | stronger launches, heavier ships allowed, less energy per shot |
| Solar panel (planet) | `SP-1` → `SP-4` | `power (W)` | pad | medium | faster charging, viable on dim worlds |
| Battery (planet) | `BAT-1` → `BAT-5` | `capacity (kWh)`, `leak` | pad | medium | buffer one or two heavy shots without waiting |
| Cargo hold (ship) | `0` → `8 t` → `24 t` | `capacity (t)` | empty mass + cargo | medium | unlocks delivery; bigger holds need stronger catapult |
| Impulse engine (ship) | none → `IMP-1` → `IMP-3` | `thrust (kN)`, `Isp (s)` | ship | high | pause-at-any-time Δv impulse |
| Fuel tank (ship) | `FT-0` → `FT-3` | `fuelCap (kg)` | light + fuel mass | medium | more Δv budget per flight |
| Auto-helpers (GUI) | tier 0 → 2 | `helperBudget` | none | 1–4 | unlocks scan/tweak launch helpers |

Balance note: **cargo capacity directly trades against catapult requirement**.
A 24 t hold full of ore is not lifted by `EMC-1` at all — the UI must show this
as red ("too heavy: need EMC-3 or less cargo").

Auto-helpers start locked; buying them unlocks the helper logic already shipped
for `route` (broad `quick scan`, focused `scan here`, `tweak best`). In career,
`quick scan` also proposes return legs to HQ when evaluating delivery
profitability.

## 8. Evacuation (the no-catapult penalty)

When the player lands on a planet with no built catapult:

- launch is disabled;
- the only exit is **evacuation**: automatic return to HQ at a flat, high fee;
- the fee is large enough that delivering to a catapult-less planet loses money,
  so the rational play is to build a catapult on the target before (or right
  after) the delivery.

The evacuation fee and catapult build cost are tuned in `career_bench.js` so
that a delivery is profitable only once the target has a catapult strong enough
to launch the loaded ship back.

## 10. Science (knowledge volume)

- User sets a floating-point slider for the parameter they want to research.
- A calculator shows the preview cost; on accept, the item becomes available.
- Knowledge volume = N-dimensional space of discovered item parameter vectors.
  Items inside the discovered volume are cheap; items outside need new
  scientific work whose cost grows with distance from the hull (e.g. `k·dist`).
- Convex hull of discovered points defines the cheap interior.
- Example: engine {force: 1, mass: 10} discovered; invent {force: 2, mass: 15}
  → a line of possible cheap products. Next invent {force: 1, mass: 1} → the
  hull becomes a triangle on the 2-D (force, mass) space of cheap products.
- Slider UI: pick target parameters, preview cost from distance-to-hull, accept
  to unlock/buy. The panel also draws the fee surface over both slider axes,
  the hull outline and the dashed per-axis researched range.
- Four 2-D families, seeded by their own tech tables: `engine (thrust, mass)`,
  `catapult (maxForce, efficiency)`, `tank (fuelCap, emptyMass)` and
  `battery (capacity, leak)`. One table (`SCIENCE_FAMILIES` in
  `gravity_game.js`) holds the family → ladder table, its `params` axis order
  and its record builder; hull seeding, prototype lists and the accept gate
  all follow from it, while `gravity_gui.js` only adds slider ranges and draft
  defaults. The tier-0 empty slot of a part ladder is not a known tradeoff and
  is not seeded.
- An accepted point becomes an owned prototype on its family's ladder:
  engine/tank join the HQ refit block, catapult/battery join the pad rows and
  obey the same upgrade rule and `all N` bulk total as a bought tier.

### 10.1 Directional difficulty (frontier)

Difficulty is not symmetric: a lighter engine is much harder to invent than a
heavier one, and higher efficiency is harder than lower. Because the seeded
tech ladders lie along diagonal performance chords (`IMP-1 12 kN / 1.0 t →
IMP-3 40 kN / 2.5 t`, `EMC-1 15 kN / 0.62 → EMC-5 180 kN / 0.88`), a symmetric
Euclidean hull distance would charge almost as much for an easy heavy/weak step
away from that diagonal as for a genuinely hard lightweight/high-efficiency step.
Both terms therefore respect per-axis direction:

```
stepWeight   = min(1, below) when p[axis] < hullPoint[axis] else min(1, above)
dirDist      = min over hull of || (p - hullPoint) · metricScale · stepWeight ||₂
range[axis]  = [min, max] over every discovered point of the family
overshoot    = max(0, min - p[axis], p[axis] - max) · metricScale[axis]   (RU)
frontier     = Σ overshoot · (below | above)[family][axis]                  (RU)
fee          = base + k · tierScale[family] · (dirDist + frontier)
```

- `dirDist` discounts easy-direction steps (`weight < 1`, e.g. heavier mass or
  sloppier efficiency) inside and outside the researched range via a lifted
  positive-combination Wolfe minimum-norm solve, while keeping full Euclidean
  weight (`1.0`) along hard directions and preserving exact convex-hull interior
  classification (`inside ⇔ fee = base`).
- `metricScale` makes one RU a deliberate invention step (10 kN thrust = 1 t
  engine mass; 30 kN rail force = 0.10 efficiency; 50 kg fuel = 0.5 t tank
  mass; 5 kWh battery capacity = 0.0004 leak), so engineering units do not set
  the balance by accident. The panel prints `1 RU = …` straight from that
  table, so the readout cannot drift from the balance data.
- `below`/`above` is a flat balance table per family and axis in
  `orbit_science.js`, printed by `career_bench.js` next to the `tech:` spec
  lines. Engine and tank mass are `↓×3.00 / ↑×0.10`, catapult efficiency
  `↓×0.10 / ↑×2.50`, battery leak `↓×2.50 / ↑×0.10`; thrust, force, fuel
  capacity and battery capacity are easy downwards (`↓×0.10`) and hard upwards
  (`↑×1.00`–`1.20`).
- A point inside the hull is a recombination and pays only the base fee — no
  frontier. A point outside the hull but inside the range pays `k·dirDist`
  (cheap on the easy side of the chord, dear on the hard side). Outside the
  range, `frontier` adds the steep breakthrough surcharge on the hard borders
  (highlighted in amber on the cost plot).
- Accepting a point expands the hull **and** the range, so a paid breakthrough
  makes the whole new span cheap for later inventions; the cost surface and the
  dashed range box follow it.
- The preview readout names both terms (`dist … RU · frontier +… RU · fee …`),
  and the metric line prints the current family's `↓×… ↑×…` knobs. Its `copy
  knobs` button shares that family's `frontier: … { below, above }` balance line
  like the shop's `copy` buttons, so the difficulty is as easy to tune as a tech
  record.

## 11. Balance tooling

Balance is hard, so every tech item gets a "copy" button that copies
`tech: <id> { name, params, price, mass, … }` to the clipboard so the user can
paste it into an LLM chat and ask for a price/mass adjustment. Keep a bench
that prints all tech stats so numbers can be tweaked and re-checked quickly.
