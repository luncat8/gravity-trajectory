# 0.4.22 worklog — launch label, career in-game time, rival EMC alignment, host battery drain

## Goal (user brief)
- The launch button states what the shot uses: an own EMC spends pad energy
  (`launch, 1.23 kW`); a fallback rail (commercial EMC-1 or a rival's pad) shows
  its price (`launch, 3.00`).
- Other modes may animate always — their animation is background, not gameplay.
- Career lives on the in-game clock: a paused career shows no animation and no
  EMC barrel rotation.
- Rivals obey the same EMC alignment queue as the player: the barrel is a fixed
  facility on the planet that turns with the world clock.
- Host-battery drain on guest launches: the pad that fires spends its charge,
  guest or owner alike; whoever queued the launch first reserved that energy.

## Implementation
- `gravity_gui.js`
  - `launchRideText` / `launchRideTitle`: the armed career launch labels its
    firing rail — `, <kWh> kW` for an own EMC pad, `, <fee>` for the commercial
    EMC-1 or a rival pad. The label text is now the change key of
    `setLaunchState`, so the live numbers refresh as the aim moves.
  - `drawCareerEmc` turns the barrels on `game.clockSteps · DT` (world seconds).
  - A paused career draws animated ticks at the pause instant (`tickNow`), so
    visits, ships, landed base and cargo pulses hold still; other modes keep
    animating.
  - The turret queue works in world steps: `queueTurretShot` records
    `fireAt = clockSteps + wait · 120`, resumes the clock so the barrel can
    turn, and reserves the shot energy on the firing pad; `tickTurret` fires
    once the clock reaches the aligned step. The wind-up no longer freezes the
    world, so pads charge, visits grow and rivals keep flying.
- `gravity_game.js`
  - `careerBeginFlight` drains the firing pad's battery for any non-commercial
    EMC (own or a rival's) and keeps the rival usage tax; the commercial rail
    stays charged and charges its flat fee.
  - `reserveEnergy`/`releaseEnergy` + `pad.reservedKwh`: the first queued launch
    claims the charge. `COMMERCIAL_PAD` never reserves or drains.
- `gravity_sim.js`: `speedLimit` aims with `chargeKwh − reservedKwh`.
- `gravity_bot.js`: rivals plan, queue (reserving energy), and fire only once the
  barrel reaches their aim on the shared in-game clock; `careerDropQueue` gives a
  restarting company's claim back.
- `orbit_view.js`: `setFrozen` stops the ambient `framePulse` while a career
  clock is paused (moons and ports already follow `sim.t`).

## Validation
- `node experiments/dom_smoke.js` — SMOKE OK, including the new label assertions
  and the wind-up now advancing the clock instead of freezing it.
- `node experiments/career_bench.js` — CAREER BENCH OK, plus new checks:
  * guest launch drains the rival pad battery, pays the usage tax to its owner
    and releases the reservation;
  * a queued launch lowers the aim ceiling and release restores it; the
    commercial rail never reserves.
- `node experiments/career_bot_bench.js` — CAREER BOT BENCH OK, plus a new check
  that a rival queues, holds the pad charge and fires exactly at the aligned
  step.
- `game_bench`, `bot_bench`, `route_bench`, `gravity_bench`, `colony_bench` —
  unchanged results.

## Decisions / open points
- The energy unit on the button follows the brief's example (`kW`), while the
  drawer keeps showing `kWh` for pad charge.
- `padAt` still prefers your own rail even when it is flat; the fallback
  (commercial or rival) is picked with the planet-action button. A drained own
  pad shows its shortfall on the button instead of silently charging a fee.
- The turret period stays 2…5 s of world time, so a fast career playback winds
  the barrel proportionally faster.

## Next step suggestion
- Career ticker for reserved charge (e.g. dim the pad bar by `reservedKwh`) so a
  guest on a rival rail can see the charge a queued neighbour already claimed.
