# 0.4.6 Worklog — a shop that states its caps and a controller that will not bounce

## Review of 0.4.5

0.4.5 gave the drag the pad's real ceiling and added the experimental
gravity/force panel. The ceiling was right; the *shop* was still silent about
it. The drawer printed only the landed pad's live cap, so a player could not
answer "can this pad lift this cargo, and what does the next rail buy?" before
paying for it. The planet picker listed gear ids and nothing else.

Three smaller findings:

- The pad readout had no full-battery cap, so a charging pad (`max 20 px/s`)
  looked weaker than the pad really is; only the drawn canvas plate had the
  number.
- `clampArmedToPad` could only *lower* the armed speed. Raising the force
  slider grew the drag range under an unchanged `gripLen`, so the drawn grip
  dot no longer pointed at the armed speed until the next pointer move.
- The scaled world was never measured. `career_bot_bench` only flew `g 1 · f 1`,
  so a slider change had no headless guardian. Probing the hostile pair
  (`g ×1.4`, `f ×0.6`) found a real controller bug: a shot that lands back on
  the pad it left scores `CAREER_SAME_PAD_VALUE = -1`, and because any finite
  score beats none, the controller flew that pointless bounce **400 times**
  (purse stuck at 4.85, 8 pads, 0 deliveries, 253 waits) instead of waiting or
  upgrading the rail.

## Implementation

### 1. The drawer states every cap

`padCap(career, catapult, battery)` = `OrbitTech.speedLimit(full battery,
ship.mass, catapult, gravity.forceScale)` — the same gate the pad fires under,
so the shop and the launch button can never disagree.

- **planet picker**: `P4 · 118 px/s · EMC-2`, `P7 · no pad`, `P9 · cannot lift`
  (`cap < SPEED_MIN`). The cap comes *before* the gear id so a narrow picker
  clips the id, not the answer.
- **shop rows**: the rail row prints `92 → 119 px/s`, the battery row prints
  what a bigger battery adds (often nothing while the rail is the limit), and a
  panel row prints nothing — a panel moves the recharge time, not the cap.
- **pad block**: `max 92 px/s · shot 1.02 kWh` on a full battery, `max 20 px/s ·
  full 92 px/s · shot 0.02 kWh` while charging (the `full` term appears only
  when the charge actually bites), and `cannot lift 38.0 t · buy a bigger rail
  below` when even a full battery cannot fire.
- **first pad**: the `build EMC-1 here` title states the cap a fresh pad would
  reach with the starter battery, and the row carries the same number.
- Every number follows `gravity.forceScale`, so the shop moves with the
  experimental panel (proved in `dom_smoke`).

### 2. The grip follows its drag range

`aimCeiling` records the range the grip was placed on. `clampArmedToPad` now
re-places the grip whenever the ceiling moved in *either* direction (still
clamping a shot the pad can no longer fire), so the dot keeps showing the armed
speed. `aimState()` exposes `{ angle, speed, grip, ceiling }` for the bench.

### 3. A pointless bounce is not a plan

`careerScoreProbe` returns `-Infinity` for a shot that lands back on the pad it
left when it pays nothing (no new systems, no delivery, no sport reward). The
idle policy then waits for charge or upgrades the pad instead of flying the same
empty shot. The four standard careers are bit-identical — the guard never fires
in a `6.00` world — while the hostile scaled world goes from "400 flights,
never finishes" to 26 pads in 69 flights.

### 4. Scaled careers in the controller bench

`career_bot_bench` takes `(gravityScale, forceScale)`, applies them to both the
sim and the controller, and flies two scaled worlds, each with its own purse
gate of `6.00`:

| world | seeds | result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `g ×0.8 · f ×1.5` | 42, 7 | 10.83 / 6.60 |
| `g ×1.4 · f ×0.6` | 42 | 6.32 (the §3 regression test) |

## Next step

The panel is now the only shop row without a number, and the drawer never says
how soon a pad can fire again. Give the panel row the same treatment as the
rail: `+N kWh · ready in N s` from `career.light[planet] × panel.power`
(`waitGain` already computes the 30-minute gain for the wait button), and let
`wait · 30 min` state what it will settle — kWh per pad, board ageing, planet
profit — in its title before it runs. That closes the shop: every purchase can
be judged by "how fast", "how far" and "how much".
