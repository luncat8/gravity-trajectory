# 0.5.6 — income-priced career pads: pad gear, launch-book money, shallow HQs finished

## Review of the previous change (e6da7d9)

The committed change replaced "force the player's HQ gravity to 1" with a seeded
shallow HQ (`HQ_GRAVITY_MAX = 1`; `pickHq` rejects deep worlds for every company)
and taught the career site value to pay for reach, so a bot no longer builds on a
well its starter rail cannot leave. Three things were left undone:

- `career_guidance_test` and `career_departure_test` were red. Both hard-coded
  angles and expectations from the forced-gravity HQ. The guidance fixture now
  searches the live seeded pad (`seek()` over 64 angles) for a blocked and a safe
  landing; the departure test states the spread the new rule produces (mean 3.78
  starter-blocked planets over 100 seeds, 71/100 inside the 2–4 target).
- The pad-gear follow-up sat half-built in the tree: the winning rung was only
  ever bought when it happened to be a rail or battery (a panel could win the
  offer and never leave the shop), a rung the purse could not afford blocked the
  cheaper profitable ones, and no launch remembered what it actually earned, so
  nothing could be priced per world second.
- README and the career help still described only the old site rule.

## Changes

- **`careerPadGear`** (`gravity_bot.js`): once per idle turn, standing on its own
  pad, the bot prices the next panel, battery and rail from that pad's own launch
  book. `cycle = flight + refill(energy − current charge)` in world seconds,
  `yield = money / cycle`; a rung is bought when `Δyield × CAREER_PAD_HORIZON`
  (40 min of world time) covers its price. A rail is priced as rail + the cheapest
  known battery that reaches its force cap. `careerGearOffer` drops rungs the
  purse cannot pay while keeping `CAREER_PURSE_RESERVE` (3.00, one commercial
  launch) in hand, so the best *affordable* rung wins instead of the best rung
  being refused. The decision log prints
  `gear · P11 EMC-2+BAT-3 · 9.57 · yield +0.42/h · payback 82 s`.
- **Launch-book money** (`gravity_game.js`, `gravity_bot.js`, `gui_book.js`): a
  saved trace now carries `money`, the cash the flight itself collected, beside
  the net `score` the list is ordered by; `recordLaunch` falls back to `score`
  when a caller has none, and `gui_book.saveTrace` stores `money.cash`.
- **Bench** (`experiments/career_bot_bench.js`): career lines print `gear N` and
  `$/h` of world time; `plantLaunch()` seeds a pad's book; `makeSeededWorld()`
  builds the seed's own wells for the checks that need reach; two new checks
  cover the panel buy on a starving pad and the rail+battery pair price with its
  refusal under a thin purse; `NOGEAR=1` stubs the policy for A/B runs.
- **Docs**: README career row and `gravity_bot.js` file row describe the pad-gear
  rule; `gui_tables.js` career help mentions it.

## Next step

The shop scores gear in money per world second while `careerScoreFacts` still
scores a shot in plain money, so a rich shot that drains a dim pad looks better
than it is and the panel row can only win on pads the battery cannot help. Add the
same cycle term (flight + the pad's refill at its own panel) to the plan score and
re-run the eight-seed bench: the panel should then compete with the battery on
equal terms, and the `CAREER_PAD_HORIZON` payback can be shortened to match.
