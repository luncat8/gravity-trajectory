Both retain the current Capital recovery code, so this isolates exploration rather than reproducing the complete historical `1bd3eb1` revision (the shallow checkout does not contain that commit). The control reproduces the previous baseline's printed aggregate: 0.036 profit/s, 59.64 property. Candidate: 0.028/s, 47.51. The figures are endpoint snapshots and rounded; shared harvest and changing flight timing mean they are not independent player trials.

The control reuses a book shot, angle 4.32, speed 83.5, predicted pay 7.62 and score 6.79; the candidate scans 818 traces and selects angle 3.32, speed 83.6, predicted pay 10.78 and score 9.27. The candidate lands at 478.3 rather than 439.7, repairs for 2.00 rather than 1.32, and takes a 3.11 rather than 2.48 bribe. The fresh route's longer trip and different visits also change shared harvest available to R1 before its decision at 394.2. Later R1's candidate route to P9 is delayed, followed by an evacuation; both players' purchases and trajectories diverge. This demonstrates an exploration-triggered regression, not proof that the first shot alone accounts for the entire property difference or that predicted pay was realized in full. Do not increase exploration frequency on this evidence; inspect the first shot's visit-level forecast versus realized income and wear in a follow-up.

## Career recovery: gameplay review

The current `checkEnded` treats a negative purse, or a landed unusable ship without the full repair price, as permanent bankruptcy immediately. `bribeRefusal` rejects ended careers and the wait UI is disabled, so the rule prevents the very recovery it fails to consider. A bribe can grow while landed when its visit window remains open, subject to political restrictions and arrest risk. Rival rail-use tax is contingent on rivals actually launching; neither is guaranteed. Ten minutes passing therefore cannot establish that recovery is impossible, nor should lack of income at that deadline automatically end a human career.

Recommended design: call the condition **stranded / needs repair**, keep the career open, allow waiting and otherwise legal income actions, and show repair cost, purse and available recovery options. Let the player voluntarily restart rather than force an irreversible loss merely because a timer expired. For bot-controlled companies, allow at least 600 game seconds of recovery before considering a restart; reset the window when the company becomes able to repair or otherwise recovers. The bot should take legal bribes and repair when affordable rather than wait blindly, and must not repeatedly try impossible launches. Game time, not wall time, makes pause, accelerated wait and replay deterministic. A 600-second minimum is a proposed interpretation of the user's ten minutes, not a claim about real-time duration.

Separate decisions are needed for negative purses, lost-in-space/evacuation, already-closed bribe windows, no rivals and permanently unavailable income. Consider an explicit bot give-up policy after the minimum rather than claiming those cases can recover. Preserve completion precedence, voluntary player restart, state reset, seat handoff and replay determinism; old recordings can change outcome when the rules change. Regression coverage should exercise income during the window, a bribe that becomes sufficient only after waiting, tax-funded recovery, no-income waiting beyond ten minutes, pauses/fast-forward, consecutive episodes and bot restart timing. No gameplay rule has been changed in this review; the minimum's clock and eventual bot give-up policy need agreement before implementation.
