## correction — the landing-time race (replaces the 45 s horizon)

The first pass invented `RADAR_HORIZON = 45` and made the bot **avoid** any
planet a rival's trajectory landed on inside that window. That is not the
draft: the draft's reveal is the *time to the next landing* (`+35 s` on his,
`+41 s` on mine) with no horizon, and its help text says a radar reader should
"know competitor's move and adjust own aim to **outperform** or avoid risky
landing". The 45 s window also contradicted the reveal itself — a rival landing
at +60 s was drawn on the map but ignored by the bot.

- `gravity_game.js`: dropped `RADAR_HORIZON`; added `RADAR_BEAT` (the same
  fraction as `RADAR_AVOID`) and fixed the `RADAR_BUDGET_MS` comment — it is a
  real-CPU-millisecond budget (the aim-trace class), not "six game seconds".
- `gravity_bot.js`: `careerRadarHot` now memoizes the **earliest rival landing
  time** per planet (no horizon filter), and `careerRadarAvoid` takes the
  candidate's own landing time (`steps · DT`, passed from `careerScoreFacts`)
  and races it against the rival's: land first → gain `RADAR_BEAT`; land later
  → concede `RADAR_AVOID`; a sat-on planet or a rival-owned rail always
  concedes. Bots without radar are unchanged.
- `experiments/radar_test.js`: the reveal asserts the real landing time; the
  bot check asserts both race outcomes (slower concedes, faster gains).
- `README.md` and the plan's section 4/6/files updated to the race.
