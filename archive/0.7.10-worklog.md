# 0.7.10 — the url is the run, and a refused opening costs what a fair one costs — 2026-11-16

## review of the previous stage

0.7.9 was reviewed by reading its code, plan and worklog; its tests were not
rerun as a verdict on it. The base commit `eb5b5a6` was run once, in a scratch
export, as the reference for the two numbers this stage moves (a career boot on
a refused seed, and the wave of bench checks).

Two things 0.7.9 left "for the human" were decided the way the human asked:

- **the url follows the state.** 0.7.9 wrote "`?map=` pins the roster size and
  the universe to the record and ignores `bot`, `cheat`, `src` and `rule` in the
  address … it is the human's call". The call: a url must be consistent with
  what the page is after load.
- **a seed with no fair opening.** 0.7.9's next step was "make the opening
  search cheap when it finds nothing fair", with the fairness rule unchanged.

Seen while reading, not changed (not this stage's subject):

- `career_start_fairness.js` is in no tier and is still red at its own default
  (`SEEDS=1-8`: seeds 4 and 6 at 52% and 25% of the best, against 0.55), on this
  tree and on the base commit. It does not measure what the search measures:
  seed 6's plan reads 25% through its own `assignDistinct` and 79% through
  `openingGroupStats` on the same three seats. **Correction (0.7.13):** it
  already traced the final pinned field; the difference was its matcher
  maximizing the weakest pay without also limiting the richest, and testing
  exits after matching. Its old columns were not a second opinion on the
  search; nothing in 0.7.10 changed its output.
- `gravity_bench` and `route_bench` are red on this box at the base commit too
  (`field 1/4-res 2D raster` 21.62 ms against a 16 ms budget, `whole trace`
  97.9 ms against 60). They are not in `KNOWN_RED` and they fail for machine

## the plan

1. **The url says what the page is.** One editor (`syncSeedUrl`) writes seed,
   mode, universe, roster, bot, player, cheat and rate from the live state and
   drops every param it does not read. The boot reads the two new ones
   (`universe=`, `players=`), so a reload of the address runs the same map.
2. **A refused opening costs what a fair one costs.** Measure where the 13 s
   went, keep the fairness law, cut only the part of the walk that cannot
   change the answer.
3. Pin both with tests: the address rule in `saved_map_test`, the miss bound in
   `career_start_fairness` (`REFUSED_SEED=10`).

## steps

1. **url** — `gui_world.js` `syncSeedUrl` writes `universe=` (the live subset,
   or `game.planetUniverse` for a run that is about to start) and `players=`
   (the career roster only: other rules field their rivals from the level).
   `gui_share.js` reads both at boot (`readUniverseParam`, `readPlayersParam`);
   out of range is refused like a bad seed. `gui_maps.js` `loadSavedMapParam`
   now also takes `bot=` and `cheat=` from the address, as a seed link does;
   the record alone decides the universe and the roster, because the seats were
   proven on that world for that many companies — and the address written after
   the boot says so.
2. **`?map=` is a way in, not a state** — the two lines of reasoning 0.7.9 left
   open now meet: `?map=3` boots, then the address becomes
   `?seed=4&mode=career&universe=26&players=3&rate=1`, and reloading *that* is
   the same map with no search (the seat plan is cached per seed/universe/
   seats). `map=` is always dropped, so the first refresh never carries a local
   file into an address that no longer depends on it.
3. **the search** — measured, then bounded. `OPENING_EXACT_MISS = 48`: a walk
   that has found no accepted set in three batches stops there. A fair world is
   untouched (its accept came at index 0-14 for 24 of seeds 1-40 and at
   17,18,19,27,28,34 for the rest; never past batch 3). A refused world stops
   at set 48 of 220 and keeps the best set the ranking put in front of it.
4. **tests** — `saved_map_test`: the address after a boot names no `map=`, names
   the universe and roster the run has, and reloads to the same run (four boots:
   the default map, a map at another clock, a 26-world map, a 2-seat map).
   `career_start_fairness`: `REFUSED_SEED=10` seats a refused world in under
   12 s (it was 13 s before, 5 s now, so the bound is watched).
5. **docs** — `experiments/README.md` (the address rule, the ~5 s boot),
   `AGENTS.md` (the seat-search row), `findings-pitfalls-skills.md` (the 13-15 s
   figure), `experiments/logs/0.7.10-seat-search.txt` (the measurements).

## the search: what the measurement said

Full table and probes in `experiments/logs/0.7.10-seat-search.txt`.

- A career boot on a fair seed: ~0.3 s for the single-home table, ~2 s for the
  pair ranking, then one batch of 16 exact sets (3 s in total). On a refused
  seed (10, 61, 73, 98) the same start, then **all 220 exact sets**: 13 s.
  There is no slow function to find — one exact set is 66 ms warm.
- The accepted set is in the first three batches whenever a fair set exists.
  After that the walk is not looking for fairness any more; it is picking the
  least-bad *unfair* plan for a world that has none.
- Rules that were tried and rejected, each for a measured reason: an unpinned
  site ceiling (0.50-0.95 on the refused seeds, above the 0.55 band, so it can
  refuse nothing); sites "exitable from another home" (true on refused worlds
  too — only the exact pinned trace sees what kills them); all 286 sets scored
  from the single-home table (accepts nothing on any of 8 worlds); a
  starter-exitable-site count (catches 10 and 73, not 61 or 98).

## numbers

Wall seconds, 2-core sandbox, `node` v22.22.3, main realm (no vm).

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| career boot, seed 42 (fair) | 3.0 | 3.1 |
| career boot, seed 1 (fair, accepts in batch 2) | 2.6 | 2.7 |
| career boot, seed 10 (refused) | 13.3 | **5.0** |
| career boot, seed 61 (refused) | 12.1 | **4.2** |
| career boot, seed 73 (refused) | 12.8 | **4.7** |
| career boot, seed 98 (refused) | 11.2 | **4.0** |
| `run_fast --slow` | 303 (0.7.9 table) | 338 (this box, one pass) |
| `player_growth_test` | 42.3 | 36.2 |
| `career_start_seat_test` | 8.3 | 8.2 |
| `make_saved_maps --check` | 21.4 | 23.0 |

The refused seeds now cost 1.5-2 batches more than a fair one, which is the
difference in the answer, not in the search. What is left of a refused boot is
the table (~0.3 s) and the pair ranking (~2 s): a set no batch accepts cannot
skip the ranking that orders the walk, so ~2.5 s is the floor for either case.

The changes bite, on scratch copies with one mutation each:

| mutation | caught by |
|---|---|
| `syncSeedUrl` does not write `universe=` | `saved_map_test`: "the address names the universe the run has" |
| `players=` dropped from the address | `saved_map_test`: "the address names the roster the run has" |

Measured, not guessed:

- whole-plan diff, base commit against this tree, 30 combinations of shapes
  (13/2, 13/3, 26/3, 26/4, 8/2) and seeds (1, 3, 7, 11, 24, 42): **identical
  seats, character for character**, plus 48 worlds / 3 seats on seeds 1 and 7
  (the `pickFairHq` path, not the walk). Every seed 1-60 at 13/3 is a fair plan
  or a refusal before and after.
- a world where the fairness law cannot be met is a *refusal either way*, and
  there the plan moves: the bound stops the walk before the best of all 220
  unfair sets. Of the refused worlds this was checked on, 46 and 61 keep the
  seats the full walk gave them, and 10 (7,4,8 → 5,4,9), 73 (12,4,9 → 11,4,7)
  and 98 (9,6,4 → 12,8,7) change. Every seat is still distinct and G 1.00, and
  the plan is still the best set the ranking's head produced — `saved_maps.js`
  has no record for a refused world, so no fixture moves.
- the address, reloaded from the url each boot wrote: `?map=0`,
  `?map=0&rate=0.5&you=bot`, a 26-world record and a 2-seat record all reload
  to the same seed, universe, roster, seats, HQ and clock. `?map=3` boots 26
  worlds in 0.2 s and its address boots the same 26 worlds in 3 s (a search,
  not a map, because the cache is per process).
- `?mode=lab&seed=7&universe=8` boots 8 worlds and writes
  `?mode=lab&seed=7&universe=8`; `?mode=career&seed=42&players=4` boots four
  seats and writes `players=4`; `universe=99` and `players=0` are refused like
  a bad seed (the lab fallback, then the address says what the page is).

## next step

The two red assertions in `gravity_view_test` (is the seed-61 raster or the
expectation wrong; `+0.46` or `+0.47`) are still the last red file in the slow
tier, and 0.7.9 asked the same question. Beside them, the two wall-clock
budgets (`gravity_bench` field raster, `route_bench` whole trace) fail on a
clean main on this box: either they are budgets with no reference machine or
they need to be measured against a fixed workload. A tier where a red line is
always news needs both questions answered.

On the search itself, one measurement is left: a refused world still pays the
~2 s pair ranking before it can be refused, and the ranking is only needed to
order a walk that finds nothing. Deciding whether a refused world may keep a
plan that was a fair tie-break of the head (which is what it keeps now) or must
be the best of all 220 — and so whether the 2 s is worth spending — is the
question a cheaper refusal has to answer first.
