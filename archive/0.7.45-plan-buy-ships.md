# 0.7.45 — Automatic company fleet

Source: `../drafts/career-economy-buy-ships.md`.

## Company, not player slots

An automatic ship is a vessel inside YOU's company career. Buying one must
not add a company, scoreboard player slot, planet, start gift or science
research payment. `MAX_PLAYERS`, `MAX_RIVALS`, `PLAYER_INDEX`, `slotCount` and
fair-start seat composition are unchanged. There is no fleet size cap.

The company shares its purse, pads, launch network, owned parts, science,
prototypes and market. Each vessel owns its fit, fuel, condition, location,
flight ledger, simulation, launch queue and controller schedule. Later
refitting one vessel does not mutate another vessel's fitted devices.
The colony kit is per-vessel and is not copied by a purchase.

## Purchase and settings

- `#expFleet` is checked by default. Its standing page choice mirrors into
  `career.fleetUnlocked` on a new company; a full saved run restores its
  saved choice. It needs no URL flag.
- Buying requires an open career, a landed flagship on an in-play inhabited
  planet, and at least 30 cr. Refuse in space, on an empty world, during
  flight, arrest, evacuation, a pending flagship launch or eased wait.
- An HQ is not required: a Capital start can buy there, and a space start
  can buy after landing on an inhabited world.
- Deduct `price(career, FLEET_COST)` (30 cr, free under communism), add the
  amount to `career.fleetSpent`, and copy the flagship's current fit into
  a fresh full-condition vessel with no fuel, cargo or colony kit.
- The vessel and its simulation start on the purchase planet, not the HQ.
  Give it a monotonically increasing ID and a staggered first launch.
- Turning buying off does not remove existing ships or switch their pilots
  off. Their individual bot checkboxes are independent.
- The page exposes buying only for YOU's company. A rival being inspected
  must not steal the fleet controller or create unstepped fleet records.

## Context swapping

`career.fleet` holds vessel records. `career.contextBase` is a reusable
scratch record. For each synchronous vessel step:

1. Save the flagship context once with `saveCareerContext`.
2. `loadCareerContext` installs the vessel's location, ship, contract-free
   task board, flight fields, evacuation/arrest state and ledger visit mask.
   Set `career.fleetShip` to identify the borrowed context.
3. Run the shared flight/controller functions against that context.
4. `storeCareerContext` writes it back to the vessel.
5. After all vessels, `restoreCareerContext` restores the flagship and clears
   `career.fleetShip`.

The company ledger object and purse never change identity. The ledger's
per-flight fields (`fee`, `shotGain`, `shotVisits`, `lastPlanet`, `reports`,
`inside`) do travel with each vessel. Separate visit masks prevent one ship
from resetting another's harvest state.

## Controller and wear

Run `stepFleet` after the company's normal advance and rival stepping.
Each vessel gets its own `GravitySim`, current world time/rate, queue,
ready-step schedule, burn state and shot list. Its geometry, mask, field and
launch network derive from the same world as the flagship.

- Reuse the existing career planner, physical departure checks, fuel policy,
  queue reservations, flight burns, harvest and landing rules.
- Set vessel `wearScale = 2`; only call `careerAdvanceShip` from this loop.
  The company advances its pads exactly once, regardless of fleet size.
- The between-flight shop only repairs and refuels. It never builds pads,
  researches, buys gear, accepts contracts, enters races or founds colonies.
- The shared purse pays launch fees, fuel, repair and evacuation. An
  unrecoverable lost ship can be decommissioned without ending the company.
- A per-vessel `automatic` flag defaults true. Off cancels its queued launch
  and releases reserved energy; an existing flight or tow still advances,
  but no new plan, impulse or between-flight shopping is initiated. On
  resumes the controller without restarting the company.
- Queue cancellation outside a context swap must not clear the flagship's
  planned landing. Inside a swap it clears both the record and live context.

## Per-ship net profit and logs

Each record has `purchaseCost` and cumulative `profit`, initially minus the
actual purchase price. At load, remember the shared purse; at store, add its
change during this vessel's synchronous work. This captures visit income,
launch fees, refuelling, repairs and evacuation without duplicating those
transaction rules. Flagship transactions and other vessels' steps do not
belong to this vessel. Company-wide passive pad income is not assigned to a
ship. Profit is a readout, not a second purse or a company ledger entry.

Keep a bounded recent decision log (the existing `GravityBot.TRACE_MAX`
limit) per ship. Prefix fleet decisions with their ship ID in company logs.
Fleet events must not masquerade as flagship fire/burn events in action
recordings. Record purchase, buying-setting and automatic-pilot changes as
career actions.

## Rows and prediction

Place `#scoreFleetRows` immediately below YOU, backed by a reusable
`#scoreFleetTemplate`. Grow DOM rows only when needed; reuse them by index,
and bind events to the record currently occupying the row.

Each row shows:

- `YOU · ship N`, owner colour, a bot checkbox and a recent-log copy button;
- cumulative signed net profit in cr, with a tooltip explaining purchase
  and running-cost inclusion;
- location, condition and ready/flying/parking/parked/evac/lost status;
- inspection selection by click, Enter or Space, with shot count and fuel.

The flagship remains the manually controlled ship. Selecting a fleet row
inspects it rather than changing company slots or handing it the keyboard.
The bot checkbox means automatic/parked, not automatic/manual.

Draw each ship's marker and ID. Predicted fleet paths are strictly
hover/press/focus reveals, independently of the global predicted checkbox
and HQ radar. Canvas hit testing includes flying fleet ships without adding
them to `radarShips` (that API remains a company/rival reveal). Reuse the
radar path/landing-ghost renderer and the vessel's memoised prediction.
Allocate prediction scratch at setup; do no prediction work for hidden fleet
paths. Memo invalidation follows live steps, field time and velocity/burns.

## Full save / restore

Pack each vessel's data separately from its simulation flight state. Rebuild
live company/world/network references before unpacking; do not serialize
career back-references, shared task arrays or prediction scratch.

Persist profit, purchase cost, pilot flag, recent log, shot/burn history and
vessel ID. Preserve the company sequence even when the highest-ID vessel
has retired. Save a queued launch without its pad object and rebind that pad
after loading; the restored reservation must have its queue and release only
once. Reattach the rebuilt fleet to YOU's controller, including an empty
fleet (no stale alias). Mirror the saved buying preference into the page
checkbox before refreshing controls.

## Validation contract

`experiments/career_fleet_test.js` covers purchase price/gates, inhabited
non-HQ and no-HQ starts, shared fit/property/science, context restoration,
wear ×2, concurrent flights, one pad advance, ten unlimited purchases,
founding/radar isolation, per-ship accounting and log attribution,
park/resume/cancel behaviour, lazy hover/press prediction, page wiring and
full-save restoration of profit, pilot settings, IDs and queued charge.

Run saved-map, wear and radar regressions as well as the fast tier. Changes
to page plumbing require one DOM smoke run. Check touched scripts with
`node --check` and the patch with `git diff --check`.

## Scope boundary

No rival fleets, resale, insurance or fleet-vs-fleet contracts. Manual fleet
takeover needs a separate per-vessel UI/control design: it must preserve the
flagship's contracts, queues, flight ledger, replay identity and pause state
without making a vessel a new company/player slot. The automatic/parked
checkbox is not a substitute for that manual-control design.
