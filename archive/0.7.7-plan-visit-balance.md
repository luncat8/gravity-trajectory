# 0.7.7 plan — visits worth taking: a higher cap, a flat bribe, a lighter middle

Standalone spec: a fork can rebuild this from this file alone. It prices three
laws (visit cap, bribe, seeded gravity) and the forecast that keeps every
readout honest about wear. Source: `drafts/politics-quests.md` §3.2–3.3 and the
balance review of `archive/0.7.2-worklog.md` §5 (wear took 48 % of a rival's
income, so a visit had to be worth more, not the repair cheaper).

## 1. the three laws

### visit cap

	MONEY = { cost: 1, k: 0.00625, initSec: 40, cap: 4, purse0: 3 }

`planetProfitAtStep` keeps its shape, `cap·(1 − e^(−k·age/cap))`, so the **initial
slope is unchanged** (+0.00625 per flight second, +0.25 per 40 s) and only the
asymptote moves 3.00 → 4.00. A visit that waited long enough pays a quarter more
than the old ceiling; a quick hop pays what it always paid. Every derived number
follows the constant: the Capital's `−3` entry, the rebel planet's `4 ×` fly-by
ramp (ceiling `16.00`), the pill's rate text, the bench, the tests.

### bribe

	BRIBE_VALUE_COEF = 2
	bribeValue(value) = value > 0 ? value * BRIBE_VALUE_COEF : 0

The offer is **twice the visit value at every rot level** — the landing payout
plus what regrew since (half speed while any ship is landed, see
`advancePolitics`). Corruption governs the **arrest risk** and the rot the bribe
adds (`CORRUPTION_BRIBE_BASE + CORRUPTION_BRIBE_SCALE · money`), never the
multiplier. The old `rot/100 × 4` law was replaced because a clean world (mean
rot in the bench is ~11) paid ~0.44 × the visit: nobody had a reason to press it.

`bribeAmount` reads the **market**, not the ship: a dead observation device pays
nothing for a visit, and the same visit value is exactly what the rulers cash.
That is the only way to take a bribe on landing once the device is worn out
(there is no uninstall action; `go quiet` silences a planet for everyone and is a
different, political act).

### seeded gravity

	PLANET_GRAVITY_MIN = -1, MAX = 3.5, STEPS = 20 (0.05 grid, 91 values)
	GRAVITY_BIAS_START = 0.6
	GRAVITY_BIAS_END   = 2.8
	GRAVITY_BIAS_STRENGTH = 0.3

	u = hash01(seed, planet, SALT_GRAVITY)
	g = MIN + (u · 91 − 0.5) / 20
	if (START < g < END) g −= STRENGTH · sin²(π · (g − START) / (END − START))
	g = round(g · 20) / 20, clamped to the range, and `0` never becomes `−0`

One deterministic draw, one smooth bump subtracted from the middle of the
range: the low tail and the top tail keep their probability, the useful
`0.9…1.5` middle gains about a third and the heavy `1.8…2.5` middle loses about
a quarter (measured over 5 000 seeds × 26 planets, see §5). No new state, no
per-planet table, and `planetGravity(seed, planet)` stays a pure function.

## 2. what prices them

Wear and the device's death are priced by four pure forecasts in
`gravity_game.js`, so no readout, scan or rival can disagree with the flight:

	launchWear(career, speed)            EMC wear of one launch
	spaceWear(career, steps)             space wear of a step count
	impulseWear(career, dv)              Δv wear of one burn
	landingWear(career)                  touchdown wear
	forecastCondition(career, condition, steps, speed, dv, landed)
	forecastReportUntil(career, condition, speed, dv)
	forecastRepairReserve(career, arrival)      a full repair when arrival hits 0
	forecastWearCost(career, condition, steps, speed, dv, landed)

`forecastReportUntil` is the **exclusive flight step** at which the device
reaches 0 (`Infinity` when the space-wear knob is 0). Landing wear is charged
after the last harvest, so it never shortens reporting.

`estimateProfitLog(market, baseStep, planets, steps, count, ledger, reportUntil,
landing)` is the one visit-log walk:

- `reportUntil === undefined` → the live ledger decides (`visitReports`);
- a number → the walk stops reporting at that step, except for the Capital and
  A.R.F., whose flat price is not a report;
- an optional `{ body, value }` **landing scratch** receives the last value
  accrued on that body **whether or not the visit reported it** — the bribe
  cashes exactly that value.

## 3. the bribe in a bot's score

	careerBribeOffer(career, market, body, value) =
		career.bribeProne && body ≥ 0 && value > 0
		&& body is not the Capital, A.R.F. or a rebel planet
		&& the company does not own the rail there
		&& bribeValue(value) ≥ BRIBE_MIN
		&& (offer ≥ CAREER_BRIBE_PAY || corruption[nody] ≥ CAREER_BRIBE_ROT)
		? bribeValue(value) : 0

The offer is added to the landing's **cash** in `careerScoreFacts` (so it also
widens the exit the bot can afford) and shown in the decision log as
`pay +3.26 (bribe 1.44)`. Only a company with the persona prices it: the human's
helpers, aim readout and launch list never do, because the offer is the player's
to take or leave. The gates are the ones `careerBribe` applies when the ship
actually stands there, so the score cannot promise money the landing will refuse
(a rival queued on the pad is the one dynamic case the score does not model).

`careerEval.bribe` and `plan.bribe` carry it; `careerTakeBest`, `careerBurnFacts`,
`careerPriceBurn` and `copyCareerPlan` all copy it, so a stored shot and a fresh
trace price the same offer.

## 4. the experimental knob

`spaceWearScale` 0…3 multiplies **space** wear only (launch, landing and impulses
keep the law), the page exposes it as the `experimental` panel's `wear ×` slider,
and a career link carries it as `&ws=`. Absent or out of range → 1; `ws=0` is a
legal value and must survive the codec. `careerAdvance` charges the base share,
`careerWeatherWear` the weather's share of the same step.

## 5. what the numbers say

`career_gravity_bench STATS=1`, 12 seeds × 2 rivals, 400 s, `WEAR=1`, one code
path per row (see `experiments/logs/0.7.7-visit-balance.txt`):

| variant | gravity | cap | bribe | bribe priced | profit/company | property | pads | maintenance | share | mean rot | standing | bribes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.7.2 (logged) | uniform | 3 | rot×4 | no | 0.009/s | 4.52 | 50 | 5.46 | 48 % | 134 | 16.4 | 0 (0 %) |
| cap 3 | smooth | 3 | 2× | no | 0.011/s | 5.29 | 53 | 5.53 | 45 % | 127 | 15.0 | 2 (2 %) |
| cap 4 | smooth | 4 | 2× | no | 0.011/s | 5.11 | 52 | 5.29 | 43 % | 147 | 18.0 | 3 (3 %) |
| cap 4, old gravity | uniform | 4 | 2× | no | 0.014/s | 5.64 | 58 | 4.58 | 37 % | 148 | 18.7 | 2 (2 %) |
| cap 4 + bot bribe | smooth | 4 | 2× | yes | 0.012/s | 6.05 | 52 | 5.28 | 40 % | 149 | 18.2 | 7 (7 %) |

Two readings, both wanted by the next session:

- **the cap alone did not raise a rival's income.** The growth slope is
  unchanged and the bot is bound by flights, pads and energy, not by a planet's
  asymptote: cap 3 → 4 left profit at 0.011/s and only moved the passive-rot
  ramp, which is written in absolute money (`CORRUPTION_PROFIT_START 1.00` …
  `FULL 3.00`, the human's own table) so a 4.00 world spends more of its time
  above the full rate: mean rot 127 → 147, standing rot 15.0 → 18.0.
- **the lighter middle is a map change, not an income change.** On the same
  seeds the smoothed map gave fewer pads (58 → 52) and less income than the
  uniform one; the bot's site pricing reads the well's depth, so the band it
  prefers moved with the bias. The bias is kept for playability (more usable
  middle worlds), not as an income lever.

## scope boundary

No change to the passive-rot ramp, to `MONEY.k`, or to the Capital's `−3` entry
(which can overdraw a purse: a debt, not a bug this plan fixes). No new price on
the bribe beyond the two laws above. No fleet ships, no per-ship report flag
(that is `0.7.3` step 1), no space weather.
