# 0.7.29 · recharge a blocked career rail before paying for a way out

## Finding and fix

The [replay review, item 2](0.7.26-replay-review-report.md) called out draining a shared battery and paying a commercial fare or a tow instead of waiting for sunlight. The [previous pass](0.7.28-worklog.md) already checks ordinary recharge, preserves the three first-landing claims and permits paying return trips. Its three-bot replay still shows a related failure: R1 had a working EMC-2 and BAT-2 on P4, but after its P4 return, SP-1 could not recharge an interplanetary departure within the bot's patience. It spent its remaining **4.94** on evacuation rather than upgrading the panel for **2.15**.

- Before paying for fallback travel, forecast unreserved kWh at each remaining retry (at most four). Use the actor's actual retry interval, sunlight, battery capacity and per-step leakage, including leakage of newly generated charge. Test snapped exit trajectories at the resulting speed cap and current cargo mass. A higher cap alone is not proof of an exit.
- Prefer ordinary sunlight, including on a host's rail. On the **first** failed recharge decision on an **owned** rail, consider the next known panel. Buy only if the current rail has no sampled exit now or at the retries, the panel unlocks one within the same window, the panel costs less than the recovery fare (the minimum commercial fare at HQ), and one cheap launch stays in the purse. Retry on the normal clock. No kWh are created, reserved or spent by the forecast.
- No change to the market, physics, seat search or profitable-return rule. A broader search for future tours and earlier speculative panel purchases did not consistently pay for themselves and were not included.
