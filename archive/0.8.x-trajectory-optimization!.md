### 1. What Was Taking the Most Time During the Scans?

Profiling showed **four stacked bottlenecks** (surprisingly, the FFT gravity lookup itself was **not** the main culprit):

1. **Cross-frame cache thrashing when unpaused (~3.4–4.1 s freeze — ~85% of unpaused wall time)**
   - `helperTick` (and hover pricing) called `bot.prepareCareerExitProbe(cargoSim)`, while rival bot planning inside `stepGame` called `bot.prepareCareerExitProbe(bot.probe)` on the same frame.
   - Because `cargoSim !== bot.probe` and each held its own `Float64Array` `strength` buffer (with identical values), `prepareCareerExitProbe` incremented `departureFieldVersion` **on every frame**, wiping `careerExitCache` and `careerReachCache` to a 0% cross-frame hit rate and severing `exitProbe`'s shared `periodicSource_` snapshot. That forced `stepGame` to run **2,879 `predictFast` traces (3.87M physics steps = ~3,240 ms CPU)** during a single scan (`careerReach` = 2,370 ms, `careerFanShot` = 538 ms, `careerExitSpeed` = 525 ms).
2. **Per-step planet proximity checks (`stepInsideAt` + `impact`) — ~65–75% of integration time**
   - On a static FFT grid, sampling gravity (`snap.sample`) took only **~70 ns/step (~15%)**, whereas `stepInsideAt` + `impact` took **~1,150 ns/step (~70%)**.
   - The old periodic spatial index was disabled for `K ≤ 48`, indexed all `192` capacity slots (even inactive ones), and scanned a `5×5 = 25`-cell neighborhood of JS `Array` buckets **twice per step** with `minImage` `Math.round` calls — even though ~65% of the torus is outside every port ellipse and ~97% is outside every impact disc.
   - Worse, `legProbe` in `game_departure.js` copied planet arrays manually without ever calling `buildPlanetIndex()`, so `legProbe.planetIndexValid` was **always `false`** (192-body brute-force loops on every step of two-leg/departure checks).
3. **Aggressive per-frame slice throttling (`HELPER_BUDGET_MS = 3` ms + `Math.min(left, 2)`)**
   - Even when paused with idle CPU, `helperTick` capped each `routeStep` call to `2 ms` inside a `3 ms` frame budget and broke out of the frame loop whenever a candidate needed another slice — stretching 34–38 candidates across **50–121 animation frames** (`1–4 seconds` depending on display/frame rate).
4. **Redundant per-candidate helper work**
   - `helperScore` computed `traceMoney(...)` (including `careerDeparture` solvency + two-leg valuation) and `saveTrace` immediately computed `traceMoney(...)` a **second time** for the same candidate.
   - `helperAttachWorld()` rebuilt `helperProbe`'s planet index on every button press, and `helperProbe` wrote/compacted up to `28,900` unused `predX`/`predY` points per candidate.

#### What was changed to fix them
- **`gravity_sim.js`**: Replaced the 25-bucket scan with two flat zero-allocation `64×64` torus CSR tables (`impCellStart`/`impCellIds` for impact discs and `sysCellStart`/`sysCellIds` for active system ellipses, built for all periodic `planetCount > 0`). Open-space steps now exit in 3 typed-array reads with zero loops and zero `minImage` calls. Added optional headless `storePath = false` support to `routeBegin`.
- **`gravity_fft.js`**: Fused the twin `gather(ax, ...)` / `gather(ay, ...)` calls inside `sample()` and replaced `%` index wraps with power-of-two bitmasks (`& (gridX - 1)`), preserving exact IEEE-754 evaluation order.
- **`bot_career_assets.js` & `game_departure.js`**: Fixed `prepareCareerExitProbe` and `prepareLegProbe` to compare actual planet/mask/strength/coordinate state instead of `sourceSim` object identity, resolve `periodicSource_` to its root sim so shared FFT snapshots are never dropped, cache `careerExitFieldStamp()` while static, and build `legProbe`'s spatial index.
- **`gui_tables.js`, `gui_helper.js`, `gui_book.js`**: Raised `HELPER_BUDGET_MS` from `3` to `12` ms, passed remaining `left` directly to `routeStep(left)`, ran `helperProbe` with `storePath = false`, reused `helperScore`'s computed `careerMoney` inside `saveTrace`, and skipped redundant `helperAttachWorld` rebuilds when world geometry/mask are unchanged.

### 2. Why Did Scans Run Fast on Non-FFT (Classic) Maps?

Classic maps avoided almost all of that work for four reasons:
1. **Open boundaries terminate misses early (`END_EXIT`)**: Classic is `1600×1000` with open borders (`outside() === true` past a 35% margin), so missed shots fly off-screen and stop after ~2,000–4,000 steps. Periodic FFT is a **borderless `3200×2000` torus** (`outside() === false`, 4× area) with `PERIODIC_STRENGTH = 0.5`, so any shot that misses a planet wraps around for the full **`CAP_SECONDS = 120 s` (`14,400` steps)** — ~6× more total steps per scan on sparse rosters.
2. **Whole-cluster AABB fast-skip (`beyondPlanets`)**: Classic skips `stepInsideAt` and `impact` in 4 comparisons whenever the ship leaves the planet bounding box; a torus has no outer void, so every step had to run proximity checks.
3. **16 planets vs. 64–192 capacity**: Classic loops over `16` planets (~0.47 µs/step total), whereas periodic worlds allocate `192` slots (`64` active by default) and paid `minImage` `Math.round` wraps in 25-bucket scans.
4. **Far fewer `careerExitSpeed` / `careerReach` steps**: When rival bots or departure checks missed on Classic, those probes exited quickly too rather than integrating 14,400 steps per bearing.

### 3. What "Resolution" Was Referred To — Does It Need to Be Large or Is Scaled Fine?

In the `0.8.x` notes/plans, "resolution" refers to **two distinct things**:

1. **The FFT Poisson solver grid (`512×256` on the `3200×2000` torus, `gravity_fft.js`)**
   - Controlled by `CELL_MAX_PX = 10` (`gridFor(3200, 2000)` picks powers of two with cell size `≤ 10 px` → **`512×256`**, i.e. `6.25 × 7.81 px` cells). When the periodic world was doubled from `1600×1000` to `3200×2000` in `0.8.0`, the grid scaled from `256×256` to `512×256` to keep cell size below `10 px`.
2. **The visual gravity-field / potential backdrop canvas (`gravity_field.js`)**
   - Uses `LOW_DIV = 4` (quarter-resolution CPU raster upscaled smoothly to the viewport, or sampling the `512×256` periodic grid; `0.8.11` adds full-pixel WebGL shaders). **This visual backdrop never runs during helper scans and has zero impact on scan speed.**

#### Does the FFT solver grid need to be `512×256`, or is a smaller/scaled grid fine?

Because trajectories sample the pre-solved grid via **O(1) bilinear interpolation** (`4` cells per step regardless of grid size), **grid resolution has virtually zero effect on per-step trajectory or scan speed** — it only affects the **one-time static `GravityFFT.solve()` cost** (when loading a map or changing planet strengths) and **memory**:

| FFT Grid (`3200×2000`) | Cell Size (`px`) | Cells per `SOFT = 60 px` | Static `solve()` | Per-step `sample()` | Snapshot RAM | Mean Force Error vs. Plummer Oracle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **`512×256` (current)** | `6.25 × 7.81` | `9.6 × 7.7` | `~38.0 ms` | `~45–70 ns` | `3,072 KiB` | `0.86%` (`≥2ε`) / `0.69%` (`<2ε`) |
| **`256×256`** | `12.50 × 7.81` | `4.8 × 7.7` | `~17.8 ms` (**2.1× faster**) | `~45–80 ns` | `1,536 KiB` | `1.07%` (`≥2ε`) / `1.26%` (`<2ε`) |
| **`256×128`** | `12.50 × 15.63` | `4.8 × 3.8` | `~8.2 ms` (**4.6× faster**) | `~50–100 ns` | `768 KiB` | `1.50%` (`≥2ε`) / `2.06%` (`<2ε`) |
| **`128×128`** | `25.00 × 15.63` | `2.4 × 3.8` | `~3.9 ms` | `~50–100 ns` | `384 KiB` | `1.93%` (`≥2ε`) / `4.33%` (`<2ε`, coarse near wells) |

**Takeaway:**
- **For scan speed:** Shrinking `512×256` wouldn't speed up per-step tracing because `sample()` is O(1) (`~50 ns`) at every grid size; keeping **`512×256`** preserves maximum near-well fidelity and existing `periodic_gui_test.js` contracts at zero per-step penalty.
- **If you ever want faster live strength-slider / colony-founding re-solves or half the snapshot memory:** Because Plummer softening is quite wide (`SOFT = 60 px`), **`256×256`** still puts ~5–8 grid cells across every softening core (~1.1% force difference, visually and gameplay-indistinguishable) while cutting `solve()` from `38 ms` to `18 ms` and RAM in half (`1.5 MiB`). **`128×128`** (`25 px` cells, only 2.4 cells across `SOFT`) starts introducing ~4.3% near-well anisotropy and is best avoided for physics, while `LOW_DIV = 4` scaled rendering remains completely fine for the visual background.
