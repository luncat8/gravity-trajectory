# 0.7.28 · career bot harvesting and launch choices

## What changed

The [0.7.26 review](0.7.26-replay-review-report.md) identified unaffordable travel and missed first claims. The 0.7.27 control already buys an EMC on every bot's first landing on map 10. Its remaining problem was especially visible at P11: YOU made three flights, then spent the final ~706 game seconds there. A return could earn flyby money after wear and still be refused as a bounce.

- A same-pad return gets no pad bonus and must pay for its launch fee, wear and any burn fuel. A profitable excursion is allowed; an empty or money-losing bounce doesn't compete with useful departures. A temporary commercial rail is priced on the outgoing shot only: the landing forecast sees the natural rail again.
- The launch book still skips tracing for a strong entry, but only if the live cash forecast covers wear, launch and exit costs **and** leaves one cheap launch fee. A high pad value or speculative pickup credit alone no longer ends the search. Normal and hard refine up to two or three coarse aims, including promising multi-visit traces cut short by the 45 s pass. The bounded refinement can look out to 90 or 120 s respectively, without extending the whole first fan or replacing a funded first claim.
- Bots skip the exact snapped aim of another queued or coasting ship from the same planet. Nearby routes remain available; the existing destination discount still handles different routes that converge. A bot on a recharging rail will wait rather than buy a cash-losing commercial ticket that doesn't advance a contract or fund a claim, even after its ordinary sit allowance runs out, provided the projected charge buys an affordable exit.
- A directed flight must actually advance the contract before its deadline. Fragile cargo caps the launch speed. A genuine reposition is still allowed, but if the relaxed plan only returns to the same pad or reaches the goal too late, the bot drops the job, re-prices at dry mass, and lets the board cool down.

## Wider check

In a separate matched run with an idle first seat and two normal rivals (`SEEDS=1-4 SECONDS=1004 STATS=1 LOG=0 node experiments/career_gravity_bench.js`), rival launches rose **101 → 113**, pads **39 → 41**, and mean distance per game second **26.3 → 37.9 px/s**. No rival finished with zero launches. Mean profit per rival per game second improved on three seeds but fell overall (**0.026 → 0.023**): on seed 1, R1 spent the later game escaping two deep-well landings and was still in its second evacuation at the cutoff. Mean maintenance rose from 28% to 33% of income. The policy improves activity more consistently than profitability.
