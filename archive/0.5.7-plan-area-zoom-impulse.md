# 0.5.7 plan — play area, zoom frames, bot impulse

Three requests in one change set; they meet in the same two questions: where a
flight may go, and what the controller does with the drive it has.

## 1. the play area is 1.5× the planet field

Today the sim ends a flight 12 px outside the 1600×1000 planet field, so the
framed canvas *is* the whole game and a shot that misses everything is lost at
the edge of the screen.

The play area becomes the planet field grown by a quarter of itself on every
side — 2400×1500, i.e. 1.5× in W and H, centred on the field. Planets stay
exactly where they were placed, so seeds, orbits and the trade world are
unchanged; only the edge a flight may reach moves out (x −400…2000,
y −250…1250).

One setting, one owner: `orbit_world.js` keeps `AREA_SCALE = 1.5` and derives
`AREA_W`, `AREA_H`, `AREA_PAD_X`, `AREA_PAD_Y`. `gravity_sim.js` derives its
bounds from that ratio, so the live flight, every prediction, every bot probe
and every bench end a flight at the same edge without carrying a copy.
`orbit_sim.js` re-exports the four numbers for the page.

Consequences to expect and to watch in the benches: `END_EXIT` gets rarer,
route plans get longer (more distance before the edge), and a career shot that
used to be lost in space may now reach a system.

## 2. zoom frames: `main` and `full`

The renderer's camera (the shared `view` object in `orbit_view.js`) gains a
frame:

- `main` — the planet field, exactly the old framing (what the player knows);
- `full` — the whole play area, so a ship that left the framed canvas is still
  on screen.

`resize()` fits the chosen box instead of the world, so both canvases (trade
and gravity overlay) and the field shader follow without a second code path.
The backdrop paints the area, then the field on top of it, with both edges
drawn: the field edge bright, the area edge faint. The switch is a radio row in
the `view` group plus `z`; `GravityScene.frame` / `setFrame` expose it and the
URL keeps no state (it is a camera, not a rule).

## 3. the bot uses the impulse drive

`careerBurnPlan` already existed but nothing called it: rivals and the page's
own bot flew every shot ballistically, and `careerUseResearch` (the switch that
fits an engine at HQ) was off in the page, so no bot company ever had a drive.
Two things are wrong with the old search for this use:

- it is a dense full-circle fan at three Δv rungs (≈100 full traces), too
  expensive to run inside a flight;
- its "target" angles sit within ±8° of the contract bearing, while a rescue
  needs the whole quadrant.

The drive's single burn is small (thrust × `BURN_SEC` / wet mass ≈ 10–30 px/s),
so the policy is:

- **one early nudge** at 0.2 s of flight, when it clearly beats coasting by
  `careerBurnGain` (`2` model money, measured on the two research worlds below;
  the landing score is partly an estimate of what a pad earns later, so a small
  modeled gain is noise): the burn is a fine aim correction that re-targets the
  whole flight;
- **rescues** afterwards, re-checked once a second: if the coast would leave
  the area, time out or miss the active contract, any landing is better than a
  lost ship (an evacuation costs `6.00`);
- at most two burns per flight, never in the last 0.1 s, and only with an
  engine and fuel (`careerFlightBurn` self-resets on a new launch);
- the search is two-stage: a 12-angle ring at two Δv rungs plus the contract
  bearing and its opposite, then a fine fan around the winner at both rungs —
  ≈40 traces, cheaper *and* sharper than the old fan.

The page owns a YOU-as-bot flight, so the controller only *plans*: `gui_game`
applies the burn and records it in the live shot, so a replay re-flies it at
the same step. Rival companies apply their own burns in `stepCareer`. Fuel is
topped up at every launch (`refuelShip` in `careerBeginFlight`), so the only
cost of a burn is the mass of the tank that was bought anyway.

## 4. the potential view paints the heavy spread

The potential backdrop maps `φ` to colour — blue where it is negative (a well),
green where it is positive (a repulsive hill) — and the legend already promised
red for heavy ground (`g ≥ +2`), but nothing painted it: a heavy planet looked
exactly like a light one away from its rim.

The same fragment pass already accumulates the pull `g` for the field view, so
the red wash costs one `dot` and one mix there: it starts at the legend's
`g² = 4` and is solid at `g² = 64` (a pull of 8 px/s²), mixed over the potential
colour at `0.65`. The result reads as a halo whose size *is* the heavy planet's
reach; the blue/green sign stays visible past that halo because the wash fades
out before the potential gets shallow.

The 1/4-resolution 2D fallback and the `samplePotential` reference take the same
pull: the potential row now accumulates the pull beside `φ` (two extra multiply-
adds per body per pixel on the fallback path only) and the reference signature is
`samplePotential(phi, g2, out)`, so the shader, the raster and the tests agree.

## validation

- `career_bot_bench` flies the same seeds with `NOBURN=1` (coast) and with the
  policy (`BURN_GAIN=n` tunes the nudge margin); the worklog records what the
  drive is worth on the two research worlds.

  edge, not at the field edge.

  area, the field stays centred, `z` toggles.

  red-dominant, a shallow pull keeps the blue/green sign, and the fallback

  `career_bench`, `career_gravity_bench`, `colony_bench`, the balance-lab tests
