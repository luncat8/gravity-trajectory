# 0.5.8 plan — price a career launch as a flight with one burn

Standalone spec: a fork can rebuild this from this file alone.

## Goal

A career company that fitted an impulse engine should choose and score its
launch shot by the landing its flight will actually reach, not by the ballistic
landing. `careerNextShot` (shared by the rival companies and by the page's own
bot company) searches a fan of ballistic traces and queues one shot; the flying
controller then nudges that shot 0.2 s into the flight with `careerBurnPlan`.
The queue therefore prices a different flight from the one it fires: a shot
that looks poor ballistically is sat out even though the drive could turn it
into a paying landing, and a shot whose burn reaches a new system is never
preferred over a slightly better ballistic one.

Close the gap without adding a second search policy: the plan keeps its
ballistic fan, then re-prices a bounded shortlist of the fan's best shots with
the *same* `careerBurnPlan` the flight uses, at the *same* step, with the
launch's fee, battery wait and the burn's fuel in the price. The queued shot is
then the best burn-priced one, and its score describes the landing the drive can
reach.

## The defect, measured

`career_gravity_bench.js` (12 seeds, 400 s, 24 rival companies) reports
167 flights / 82 pads / mean reach 4.04 and **zero** burns: the rivals never
fit an engine inside the bench's horizon, so that bench alone cannot show the
effect of launch pricing. `career_bot_bench.js`'s research careers do fit one
(14–15 burns per career), but they call `careerPlan` directly, bypassing
`careerNextShot`. Both benches need an engine-fitted, `careerNextShot`-driven
case before any A/B number means anything.

Two smaller inconsistencies the same change fixes:

- a burn search scores a landing with `fee = 0` (correct mid-flight, wrong
  before the launch fee is paid), so a pre-launch price must pass the fee into
  the solvency gate;
- `careerBeginFlight` refills the tank, so a pre-launch price must model a full
  tank, not the fuel left over from the last flight.

## The change

`gravity_bot.js`:

1. **Shortlist.** `careerPlan` records the best `CAREER_BURN_PRICE_TOP = 3`
   finite candidates (score, angle units, speed units, book flag) in
   preallocated typed arrays, over all fan passes of one `careerNextShot`
   (normal, fallback, commercial). No extra traces for this.
2. **One scratch flight.** `careerPriceBurn` mirrors the live sim's field,
   planet mask and scales into a dedicated `GravitySim`, sets its ship to the
   career's (with a full tank for the duration of the search), launches each
   shortlisted shot, steps it to `CAREER_BURN_AT` and calls the existing
   `careerBurnPlan(..., rescueOnly = false, fee)`. A shot that lands before the
   nudge window is skipped, not priced.
3. **Price.** For a burn plan, `score = burn.score − fee − energy·wait·cost`
   (`burn.score` already subtracts `fuelKg · CAREER_BURN_FUEL_COST`). The best
   score must beat the ballistic winner by `CAREER_BURN_PRICE_MARGIN`. `fee`
   is passed into `careerBurnScore` → `careerScoreFlight` →
   `careerScoreFacts` so the launch fee gates solvency; mid-flight calls keep
   `fee = 0`.
4. **Take it.** The winning burn's landing facts (`body`, `taskPay`,
   `padValue`, `exitCost`, `exitSource`, `builds`, fuel) replace the plan's
   ballistic ones, together with that candidate's `angleU`/`speedU`, `fee`,
   `energy`, `wait` and `fromBook`. The fuel cost is folded into `taskPay`, so
   the plan's money identity `score = taskPay + padValue − fee −
   energy·wait·cost` still holds. `burnGain` and `burnBody` are exposed for the
   decision log. When no burn pays, the plan stays exactly as the fan left it.
5. **No physics change.** The mid-flight controller still finds and applies its
   own burn at step 24 with the same margin; nothing is pre-applied to the
   ship, so replays, shots, fuel and the launch book are untouched.

`careerLaunch` and `tickCareerPlayer` print `burn +X.XX → P#` on the plan and
queue lines when a shot was burn-priced.

## Bounds

Only careers with an engine and a tank pay for the extra work; at most three
shortlisted shots, one 24-step trace each and the same ~40-trace burn fan the
flight runs, once per plan. The scratch sim is allocated once at construction
and reused; the shortlist and the winning snapshot are preallocated too.

## Validation

- `node experiments/career_bot_bench.js` — all checks OK, including the new
  burn-pricing ones; the research careers still complete with 26 pads.
- `node experiments/career_gravity_bench.js` — the unfitted baseline is
  bit-identical to 0.5.7 (no drive, no pricing).
- `ENGINE=1 node experiments/career_gravity_bench.js` A/B against
  `ENGINE=1 NOBURNPRICE=1` on the same seeds: report flights, earned, pads,
  dead pads, mean reach and burns. Improvement is not required by this plan —
  the drive is a purchase that adds mass, and a burn-priced shot may trade a
  cheap hop for a longer one — but the numbers must be measured, not guessed.
- `dom_smoke`, `career_bench`, `game_bench`, `bot_bench`, `gravity_bench`,
  `route_bench`, `career_departure_test`, `career_guidance_test`,
  `gravity_seed_test`, `balance_lab_test` unchanged and green.
- `node --check` on every touched `.js`.

## Scope boundary

This does not change the impulse policy, the burn search shape, the launch book,
the page's aim predictions, replay encoding, or what a human player sees when
aiming. Pricing the *whole* fan (not just its best few shots), multi-burn plans
and a page-side "burn-assisted" hint in the launch list are separate changes.
