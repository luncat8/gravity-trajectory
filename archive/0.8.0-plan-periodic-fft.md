# 0.8.0 Plan — Experimental Periodic FFT Torus (`&fft=1`)

> **Roadmap position (Start of `0.8.*` · Periodic FFT & large-roster foundation):** Placed after the classic bounded-map `0.7.*` plans (`0.7.44`–`0.7.45` and the shipped `0.7.47`–`0.7.49`) and immediately before `0.8.1-plan-max-distance-mode.md`, which builds directly on `0.8.0`'s `PLANETS = 192` / `MAX_BODIES = 192` capacity, 3-digit shot/end codec, non-shader field view, and radix-2 `GravityFFT` solver.

Standalone spec: a fork can rebuild this from this file alone. Source note:
`drafts/map.md` §gravity:

	add option periodical FFT gravity solver.
	if enabled then xy wrap map, 4x more default and max planets. probably 2x less gravity.

Depends on the Plummer force in `gravity_sim.js` (`MU`, `SOFT`), the 1600×1000
field in `orbit_world.js`, and the experimental panel (`g field`, `catapult`,
stay-pay). Space weather in the same draft is a different note and is out of
scope. Interacts with the shipped `0.7.47` seat search and composition,
`0.7.48` gifts, `0.7.49` radar and `0.7.51`–`0.7.52` view; §8 below is the

## 0. architecture decisions

1. **The FFT runs only on a static field.** `GravitySim` already carries
   `fieldStatic`: the planet field in career/lab/tour/money/route is static, while
   the optional `ports` source is a moving field of at most 32 bodies. Static
   planets solve once; moving ports keep the direct sum (now min-image) and never
   touch the grid. No per-step FFT, no 30 Hz solver.
2. **`route` is not periodic.** Its path-length score can reward repeated
   circulation without an escape boundary; a periodic version needs a separate
   scoring design. The checkbox is disabled (and `&rule=route&fft=1` rejected) instead
   of shipping a degenerate rule. `tour`, `money`, `career` and the lab use it.
3. **The planet cap and the array cap are two different numbers.** `PLANETS`
   becomes 192 always; the UI cap (`PLANET_UNIVERSE_MAX`) becomes a variable,
   48 or 192. Display counts use the active count; masked gameplay loops use
   the active extent, not storage capacity (§5). A 26-planet classic map must
   not read as 192.
4. **The replay end field grows to three digits.** `END_FORM` is `visited.steps`,
   and `visited` can be 104 or 192, not only two-digit counts. `SHOT_FORM`'s origin grows for the
   same reason.
5. **The periodic field view is CPU-rastered from the grid.** A WebGL1 float
   texture is an extension (`OES_texture_float`), and `file://` must not depend
   on one. A 256×256 offscreen canvas is recoloured on field/scale changes
   and blitted each frame. Moving ports use a wrapped CPU direct raster.

## problem

The force at a ship is a direct sum of at most 48 Plummer wells in an open
plane. A flight that leaves the 2400×1500 play area ends (`END_EXIT`); the
`full area` zoom exists only to show that empty margin. The WebGL field shader
loops the same 48 bodies as uniforms, so a denser map cannot even paint.

The note wants one option that changes three things together: the solver
(FFT on a torus), the topology (xy wrap, no margin), and the roster (4×
planets, gentler wells). Off, the open-plane numerical results and seeded
worlds are unchanged (extra array capacity and internal cache-key representation are not saved physics).

## 1. the option

One checkbox in the experimental panel, beside stay-pay:

	periodic FFT

- Off is the shipped game: open plane, `END_EXIT` at the area edge, 26 / 16 /
  48 planets, the body-loop shader, `full area` zoom.
- On is a different world, not a filter: the same seed with `&fft=1` is a
  torus of 104 (lab/tour/money) or 64 (career) planets, max 192, no margin,
  FFT gravity, half the per-body well.
- `route` is refused while the option is on: the rule's score is trajectory
  length and a torus has no edge. The checkbox is disabled in `route`; a link
  that carries both is rejected before it starts. The lab, `tour`, `money` and
  `career` keep it.
- The URL carries `&fft=1` the way it carries `&ht=1`. Replay links include
  it. It is not a `localStorage` pref — it is topology, and belongs with the
  seed.
- Toggling it rebuilds the world. In lab that is a field change (stale shots
  already say so). During a run it restarts the seed, like a mode switch:
  wrap mid-flight would teleport, and the roster size is part of the map.
- Locked at the recorded value while a replay runs, same as the two sliders.
- The experimental `g field` slider still multiplies every well on top. It is
  applied at sample time (`MU * gravityScale`), never deposited into the grid,
  so moving it does not rebuild or re-solve the field.

## 2. the force law

Keep the shipped 3D Plummer, evaluated in the plane:

	a = μ s r / (|r|² + ε²)^{3/2}
	φ = −μ s / sqrt(|r|² + ε²)

	μ = 400000, ε = SOFT = 60

Do **not** switch to 2D Poisson (log potential, 1/r force). That would be a
different game. The periodic option adds the torus, it does not change the
kernel.

The chosen force is **minimum-image** Plummer: one nearest image per body,
not the infinite periodic lattice sum. This is a deliberate game law; omitted
images are not uniformly negligible near the half-world cuts. The oracle is
an independent wrapped direct sum, never an Ewald sum or a 2D Poisson solve.
The force component normal to a half-world cut is discontinuous. CIC smooths
that jump over a few cells; treat this explicitly in accuracy tests (§Validation).

Use `d = sample - source` for convolution: `K_a(d) = -d / (|d|² + ε²)^(3/2)`
and `K_phi(d) = -1 / sqrt(|d|² + ε²)`. A positive source must attract a point
to its right toward the left; a negative source reverses it. For the direct
oracle, reduce each component to its nearest image; at exactly `±L/2`,
average the two equally near forces (that component is zero, the other keeps
`(L/2)²` in its denominator). The grid kernel uses the same tie convention,
including the complete Nyquist row/column. This makes the discrete force odd
and its self-force zero. Do not zero the whole row/column or remove that
squared distance when zeroing its tied component.

`PERIODIC_STRENGTH = 0.5` scales every deposited mass (the note's «probably
2× less gravity»). Half strength is an initial tuning choice, not a claim that four times as
many signed wells preserves the classic force distribution.
The `g field` slider stacks: ×1 → 0.5 effective, ×2 → classic per-body. One
named constant, tuned from `gravity_bench` / the stats strip, not a third
slider.

## 3. the solver

New file `gravity_fft.js`, classic script, `module.exports` guarded, no
library. One radix-2 complex FFT, 2-D as row then column. Loaded by
`index.html` before `gravity_sim.js`; `gravity_sim.js` reaches it with
`require('./gravity_fft.js')` under Node and `root.GravityFFT` in the page.

	GRID_X = 256, GRID_Y = 256    // powers of 2 (512 × 256 when worldW = 4800 in 0.8.1)
	dx = worldW / GRID_X          // 6.25 px (9.375 px at 4800)
	dy = worldH / GRID_Y          // 3.906 px

Cells are anisotropic; `GravityFFT` parameterizes `(gridX, gridY, worldW, worldH)`
from day one (each axis uses at least 256 cells, rounded up to a power of 2
that keeps `dx, dy <= 10 px`, i.e. `256 × 256` on `1600 × 1000` and `512 × 256` on `0.8.1`'s `4800 × 1000` world)
so `0.8.1` reuses `GravityFFT` with zero solver rewrite. Sample the real-space
kernel at the physical `dx`, `dy`; transform that kernel directly. No spectral
`1/k²` multiplier or differentiation is needed. Softening spans ~6–15 cells,
so CIC interpolation of this kernel needs no P3M near-field.

### Kernel and transform contract

Cache kernel spectra by `(gridX, gridY, worldW, worldH, SOFT)`, not one global
256² table. Index cells as `y * gridX + x`, with the origin at index zero
(no `fftshift`). Forward FFT is unnormalized with negative exponential;
inverse has positive exponential and divides by `gridX * gridY` exactly once.
Reuse bit-reversal, twiddle and column-scratch buffers. Validate rectangular
power-of-two grids as well as squares.

- `kAx`, `kAy`: complex spectra of unit minimum-image acceleration. Zero the
  **Fourier DC bin** of both after transforming; this is distinct from the
  real-space origin, which is already zero. An odd kernel and zero DC give
  zero uniform-field acceleration to floating-point tolerance.
- `kPhi`: complex spectrum of unit minimum-image potential. Also remove its
  DC bin: periodic potential is displayed relative to its grid mean. This
  changes no force; it fixes the colour gauge for signed fields.
- Signed masses are legal. Never normalize by total mass (which can be zero).
  CIC deposits **mass**, not density: there is no extra cell-area factor.

### Field snapshots and invalidation

Allocate solver work buffers only for a periodic solve, never for classic or
moving-only sims and never inside `advance`/`fieldAt`. A solve deposits masses
with CIC, FFTs `rho` once, multiplies that spectrum separately by the three
kernels, and inverse-transforms `Ax`, `Ay`, `phi`. CIC gather wraps both
neighbours in each dimension and multiplies acceleration/potential by
`MU * gravityScale` at sample/paint time. `PERIODIC_STRENGTH` is applied
exactly once on either path: to deposited mass, or to direct moving-source
strength, never both.

A solved field is an immutable snapshot: geometry, dimensions, active extent,
source positions and effective signed strengths identify it. Sims with the
**same** field (live, rival, helper, fleet, burn/exit probe) may explicitly adopt
the same snapshot. They never write to its grids. A changed opening HQ pin,
mask or manual strength gets a new snapshot; it cannot mutate the live one.
Keep cache ownership bounded to the world/current probes, not a global history
of every candidate seat. Reuse synchronous transform workspace between solves;
keep output grids alive while a sim references them. At 256², three Float64
output grids cost 1.5 MiB **per distinct field**, in addition to complex kernel
spectra and transform workspace; measure peak memory with the opening planner.
Do not eagerly allocate this for every sim constructor.

**Invalidation is explicit.** Changed `setField` identity, changed `setCount`,
world/topology changes, strength edits (`setStrength`, `resetField`), masked strength rewrites,
seed rerolls, designed-field adoption and save restore invalidate the snapshot.
Direct array writers must publish the field change too. `refreshField` only
refills positions: it is also used for time sync and must not trigger an FFT
on an unchanged static field. Switching to ports releases the static handle;
switching back checks the current field identity. A dirty static field is solved
once before its next sample, prediction or paint. Repeated `spawn`, `fillStep`,
`refreshField`, prediction slices and `setScales` do not solve again.

Only `periodic && fieldStatic` samples the grid. Moving ports keep the direct
minimum-image sum even at `fieldRate = 0`; they do not allocate or solve a grid.
No per-step FFT. Ensure every probe adopts topology **before** field setup.
Prediction, live flight, helper, bot and opening planner use the same law.

**Cost.** An eager acceleration + potential solve requires **four** 2-D complex
transforms (one forward, three inverse), plus three cached kernel transforms
per geometry. A radix-2 256² transform has
`gridX * gridY * (log2(gridX) + log2(gridY)) / 2 = 524288` butterflies;
a solve has about 2.1 M butterflies, plus products and CIC. Measure cold kernel
setup, first solve, shared-field adoption, repeated prediction and opening
candidate solves separately. O(1) trajectory sampling does not make thousands
of opening-field rebuilds free. If UI startup is slow, budget setup as a job
and block dependent launch/prediction until the matching snapshot is ready;
never let physics use a stale grid while the view uses a new field.

`GravitySim.MAX_BODIES = 192`; the WebGL shader's `uBodies[48]` does not grow,
because the periodic field view is a grid blit (section 6).

## 4. the torus

The play area **is** the planet field. `padX = padY = 0`. `outside()` is
never true. `END_EXIT` stays in the enum (replays, tests) and is simply
not produced.

Wrap exactly once inside `advance` (shared by live, sliced and full prediction):

	x = x - W * floor(x / W)
	y = y - H * floor(y / H)

`spawn` wraps the initial state too: a shot fired from a core at x = 10
towards the left starts at x = W − gap, not at −gap. Launch velocity is
unchanged.

Length uses the pre-wrap step (`hypot(vx, vy) * dt`), so a seam crossing does
not add 1600 px. Impact, system ellipses, `markSystemsAt`, `stepInsideAt`,
`fieldDrift` and Voronoi sound use the minimum-image delta:

	dx -= W * round(dx / W)
	dy -= H * round(dy / H)

At exactly half a world, force uses the averaged tie rule in §2. Geometric
distance/impact checks retain the half-world distance, not zero.

`fieldDrift` compares a moving source with its prediction snapshot. Its
minimum-image delta makes equivalent positions near opposite seams report a
small drift. Unchanged static positions already have zero drift. Bypass the
classic `beyondPlanets` AABB early-out for periodic impact and visit checks:
a single unwrapped box is not a valid toroidal broad phase.

A planet at x = 10 is hittable from x = 1590; a visit ellipse that straddles
the edge counts on both sides. `planetXAt` / `planetYAt` keep returning the
unwrapped core + ellipse position: they are a drawing and landing identity, and
wrapping them would make a station jump once per orbit. The drawing layer draws
the ellipse as it is and adds ghost copies where it crosses an edge.

The `full area` radio and `z` hide. The camera stays on `main`. If the current
frame is `full` when the option turns on, reset it to `main`. The backdrop
paints one rect, not two. `?view=` does not carry the frame today and still
does not.

Bot rescue-on-`END_EXIT` becomes a dead branch; leave it, it never fires.
`careerDeparture` and the helper scans already require `END_IMPACT`, so a
periodic launch's `END_TIME` is not mistaken for an exit.

## 5. four times the planets

Constants, gated by the option:

| | off | on |
|---|---|---|
| array capacity `OrbitWorld.PLANETS` | 192 (both; see below) | 192 |
| UI / universe cap | 48 | 192 |
| lab / tour / money default | 26 | 104 |
| career default | 16 | 64 |
| min centre gap | 165 px, Euclidean, 110×90 inset | 81 px, toroidal, no inset |
| orbit cap | room-to-edge, up to 180 | 78, and the ellipse wraps |

`PLANETS` becomes 192 always so career pads, market arrays and the sim body
buffers do not resize on toggle. `PLANET_UNIVERSE_MAX` stops being
`const ... = PLANETS` in `gravity_gui.js`: it becomes `planetUniverseCap`, a
`let` initialised to 48. `planetMask` and the strength arrays are allocated at
`PLANETS` (192), so the cap can rise after the URL is read without reallocating;
every loop that means "the usable universe" uses the variable, not `PLANETS`.
`PLANET_DEFAULT` and `PLANET_CAREER_DEFAULT` follow the same option.
The career total is four times the current **16**, not the retired 13 settled
worlds: use 64 and apply the existing `deriveEmptyWorlds` rule to that total.
Keep uninhabited/founded flags and their original heavy wells; do not treat
founding as a reason to reroll the field. The planet
input's `max` attribute and the `+` / `−` player buttons read the cap, not
`PLANETS`.

**Classic is a prefix, exactly.** With the option off `placePlanets` /
`placeOrbits` / `placeTraits` still consume the historical stream for 26,
`placeExtraPlanets` still fills 26…47 from `seed ^ 0x48ab91` with its 165 px
threshold, and 48…191 are left zero and never in play. Do not run the classic
placer for 48…191: it would spend time and leave unusable slots. With the
option on, `OrbitWorld.create(seed, rand, { periodic: true })` fills the 192-cell lattice,
but the active map still takes the first `n` (104 lab / 64 career) in
bit-reversal order; the rest are latent capacity for a later `+` or a
max-universe setting.

**The toroidal placer is a lattice, not rejection sampling.** At 192 bodies a
81 px gap covers `192 · π · 40.5² ≈ 0.99 M px²` of 1.6 M (62 %), above the
random-sequential-adhesion limit; a 300-attempt rejection loop would either
stall or quietly accept overlaps. Use a 16 × 12 lattice:

	cell = 100 × 83.33 px
	col  = 0…15, row = 0…11
	x = (col + 0.5 + jitterX) * cellW
	y = (row + 0.5 + jitterY) * cellH
	jitter = ±1 px, seeded

The lattice's own nearest spacing is 83.33 px vertically, so ±1 px jitter still
guarantees 81.3 px. Visit ellipses overlap; that is the denser map. To keep a
small universe well spread, order the 192 cells by **bit-reversal** of
`row * 16 + col` and take the first `n`; the first 64 career worlds then sample
the whole field instead of the top three rows. Define the order exactly: enumerate `j = 0…255`, reverse its eight bits,
and keep results `< 192` as cell indices. The same order makes a seed's
first 26/64 stable when the universe grows. Check prefix coverage in tests,
not only the 192-cell separation.

Specials remain two indices among the first four, so every legal universe
(`PLANET_UNIVERSE_MIN = 4`) still holds both.

**Capacity, count and extent are different.** `PLANETS` sizes storage;
`planetCount` counts active entries; `activePlanetExtent()` / the sim's
`planetCount` bound masked iteration and may exceed the number of active
entries. A sparse mask after removal must not truncate a high-index planet.
Audit existing loops by meaning rather than replacing all capacity bounds.
`gui_replay.js` must show the run's active `planetCount`, not capacity.

**Planet-mask keys must represent all 192 bits.** `game_opening.js`
`openingMaskBits` currently packs two 32-bit words into a Number. Above index
63 shifts alias; above 53 bits that Number also loses precision. Replace it
with an exact bounded string of unsigned 32-bit words (six for 192), with a
distinct null sentinel. Use it for active, forced-home, empty and heavy masks
in opening/seat cache keys; test differences at 0, 32, 53, 63, 64, 128, 191.
Keep player sport bitsets separate: they index actors, not planets.
All field-dependent opening, leg, exit and pair-track cache keys also include
topology, dimensions and field revision so classic/periodic or changed wells
never reuse one another's traces. Bound retained opening traces by bytes as
well as entry count: the current 4096-entry cap with two 192² Float64/Uint8
pair tables alone permits about 1.27 GiB. Retain reusable results selectively;
measure peak retained cache + grid memory, not only one solve's workspace.

`GravitySim.MAX_BODIES = 192`. The WebGL shader's `uBodies[48]` does **not**
grow — periodic view does not loop bodies (section 6). Classic view still
caps at 48, which is the classic max.

Shot encoding: `SHOT_FORM` origin `(\d{1,2})` becomes `(\d{1,3})`. `END_FORM`
becomes `(\d{1,3})` too: `end=visited.steps` carries the number of visited
systems, and a 104/192-world run can print three digits. Both decoders keep
their existing numeric bounds (`origin < PLANETS`, `visited` is just a count),
so old links still match.

## 6. what you see

For static sources, physics, prediction and the field/potential view sample
the **same grid**. Moving sources share the same direct minimum-image law.
A 192-body uniform array would also blow typical WebGL1 fragment-uniform
limits, so the body-loop shader is the classic path only.

**Field view.** Reuse the existing 1/4-resolution field canvas instead of a
texture: after a solve, write `gridX × gridY` pixels into an offscreen canvas,
then `drawImage` it stretched over `FIELD_BOX` with smoothing. Hue = direction
of `a`, lightness = |a| on the same knee as today (`L_MAX`, `G_KNEE`); the
existing 2D fallback already owns the colour ramp. Recolour only when the
snapshot, view mode, palette or `gravityScale` changes; redraw by blitting that cache each frame. The scale slider must update
colour without solving again. No extension, no float readback. Moving-port
periodic view uses a CPU minimum-image direct raster, never a stale static grid
or the nonperiodic shader.

**Potential view.** It needs its own `kPhi` solve: the existing `sampleZone`
kernel is a surface-density bump, not φ. Colour mean-zero φ on a signed
palette (negative φ / attraction green,
positive φ / repulsion blue) and label it as periodic potential, not the
classic bump. Moving ports evaluate the same mean-referenced potential law
with a CPU raster, subtracting the unit kernel mean times total signed mass
(the mean is cached by geometry). No FFT is required. The classic bump and
periodic potential are not the same quantity.

**Space view.** Trails and predictions: a polyline that jumps more than W/2
(H/2) is a seam, not a chord — split the path. Orbit ellipses, wells, visit
rings, signs and labels whose AABB crosses an edge are wrap-blitted (up to four
copies) so a well at x = 10 is visible on the right. Draw the ship at its
wrapped position, with a ghost when its radius straddles an edge so it does not pop. Batch geometry/label ghost copies with clipping;
never duplicate simulation objects. Use minimum-image deltas for aim grips,
click/hover hit tests, guide arrows, target distances and sound, not only paint.

## Validation

- `experiments/fft_test.js`, headless:
  - FFT round-trip, impulse convolution and single-mode tests on small
    square and rectangular grids, catching normalization, axis swap and sign;
  - CIC mass conservation (signed and zero-sum fields), periodic translations,
    uniform-background zero acceleration, self-force and half-world ties;
  - independent wrapped direct oracle for single positive/negative sources,
    asymmetric 8-body and 192-body fields. Use vector error, not just |a|:
    `|a_grid - a_direct| <= 0.05 * |a_direct| + 0.01 * a_ref` outside
    2ε, and `0.10 * |a_direct| + 0.01 * a_ref` inside, where
    `a_ref = MU * PERIODIC_STRENGTH / SOFT²`. Deterministic scattered and
    cell/seam-adjacent samples, including cancellation points. Ordinary-error
    bounds exclude a two-cell strip around each source's half-world cuts;
    those strips have separate finiteness, symmetry and bounded jump tests
    against `MU * PERIODIC_STRENGTH * sum(|strength|) / SOFT²`. This is an
    explicit minimum-image discontinuity, not a reason to relax all samples;
  - mean-zero potential, signed-palette polarity, and finite-difference
    `-grad(phi)` vs acceleration away from cuts within discretization error;
  - a ship that crosses x = W appears at x − W with `vy` unchanged, no
    `END_EXIT`, `flightLength` continuous;
  - min-image impact: a planet at x = 10, ship at x = W − 5 inside
