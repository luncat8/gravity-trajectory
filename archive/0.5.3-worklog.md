# 0.5.3 — Stage 3: the aim/predict/game core, and the composer keeps its state

Stage scope **changed mid-flight, on evidence.** The plan's rule 6 ("only the owning
area writes its own state") assumed most composer state has one owner. Inventory over
every top-level declaration said otherwise:

| state | consumers | writing units |
|---|---|---|
| `hudAt` | 6 | 11 |
| `dirty` | 3 | 10 |
| `aimSpeed` | 14 | 8 |
| `launchState`, `armed` | 15, 10 | 7 |
| `aimAngle`, `aimBody` | 14 | 7, 6 |
| `aiming` | 16 | 6 |

Only 6 of 323 declarations had a single consumer. And the extracted files already
wrote shared state heavily before this stage (`gui_route` 12 names, `gui_book` 11,
`gui_helper` 10) — verified as the original design, not something my cuts introduced
(`dirty = true` appears 31 times in the pre-split monolith). So **re-homing state would
create** cross-file mutation, not remove it, and make it less visible. Stages 3–4 are
therefore pure block moves; all mutable state stays in `gravity_gui.js`. A separate
state-ownership plan (route writes through the owning area's API) is the follow-up.

## Two things this stage caught that the earlier ones didn't

**1. Definition-time reads are a real load-order hazard, and I initially relied on the
smoke test to notice.** `gui_career.js` has a top-level `const careerTabs = [{ button:
careerUpgradesTabEl, ... }]`; those element consts still lived in the composer, which now
loads *after* it — `ReferenceError` at load. `gui_predict.js` hit the same with
`const MAX_PLAYERS = scoreRowEls.length`. Fix is mechanical and now automated: after each
extraction a closure pass walks the moved file's top-level statements and moves any composer
declaration they read, transitively, into that file. That is a genuine ownership improvement,
not just plumbing: the element ref now sits with the code that needs it at load.

**2. The 600-line cap caught a mis-scoped area, and the right answer was not to obey it blindly.**
`gui_aim.js` came out 810 lines. The plan assigned "pointer + trim handlers" to that file, but the
block I cut also carried the game-clock format and `updateGameHud` — a second reason to change.
Those three functions moved to `gui_hud_text.js`. The remainder stays 692: the pointer handlers,
the turret and the burn grip write the *same* `armed`/`aiming`/`aim*` state, and that state is
touched by 6–11 units, so splitting it further would trade one long file for two files that every
aim change has to touch. Cap exceeded by 92 lines, deliberately, recorded here.
