# 0.1.0 — gravity cargo on the orbital trade backdrop

Plan is written as a standalone spec: a fork can rebuild the experiment from this
file alone.

## intent

The `orbit_trade` visualisation (luncat8/exec_queue) becomes the living
background. The new experiment is a ballistic cargo container launched from a
planet: set direction and push, preview the whole path under the gravity of every
planet, then launch. Gravity strength is editable per planet. Shift gives fine
aim. A relative trim slider nudges the active value. Prediction has a hard cost
cap.

Trade time and experiment time are independent by design.

## background layer

- ports ride fixed ellipses around static planet cores; trade is a discrete-event
  simulation (load → rendezvous → arrive → colony) driven by `ExecQueue`.
- at startup `sim.step(420)` fast-forwards the trade world to `t+07:00`; the speed
  slider then runs at `0.5` sim minutes per real second.
- background keeps pause, speed and orbit/link/route visibility controls.
- the trade event log and reset button are dropped: the log is not needed for a
  backdrop, and a reset would swap the planet arrays under the cargo experiment.
- `orbit_view.js` exports `OrbitScene = { sim, view }`; `view` is the shared
  `{ dpr, scale, offX, offY }` camera so the overlay canvas matches the trade
  canvas exactly without duplicating the projection.

## separate clocks

- trade clock: `orbit_sim.t`, sim minutes, top-left HUD. Only trade responds to
  pause/speed.
- experiment clock: real seconds since page load, top-right HUD. Flight time is
  measured with `performance.now()` frame deltas in `gravity_sim` and never reads
  `orbit_sim.t`, so pausing or speeding trade cannot change a cargo flight.

## world and gravity field

- 26 static planet cores from `OrbitSim` (`planetX`, `planetY`, `planetR`);
  the trade stations ride their own ellipses, so a cargo path never collides
  with a moving station.
- per-planet strength `s ∈ [-2, 2]`, default `1`, editable at runtime.
- acceleration on the container:

  ```
  a = Σ s_i · MU · (p_i − p) / (|p_i − p|² + SOFT²)^(3/2)
  MU = 4.0e5 px³/s², SOFT = 60 px
  ```

  Softening keeps close passes finite; negative `s` is a repulsor well. Both
  values were picked from a sweep over 26 planets × 24 angles × 5 speeds:
  `SOFT = 26` made every shot under ~120 px/s fall straight back into its own
  planet, `SOFT = 60` with `MU = 4e5` leaves a workable window (~60 px/s reaches
  ~380 px, ~90 px/s crosses the field) while keeping the far end of the speed
  range from leaving the field immediately.
- fixed step `DT = 1/120 s`, semi-implicit Euler. Live flight and prediction call
  the same `advance(state, dt)` and `impact(x, y)` — no second physics model.
- collision radius `planetR + 3.5 px`; leaving the world by 12 px ends the flight.
- one active container; its trail is a polyline recorded every other step.

## aiming and launch

- pointer down within 18 screen px of a planet selects that planet and starts the
  aim. The grip starts at the pointer and follows pointer deltas.
- direction = planet centre → grip; push = grip length mapped linearly over
  `240 px` to `30…320 px/s`.
- while shift is held the grip follows deltas at `1/20` speed; releasing shift
  never snaps the aim, so precision is preserved.
- release arms the shot (does not fire). Trajectory stays drawn while the user
  trims or edits gravity. Enter or the launch button fires. A click without a
  real drag only selects the planet. Esc cancels the plan; clear removes the
  cargo and its trail.
- launch happens on the aiming side: spawn is always `planetR + 8 px` out along
  the aim angle, so a shot never starts inside the planet.

## trim slider

- range `-5% … +5%`, step `0.5%`, spring-loaded relative nudge: each input delta
  multiplies the active value by `1 + delta/100`; on release the thumb returns
  to 0 and the applied change stays.
- target: the armed aim speed when an aim exists, otherwise the selected
  planet's strength (clamped to `[-2, 2]`), otherwise disabled.
- the aim readout and trajectory update immediately after a nudge.

## prediction

- starts from the exact launch state and records one point per `DT` step.
- stops on impact, leaving the field, a `20 s` horizon, `MAX_STEPS = 2450`, or a
  `10 ms` wall-clock budget checked every 64 steps (the draft's 10–20 ms cap).
- recomputed only when aim, strength or selection changes, at most once per
  animation frame; the computed milliseconds and step count are shown in HUD.
- buffer `PATH_MAX = 2500` points, typed arrays, no allocation per calculation.

## rendering

- two full-screen canvases: `#c` trade (from `orbit_view.js`), `#g` overlay
  (`gravity_gui.js`) with `pointer-events: none` above it so trade hover keeps
  working; HUD panels sit above both.
- gravity wells: ring radius `24 + |g|·64 px`, ice for attract, dashed pink for
  repel, gold outline for the selected planet; the selected ring is not filled
  over its own planet label. Labels are drawn in screen space: the selected
  planet's label sits below its core while a shot is aimed from it, so it never
  collides with the grip readout.
- predicted path: gold dashed line with one dot per simulated second, endpoint
  marker (impact planet / left field / horizon / budget).
- live trail: cyan; cargo: gold diamond with a pre-rendered glow sprite.
- aim: line from the planet to the grip, arrow head, max-drag circle, live
  `px/s · degrees` label at the grip and a `fine 1/20` badge while shift is down.
- the aim readout follows the pointer, so it updates per frame; the rest of the
  experiment HUD (clock, status, path, calc) is throttled to 10 Hz. Elements are
  cached and only written when the text changes.
- the camera fits the world above the bottom control bar: inset 14 px on three
  sides plus a footer band of `clamp(8% of height, 76, 120) px`.

## acceptance

- trade clock starts at `t+07:00`, runs at `×0.5`; trade pause/speed does not
  touch the cargo experiment clock or physics.
- all 26 planets selectable; strength slider `-2…2`, default `1`; repulsors are
  visually distinct.
- predicted trajectory is shown before launch and matches the flight.
- shift aims at `1/20` sensitivity; trim applies relative nudges to aim or gravity.
- worst-case prediction stays under the 10 ms budget; benchmark output recorded.
