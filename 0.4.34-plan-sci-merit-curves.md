# 0.4.34 plan — one merit curve per science axis

## What the fee is for

The career pays a **research fee** to qualify a new design point, and the fee
has two independent parts:

1. **relative work** — how far the point is from what the career already knows
   (`base + k·(dist + frontier)`, already implemented). It is zero for a
   recombination of known tradeoffs and grows along a hard direction.
2. **absolute work** — how demanding the target is, whatever the career knows.
   This part is the `0.4.33` mass factor and this plan generalises it.

The second part exists because an R&D program is priced by its own spec, not
only by novelty: qualifying a 40 kN engine needs a bigger test stand, more
instrumentation and more campaigns than a 12 kN one; a 1 %/year seal needs
better materials and longer endurance runs than a 0.1 %/year one; a leaky,
heavy, inefficient device is simply less engineering. Novelty is relative,
ambition is absolute, and a believable fee needs both.

## The defect

`0.4.33` gave that absolute part to **mass only** (`MASS_REFERENCE = { engine,
tank }`, hardcoded to axis 1, `fee × (ref/mass)^log2(1.5)`). Every other
characteristic has the flat easy-direction rule from `0.4.32`: making it worse
costs nothing extra, but also **saves nothing**.

Measured with the hull seeded from the tech tables (fee, purse units):

| target | fee |
|---|---|
| battery 6 kWh, leak 0.0003 … 0.0012 /y | 0.667 for **all** of them |
| battery 22 kWh, leak 0.0003 … 0.0012 /y | 11.227 for **all** of them |
| catapult 100 kN, efficiency 0.50 … 0.88 | 2.000 for **all** of them |
| engine 26 kN, mass 1.0 → 3.6 t | 3.838 → 0.630 (the only curve) |

So a battery that leaks four times faster than the best pad battery costs
exactly as much to invent as a tight one, and a rail that throws away half its
energy costs exactly as much as one at the state of the art. Only mass is
cheap when it is bad. That is the wrong behaviour the report is about: a
leakier battery must be **cheaper**, not merely not dearer.

## The change: one merit curve per axis

Drop `MASS_REFERENCE` / `massFactor` and give **every** research axis a merit
row in `orbit_science.js`:

```js
var MERIT = {
	engine:   [{ par: 24, per2: 1.25 }, { par: 1.6, lower: true, per2: 1.50 }],
	catapult: [{ par: 135, per2: 1.25 }, { par: 0.20, lower: true, mirror: 1, per2: 1.50 }],
	tank:     [{ par: 90, per2: 1.25 }, { par: 0.9, lower: true, per2: 1.50 }],
	battery:  [{ par: 6, per2: 1.25 }, { par: 0.0006, lower: true, per2: 1.50 }]
};
```

- `par` — the **middle rung of the tech table** (IMP-2, EMC-3, FT-2, BAT-3) is
  the ordinary design: merit ×1, fee = base. `0.4.33` used the *lightest* rung
  (1 t / 0.5 t), which put the whole engine and tank ladder below par and
  discounted it systematically. Par is now one rule for every axis.
- `per2` — the fee multiplier per **doubling of merit**: ×1.25 for a capability
  axis (thrust, rail force, fuel, capacity), ×1.50 for a waste axis (mass,
  leak, efficiency losses). Capability is already priced by the frontier term
  as soon as it passes the known range; waste is not, and ×1.50 keeps the
  `0.4.33` rule the reports already approved ("2× heavier = fee ÷1.5").
- `lower` — less is better (mass, leak).
- `mirror` — an axis bounded from above (efficiency, ≤ 1). Its merit is
  measured on the **loss** `mirror − value`, so par 0.20 means 80 % and
  "halving the losses" is the same step as doubling a capability. A ratio on
  raw efficiency would be meaningless near 1.
- `x ≤ 0` (or past the mirror) is unresearchable: `Infinity`, as nonpositive
  mass already was.

Formally, with `k = log2(per2)`:

```
axisMerit   = (x/par)^k            x = value          capability axis
            = (par/x)^k            x = value          lower-is-better axis
            = (par/(mirror−value))^k                  mirrored axis
meritFactor = clamp(Π axisMerit, MERIT_MIN = 0.35, MERIT_MAX = 2.50)
cost        = (base + k·(dist + frontier)) × meritFactor
```

The clamp is a floor and a ceiling on the whole program: no design is free to
invent (the lab always costs something) and the far corners are not priced out
of existence by two curves compounding.

## Why this is monotone

Worsening one attribute lowers its axis merit, cannot raise the hull distance
or the frontier work (`0.4.32`), and the clamp is monotone — so the fee cannot
rise. `0.4.32`'s invariant holds and becomes strict for leak and efficiency.

## Expected shape (par-ref fees, hull seeded from the tables)

| slider default | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| engine 26 kN / 1.8 t | 0.945 | ~1.27 |
| catapult 100 kN / 0.76 | 2.000 | ~1.63 |
| tank 100 kg / 1.0 t | 0.444 | ~0.65 |
| battery 6 kWh / 0.0005 | 0.667 | ~0.74 |

Every default stays inside the 6.00 starter purse. Engines and tanks lose the
systematic sub-par discount; catapults get cheaper because par is now the mid
rung and the bridge-rail target (52 kN / 0.67) is far below it.
